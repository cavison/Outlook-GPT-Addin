# Lights Out for 90 Days: A Complete Survival Guide — Book Outline

Nov 14, 2026 · @Curtis

## Approach

The book is organized by phase of the outage, not by skill: what to do before, in the first 72 hours, in weeks 1–4, in months 2–3+, and after. It pulls together what the genre covers as a whole (Stein's *When Technology Fails*, Koppel's *Lights Out*, prepper-blueprint guides, FEMA/Red Cross/CDC guidance) and checks it against what actually happened in long outages: Puerto Rico after Hurricane Maria (2017), Texas (2021), the Northeast blackout (2003) and Katrina (2005).

**Assumed reader:** a household of 2–6 people, suburban or small-town, four-season climate (cold winters), modest budget.

**Design rules for every chapter**

- Each chapter has four parts: what goes wrong, the skills, the gear in three tiers (Minimum / Better / Serious), and a Day-90 check (does this still work in month three?).
- Space follows the real causes of death and harm. Carbon monoxide, cold, heat, contaminated water and loss of medical care get more pages than armed conflict.
- Community comes before stockpiling, because organized neighborhoods recover faster than lone households.
- Every chapter ends with a one-page checklist for the reader to print and file in the binder (Chapter 4).

## Part I — Before: Preparation

**Chapter 1. Why the Grid Fails, and Why 90 Days Is Different**

1. How the grid works in plain terms: generation, transmission, distribution, and why it is fragile
2. Causes: ice storms, hurricanes, heat waves, solar storms (geomagnetic disturbances), cyberattack, physical attacks on substations, fuel shortages
3. Why big transformers matter: they are custom-built and replacements can take months to years
4. The cascade: water, sewer, fuel pumps, cell towers, ATMs, pharmacies, supply chains
5. Three scales of outage: 3 days (inconvenience), 2–3 weeks (disruption), 90+ days (a different way of life)
6. Case studies: Puerto Rico 2017, Texas 2021, Northeast 2003, Ukraine's grid under attack

**Chapter 2. The Household Audit**

1. People: ages, mobility, medical conditions, prescriptions, power-dependent devices (oxygen, CPAP, dialysis, insulin refrigeration)
2. Pets and livestock
3. The house: heating type, water source (city vs well), sewer vs septic, gas vs all-electric, which room is easiest to heat
4. Skills inventory: who can cook from scratch, give first aid, fix things, garden, hunt or fish
5. Neighbors' skills and needs, the start of a street map
6. The weak-point list: the 5 things that would hurt your household first

**Chapter 3. Stockpiling Without Going Broke**

1. The "buy one extra" method and rotating stock (first in, first out)
2. Water storage: 1 gallon per person per day as a floor; containers, rotation, rain barrels
3. Food: a 2-week, 30-day and 90-day pantry built from foods you already eat; staples (rice, beans, oats, flour, fats, salt, sugar, canned protein)
4. Fuel: propane, kerosene, firewood, gasoline with stabilizer; safe storage limits and rotation
5. Medicine and first-aid supplies; talking to your doctor about extra prescriptions
6. Hygiene and sanitation stock: bleach, trash bags, toilet paper, buckets, lime or sawdust
7. Cash in small bills; copies of documents
8. Tiered shopping lists: $100 / $500 / $2,000+

**Chapter 4. The Paper Plan**

1. Why the plan must be printed: phones die and the internet goes down
2. The family binder: contacts, meeting points, medical info, insurance, maps, shut-off locations
3. Printed how-to guides: first aid, water treatment, cooking, repair
4. Communication plan: out-of-state contact, check-in times, signals for the house
5. Roles and responsibilities by person
6. Practice: a 24-hour "lights off" weekend drill and what it teaches

## Part II — The First 72 Hours

**Chapter 5. Stabilize**

1. The first hour: account for everyone, check on people who depend on medical devices, check on neighbors
2. Turn off or unplug major appliances to prevent surge damage when power returns; leave one light on as a "power's back" signal
3. Fill every container and the bathtub while water pressure lasts
4. Protect the cold chain: keep fridge and freezer shut (a full freezer holds about 48 hours, half full about 24, a fridge about 4)
5. Decide early: is this a short outage or a long one? Signs that point to a long one
6. Get cash and fuel early, before lines and limits
7. Shelter in place or leave: a decision tree

**Chapter 6. The Silent Killers**

1. Carbon monoxide: generators, grills and camp stoves never run indoors, in garages or near windows; battery CO alarms on every floor; symptoms
2. Fire: candles, lanterns, fuel storage, space heaters; extinguishers and an escape plan with no lights
3. Cold: hypothermia signs; picking and sealing one warm room; layering; keeping pipes from freezing
4. Heat: heat exhaustion vs heat stroke; cooling without AC; who is most at risk (older adults, infants, people on certain medications)
5. Food poisoning from failed refrigeration: "when in doubt, throw it out"
6. Injuries in the dark: falls, chainsaws, ladders, storm debris

**Chapter 7. Information Without the Internet**

1. AM/FM and NOAA weather radios (hand-crank or battery)
2. Keeping phones alive: power banks, car chargers, solar, airplane mode, text vs call
3. Two-way radios: FRS/GMRS, CB, and ham radio (licensing basics)
4. Rumor control: how to check information before acting on it
5. Where official information will appear: fire stations, community centers, printed notices

## Part III — Weeks 1–4: Settling In

**Chapter 8. Water**

1. How much you really need: drinking, cooking, hygiene, sanitation
2. Hidden water in the house: water heater, toilet tanks (not bowls), pipes
3. Outside sources: rain, streams, ponds, wells, snow; what each is likely to contain
4. Treatment: settling and pre-filtering, boiling, unscented bleach, filters (what they do and don't remove), solar disinfection
5. Wells without power: hand pumps, well buckets, generator wiring done safely
6. Storage and keeping water clean; labeling drinkable vs non-drinkable water
7. Day-90 check: a sustainable daily water routine

**Chapter 9. Sanitation and Hygiene**

1. Why this chapter matters: after disasters, disease from poor sanitation often harms more people than the event itself
2. When toilets stop flushing: the two-bucket system, cover material, bagging
3. Composting, burial and disposal away from water sources
4. Handwashing stations with little water
5. Bathing, laundry and dishes on a water budget
6. Garbage and pests (rats, flies) when there is no trash pickup
7. Menstrual, infant and incontinence care

**Chapter 10. Food**

1. Eating order: fridge first, then freezer, then pantry
2. Rationing and calorie planning for a family; children's and older adults' needs
3. Cooking without power: camp stove, rocket stove, wood fire, grill outdoors, solar oven, haybox (retained-heat) cooking
4. Cooking from scratch: bread, beans, rice, flatbreads, simple meals from stored staples
5. Keeping food cold without power: winter outdoor storage, ice chests, evaporative coolers
6. Pests and keeping stored food safe
7. Food fatigue and morale: why variety matters

**Chapter 11. Heat, Cooling and Shelter-in-Place**

1. Picking one living zone and insulating it: blankets on windows, a tent indoors, closing off rooms
2. Safe indoor heat: wood stoves, indoor-rated propane and kerosene heaters, with ventilation and CO alarms
3. Firewood: how much a winter takes, splitting, seasoning, storage
4. Body heat: clothing systems, sleeping bags, hot water bottles, shared sleeping
5. Keeping pipes from freezing, or draining the system
6. Summer: shade, cross-ventilation, cooling with water, sleeping low and cool

**Chapter 12. Light and Small-Scale Power**

1. Light: LED lanterns and headlamps, rechargeables, safe candle and oil lamp use
2. Batteries: types, storage and rotation
3. Portable power stations and solar panels: sizing in plain numbers
4. Generators: sizing, fuel use per day, transfer switches, never backfeeding the grid, noise and theft
5. Power budgeting: what deserves electricity (medical devices, communication, freezer cycles)
6. Using a car as a generator and charger, safely

**Chapter 13. Medical Care Without a Pharmacy**

1. A first-aid kit for 90 days, not 3
2. Core skills: bleeding control, wound cleaning and closure, burns, sprains and splinting, CPR
3. Infection prevention: the biggest medical threat in a long outage
4. Chronic conditions: diabetes, heart disease, asthma, mental illness; building a medication reserve legally with your doctor
5. Medication storage without refrigeration (insulin and others)
6. Dental emergencies
7. Illness from water and food: dehydration and oral rehydration solution
8. When to seek help, and where help may still be working
9. Recommended references: *Where There Is No Doctor*, *Where There Is No Dentist*

## Part IV — Months 2–3+: Sustaining

**Chapter 14. Producing Food**

1. A realistic view: a first-year garden adds to stored food but won't replace it
2. Fast crops (greens, radishes, beans, potatoes) and calorie crops
3. Seed saving and heirloom vs hybrid seeds
4. Container and small-space growing
5. Foraging basics and safe plant identification (the "never eat what you can't identify" rule)
6. Fishing, trapping and hunting within the law
7. Small livestock: chickens and rabbits
8. Preservation without a freezer: canning, drying, smoking, salting, root cellaring, fermenting

**Chapter 15. Skills That Become Currency**

1. Repair: small engines, plumbing, carpentry, bicycles
2. Sewing and clothing repair
3. Medical, childcare and elder care
4. Teaching: keeping children learning
5. Tool kits worth owning, and the hand-tool versions of power tools
6. Bartering skills and goods: what holds value (fuel, medicine, salt, coffee, batteries, skills)

**Chapter 16. Security, Realistically**

1. What research on disasters shows: most people cooperate; organized violence is rarer than fiction suggests, though crime and theft do rise
2. Low profile: light discipline, noise, not advertising supplies
3. Hardening the home: locks, doors, lighting, dogs
4. Neighborhood watch and shared lookouts
5. De-escalation and conflict resolution
6. Legal and ethical limits on self-defense
7. When to leave: trigger points, routes, go-bags, where to go

**Chapter 17. Community**

1. The street meeting: how to start one in the first week
2. Mapping the block: skills, supplies, vulnerable people, generators, wells
3. Shared resources: a community kitchen, charging station, water point, childcare
4. Decision-making and fairness: avoiding a few people taking over
5. Working with churches, schools, local government and volunteer groups
6. Mutual aid lessons from Puerto Rico and other long outages

**Chapter 18. Mental Health and Morale**

1. The emotional phases of a long crisis: heroic, honeymoon, disillusionment, recovery
2. Routine as survival gear: schedules, chores, rest
3. Children: explaining what's happening, play, school at home
4. Grief, anxiety, depression and substance use
5. Conflict within the household
6. Purpose, faith and meaning; music, stories and gatherings
7. Low-power entertainment: books, cards, games, instruments

**Chapter 19. Caring for Vulnerable People**

1. Older adults: mobility, cold and heat risk, medication, memory loss and confusion
2. People who depend on power for medical equipment: backup power plans, utility medical registries, evacuation thresholds
3. People with disabilities: communication, accessible sanitation and mobility
4. Infants and pregnancy: formula, diapers, safe sleep, birth planning
5. Group settings: senior living communities, group homes, shelters
6. Checking on isolated neighbors: a simple wellness-check routine
