The lights flicker on. People cheer. Then mistakes happen. The return of power is a risky moment: surges damage electronics, appliances left on can start fires, gas leaks show up when furnaces relight, and food that "looks fine" makes people sick. Water systems may still be under a boil advisory for days. Power may also come back and go out again as crews balance the grid.

Slow down. Restart the house in order.

## Priorities
1. **Keep the generator disconnected.** If you have a transfer switch, switch back to utility power before starting large loads. Shut the generator down and let it cool before refueling or storing. See [[P-06]].
2. **Restart in stages.** Before power returns, unplug sensitive electronics and turn off most breakers or appliances. When it returns, wait a few minutes, then turn on essentials first (furnace, fridge, freezer, well pump), then other loads over time. See [[V-01]].
3. **Check for gas leaks and damage.** If you smell gas (a rotten-egg odor), hear hissing, or see damaged lines, leave the house immediately and call the gas utility or 911 from outside. Do not flip switches or use phones inside. Relight pilot lights only if you know how and it is safe, or call a professional. See [[V-02]].
4. **Judge the food honestly.** Use the USDA rules: perishables above 40°F for 2 or more hours go out. When in doubt, throw it out. See [[V-03]] and [[F-02]].
5. **Flush water lines and obey advisories.** Follow your water utility's instructions. Under a boil advisory, boil water at a rolling boil for 1 minute or use bottled water for drinking, cooking, brushing teeth and formula. If you have a private well that may have flooded, disinfect and test it. See [[V-04]].
6. **Check furnace, water heater and sump pump.** Make sure vents are clear of snow and the water heater is full before turning on its power or gas.

## What goes wrong
- **Fires from appliances left on**, especially stoves, space heaters, irons and dryers, when power returns while the house is empty or asleep.
- **Electronics killed by surges.** Use surge protectors; unplug sensitive items until power is stable.
- **Electric water heater elements burned out** because the tank was drained (for drinking water) and not refilled before power came back.
- **Frozen or burst pipes discovered on thaw.** Look for leaks as water pressure returns. See [[R-05]].
- **Carbon monoxide** from a furnace with blocked vents or a generator still running near the house.
- **Eating spoiled food** because the freezer is cold again now. Refreezing does not make food that was above 40°F too long safe.
- **Downed lines still live.** Stay at least 30 feet away and report them.

## Gear tiers
| Need | Minimum | Better | Serious |
|---|---|---|---|
| Restart | Written restart order on the fridge | Restart order plus labeled breaker panel | Whole-house surge protection installed by an electrician |
| Gas safety | Know where the gas meter is, have the utility's number | Natural gas/CO combination alarm | Licensed technician inspection after a long outage |
| Food checks | Appliance thermometer in fridge and freezer | Freezer log kept during the outage | Min/max thermometer showing the warmest temperature reached |
| Water | Bottled water for advisory days | Stored water rotated, boil plan | Well test kit and disinfection plan if on a private well |

## Day-90 check (first days back)
- Breakers back on in order, no burning smells, no tripped GFCIs left unchecked.
- Furnace and water heater running normally, vents clear, CO alarms quiet.
- Fridge at 40°F or below, freezer at 0°F, spoiled food discarded and logged for claims.
- Water advisory status known and followed.

## Michigan notes
Consumers Energy and DTE Gas give gas-leak instructions on their websites and bills; call the gas utility's emergency line from outside if you smell gas. In Ottawa County, boil water advisories are issued by the local water utility or the county health department. Private wells: the Ottawa County Department of Public Health can advise on testing.

> **Renters:** Report gas smells, leaks, damaged wiring and heating problems to your landlord in writing right away. You are still responsible for clearing out spoiled food in your own fridge.

**Who's most at risk:** People who turn everything on at once, people who smell gas and investigate, households that keep "looks-fine" food, and families with infants during boil advisories.

## Checklist
- [ ] Generator off, disconnected and cooled
- [ ] Electronics unplugged until power is stable
- [ ] Essentials restarted first, others over time
- [ ] Gas meter, lines and appliances checked; any gas smell reported from outside
- [ ] Water heater full before power or gas turned on
- [ ] Fridge and freezer contents checked with a thermometer
- [ ] Water advisory status confirmed
- [ ] Pipes inspected for leaks as pressure returns
