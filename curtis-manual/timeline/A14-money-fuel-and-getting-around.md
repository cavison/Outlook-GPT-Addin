When the power is out across a wide area, the systems you use to pay for things and move around stop working. Card readers, ATMs and bank computers need power and network connections. Gas pumps need electricity to pump fuel out of underground tanks. Stores that do open may take cash only. By week two, the household that planned for cash, fuel and short trips has far more options than the one that did not.

## Priorities
1. **Have small-bill cash.** Keep cash at home in small bills ($1, $5, $10, $20); stores may not have change. Decide how much your household can afford to set aside. See [[R-07]].
2. **Protect the fuel you have.** Do not drive just to look around. Every gallon in your tank may be a gallon for the generator or a medical trip later. See [[R-09]].
3. **Store fuel safely and legally.** Approved containers, away from the house, with stabilizer, rotated. Check local fire code limits on how much you can store. See [[R-08]].
4. **Combine trips and share rides.** One car goes for the block's errands. Plan trips around what is actually open, confirmed by radio or neighbors.
5. **Switch to muscle power for short trips.** Bikes, wagons, garden carts and sleds move water, wood and groceries. See [[R-10]] and [[R-03]].
6. **Keep records.** Write down what you spend and any damage; you may need it for claims later. See [[V-05]].

## What goes wrong
- **Price gouging.** Some sellers charge far above normal prices for generators, fuel, ice and water. Michigan's Consumer Protection Act prohibits charging a price grossly in excess of the price at which similar goods are sold. You can report it to the Michigan Attorney General's consumer protection line. Get receipts.
- **Scams.** Fake contractors, fake utility workers asking for payment to "restore service faster," and fake relief offers show up after every disaster. Utilities do not charge to restore your power.
- **Siphoning and theft.** Fuel stored in plain sight disappears. Keep it locked and out of view. See [[X-01]].
- **Stale or contaminated gas.** Untreated gasoline can go bad in a few months and gum up small engines. Use stabilizer and rotate.
- **Running the tank dry.** Keep the car above half a tank in normal times so you start an outage with fuel.
- **Driving into danger.** Downed lines, flooded roads and dark intersections. Treat dark traffic signals as all-way stops (Michigan law).

## Gear tiers
| Need | Minimum | Better | Serious |
|---|---|---|---|
| Money | Some small-bill cash at home | Cash for 2–4 weeks of basics, plus copies of account info | Cash reserve, coins, barterable goods, records in a waterproof folder |
| Fuel | Car kept above half a tank | Several approved cans with stabilizer, rotated | Rotated fuel matched to a written generator and vehicle plan |
| Transport | Working bike for each adult | Bikes plus a wagon or garden cart | Cargo bike or bike trailer, sled for winter, repair kit and spare tubes |
| Records | Phone photos of documents | Paper copies of ID, insurance, account numbers | Waterproof document box, off-site copy with family |

## Day-90 check
- You know how much cash is left and what your weekly burn is.
- You know how many gallons of fuel remain and what they are reserved for.
- Every bike in the household has air in its tires and a patch kit.
- You have a list of what is open in Hudsonville, Jenison, Allendale and Grand Rapids and how they take payment.

## Michigan notes
Winter makes walking and biking slower and riskier. A plastic sled pulls heavy loads easily on packed snow and ice. Spring thaw brings potholes and flooding. Ottawa County roads and the Ottawa County Road Commission may post closures on local radio.

> **Renters:** Ask your landlord where (and whether) you can store fuel. Many leases ban it indoors or in shared spaces. Keep cash and documents in a small grab box you can take with you.

**Who's most at risk:** People without a car, people who rely on cards or online banking only, older adults targeted by scams, and anyone who needs regular trips for dialysis or treatment.
