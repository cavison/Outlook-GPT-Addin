In a long outage your phone will die, the internet will go down, and stress will make it hard to remember things. A plan on paper keeps working. Put it in a three-ring binder that lives in one known place, with a copy in your go-bag.

**What's happening now:** you still have a printer. Print what you need.

### Why paper

Cell service often fails within hours to days of a widespread outage as tower batteries drain. Cloud documents, contacts and maps go with it. Paper needs no charge and anyone in the family can read it.

### The family binder

- **Contacts:** family, neighbors, doctors, pharmacy, vet, utility outage line, Ottawa County emergency management, insurance agent, an **out-of-state contact**.
- **Meeting points:** one near home (a neighbor's porch), one in town (a church or school), one out of town.
- **Medical info:** each person's conditions, medicines and doses, allergies, device settings, blood type if known.
- **Insurance and documents:** copies of policies, IDs, deeds or leases, bank and account info.
- **Maps:** paper road map of Ottawa and Kent counties and the state; mark routes, water sources, hospitals.
- **Shut-offs:** photos or drawings showing the water main, gas valve and electric panel, with how to operate each.

### Printed how-to guides

Print or keep the modules you would need most: first aid, water treatment, CO safety, cooking without power, keeping pipes from freezing. This manual is meant to be printed. Add *Where There Is No Doctor* and a basic repair guide if you can.

### Communication plan

- Who calls the out-of-state contact, and when. Long-distance texts often get through when local calls do not.
- Check-in times (for example, 8 a.m. and 6 p.m. on the radio or in person).
- House signals: a sign in the window ("OK" / "NEED HELP"), a message board by the door ([[C-10]]).
- Radio channels if you use FRS/GMRS ([[C-08]]).

### Roles by person

Give each person a job that fits them: water, cooking, warm room, light and batteries, checking neighbors, keeping kids busy, keeping the log. Even young kids can be the "flashlight captain." Roles cut confusion and give people purpose.

### Practice: a lights-off weekend

Pick a weekend — ideally once in winter — and turn off the main breaker for 24 hours (keep the fridge closed). Cook, wash, light and stay warm using only your plan. Afterward, write what failed. Common lessons: not enough light, nobody could find the radio, kids got bored, the toilet plan was unclear. Fix those and practice again.

## Priorities
1. Build the binder with contacts, medical info and shut-offs.
2. Set meeting points and an out-of-state contact.
3. Assign roles.
4. Print key how-to modules.
5. Run a 24-hour drill.

## What goes wrong
- Plans that live only on a phone.
- Out-of-date medicine lists.
- No agreed meeting place when family is scattered at work and school.
- Plans nobody has practiced.

> **Who's most at risk:** people who rely on phones for everything, kids at school when an outage starts, and anyone whose medical details live in their head or an app.

> **Renters:** No yard or wood stove? Add your landlord's and building manager's emergency numbers, your unit's shut-off locations, and the nearest public warming center to the binder. Practice the drill by flipping your unit's breakers.

## Gear tiers
| Minimum | Better | Serious |
|---|---|---|
| Paper contact list and meeting points | Full binder, printed modules, paper maps | Binder copy in go-bag, laminated cards, drill done twice a year |

## Day-90 check
Is the binder still current? Update it at least twice a year (when clocks change is easy to remember) and after any move, new medicine or new baby.
