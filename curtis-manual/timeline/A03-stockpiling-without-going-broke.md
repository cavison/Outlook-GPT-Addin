You do not need a bunker or a big check. Most households can build a solid 90-day reserve over a year by buying a little extra of what they already use, storing it well, and rotating it.

**What's happening now:** the stores are open. Use your weak-point list from [[A02]] to decide what to buy first.

### Buy one extra

Each shopping trip, buy one more of a few staples you already eat. Put new items at the back, use from the front (first in, first out — [[F-04]]). In a few months you have weeks of food with no waste and little extra cost. Buy shelf-stable goods on sale.

### Water

FEMA's minimum is **1 gallon per person per day** for drinking and basic cooking — more for hot weather, nursing mothers, sick people, and pets. That floor does not include flushing or washing.

- **3 days:** at least 3 gallons per person, stored now.
- **2 weeks:** 14 gallons per person — about 56 gallons for four.
- **90 days:** storing it all is not realistic for most homes. Store what you can, then plan to collect and treat more (rain barrels, snow, nearby water) — see [[A08]].

Use food-grade containers, keep them out of sunlight, and label them. The water heater and pipes hold extra water you can use ([[W-01]], [[W-02]]).

### Food

Build in stages: 2 weeks, then 30 days, then 90 days. Choose foods you already eat. Good staples:

- Rice, dry beans and lentils, oats, flour, pasta
- Cooking oil, peanut butter, shortening
- Salt, sugar, honey
- Canned meat, fish, beans, vegetables and fruit
- Powdered milk, coffee and tea, spices, multivitamins

Use [[F-03]] to estimate quantities. Store in sealed, pest-proof containers ([[F-15]]). Plan a way to cook it without power ([[A10]]).

### Fuel

- **Propane:** 20-lb cylinders store outside, never indoors or in a garage.
- **Kerosene:** only in blue kerosene containers, for heaters rated for indoor use.
- **Firewood:** needs a year to dry. Order or cut it now ([[H-06]]).
- **Gasoline:** add stabilizer, store in approved cans away from the house, rotate every several months. Check your local fire code and insurance for storage limits ([[R-08]]).

### Medicine and first aid

Talk to your doctor and pharmacist about a modest extra supply of daily medicines; some insurers allow early refills or 90-day fills ([[M-10]]). Build a first-aid kit sized for months, not days ([[M-01]]).

### Hygiene and sanitation

Unscented bleach (it loses strength over time, so rotate it), heavy trash bags, toilet paper, two 5-gallon buckets with lids, sawdust or kitty litter, soap, hand sanitizer, menstrual and infant supplies.

### Cash and documents

Keep cash in small bills somewhere safe. Make copies of IDs, insurance, deeds or leases, and medical records for the binder ([[A04]]).

### Tiered shopping lists

| $100 | $500 | $2,000+ |
|---|---|---|
| Water jugs, LED lantern + headlamps, batteries, AM/FM/NOAA radio, bleach, trash bags, 2 weeks of extra staples | Add: camp stove + fuel, two-bucket toilet kit, water filter, first-aid kit, sleeping bags rated for cold, battery CO alarms, power bank | Add: indoor-safe heater or wood stove work, small generator or power station with solar, rain barrels, 90 days of food, garden and canning gear |

## Priorities
1. Water for 3 days, then 2 weeks.
2. CO alarms and safe light.
3. Two weeks of food you eat.
4. A way to stay warm and cook without power.
5. Medicines and first aid.
6. Then build toward 90 days.

## What goes wrong
- Buying expensive gear and skipping water and medicine.
- Buying foods nobody eats; they expire or cause misery.
- Storing fuel indoors or in the garage.
- Never rotating, so stock is stale when needed.

> **Who's most at risk:** families living paycheck to paycheck, older adults on fixed incomes, and anyone whose medicine cannot be stocked ahead. Start small; even one extra week helps.

> **Renters:** No yard or wood stove? Stack water and food under beds and in closets. Skip fuel cans you cannot store safely; a camp stove used outdoors and a battery power station may fit better. Check your lease for rules on propane.

## Gear tiers
| Minimum | Better | Serious |
|---|---|---|
| 3 days water and food, lights, radio | 2 weeks of everything, cooking and heat plan | 90-day pantry, water treatment and collection, safe stored fuel |

## Day-90 check
What runs out first? For most families it is water, fresh food, and fuel. Plan how you will collect, grow, or share once stores run dry.
