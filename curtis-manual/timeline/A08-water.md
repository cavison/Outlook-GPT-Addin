By the end of the first week, stored water is going fast. City water may have low pressure or a boil advisory; a home well has been dry since the power went off. Water is now a daily job.

**What's happening now:** you are rationing jugs, flushing with bathtub water, and looking for more. Take this calmly and set up a system.

### How much you really need

FEMA's minimum is **1 gallon per person per day** for drinking and cooking. Add more for heat, nursing mothers, sick people and pets. Washing, dishes and flushing take far more. Track your use daily ([[W-14]]).

### Hidden water in the house

- **Water heater:** 30–80 gallons of clean water. Turn off its power or gas first, then drain from the bottom valve ([[W-01]]).
- **Toilet tanks** (not bowls): usable if no chemical cleaners were added ([[W-02]]).
- **Pipes:** open the highest faucet, then drain from the lowest.

### Outside sources

- **Rain and roof runoff:** fairly clean but picks up roof debris and bird droppings. Treat before drinking ([[W-10]]).
- **Snow and ice:** plentiful in a Michigan winter. Melt it — don't eat it, which chills you. Treat it like any outdoor water ([[W-11]]).
- **Streams, ponds, drains and rivers:** may carry germs, farm runoff, fertilizer and chemicals. Farm fields and storm drains are common in Ottawa County. Avoid water near roads, fields, or with an oily sheen or odor.
- **Neighbors' wells:** a neighbor with a hand pump or generator may share ([[W-12]]).

### Treatment

1. **Settle and pre-filter** cloudy water through cloth or coffee filters ([[W-06]]).
2. **Boil:** rolling boil for **1 minute** (3 minutes above 6,500 feet). This kills germs ([[W-05]]).
3. **Unscented bleach** (6% or 8.25%): per EPA, 6% → **8 drops per gallon** (16 if cloudy); 8.25% → **6 drops per gallon** (12 if cloudy). Stir, wait **30 minutes**, and check for a faint chlorine smell ([[W-04]]).
4. **Filters** remove bacteria and protozoa; most do not remove viruses or chemicals. Pair a filter with bleach or boiling ([[W-08]]).
5. **Solar disinfection (SODIS):** clear plastic bottles in strong sun for a full day; less useful in a Michigan winter ([[W-09]]).

None of these remove fuel, pesticides or heavy metals. If water might be chemically contaminated, find another source.

### Wells without power

Options: a hand pump installed in the well, a well bucket (a narrow cylinder lowered into the casing), or a generator connected through a **transfer switch** by a licensed electrician. Never backfeed the house through a dryer outlet — it can kill line workers ([[P-06]]).

### Storage and labeling

Keep treated water in clean, covered, food-grade containers. Label clearly: **DRINKING** or **NOT FOR DRINKING** (flushing, washing). Use a dipper, never hands ([[W-13]]).

## Priorities
1. Ration drinking water first; use non-drinking water for flushing.
2. Tap the water heater and pipes.
3. Set up collection: rain, snow, a neighbor's well.
4. Treat all non-stored water.
5. Label every container.

## What goes wrong
- Drinking untreated snowmelt or pond water.
- Using scented or "splash-less" bleach.
- Draining a water heater with the burner or element still on.
- Mixing drinking and flushing containers.
- Backfeeding a well pump through an outlet.

> **Who's most at risk:** infants (formula must be made with safe water — [[K-10]]), older adults who drink too little, sick people with diarrhea, and well households.

> **Renters:** No yard or wood stove? Your water heater may be shared or locked — ask now. Store extra jugs in closets, and collect rain in buckets on a balcony if allowed. Know the nearest public water point.

## Gear tiers
| Minimum | Better | Serious |
|---|---|---|
| Stored jugs, unscented bleach, pot for boiling | Gravity filter, rain barrel, labeled containers | Hand pump on well, large tank, multiple treatment methods |

## Day-90 check
Can you produce enough safe water every day without stores? A sustainable routine: collect (rain, snow, well), settle, filter, disinfect, store labeled — the same steps each morning.
