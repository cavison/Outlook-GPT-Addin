Long outages are hardest on people who were already depending on others or on electricity. In past major outages and heat waves, many of the deaths were older adults, people who relied on medical equipment, and people living alone. Caring for them is not extra credit. It is the main work of a good household and a good street.

Animals depend on you too. Pets and livestock need food, water, warmth and sanitation for 90 days, and planning for them keeps people from making dangerous choices like staying behind in an unsafe home.

## Priorities
1. **Older adults: watch for cold, heat, confusion and falls.** Older bodies lose and gain heat poorly. Check temperature, fluids, medication and mental state daily. See [[K-08]], [[H-11]] and [[H-12]].
2. **Power-dependent medical devices: plan backup and know the threshold.** Have backup batteries or a power source, register with your utility's medical program if offered, and decide in advance when the person must go to a hospital or shelter. See [[K-09]].
3. **People with disabilities: keep communication, sanitation and mobility working.** Spare hearing aid batteries, accessible toilet setup, clear paths, and a plan for moving someone who can't walk. See [[S-09]].
4. **Infants and pregnancy.** Safe formula with safe water, diapers, safe sleep (on the back, on a flat firm surface, not in adult beds or with heavy blankets), and a plan for labor. See [[K-10]].
5. **Check on isolated neighbors every day.** See [[K-04]] and [[Q-07]].
6. **Pets and livestock: food, water, warmth and waste.** Store at least the same days of pet food and water as for people; include pets in evacuation plans. See [[K-11]].

## What goes wrong
- **Hypothermia and heat illness in older adults** that develop slowly and are missed because the person says they are "fine." Confusion is a warning sign.
- **Medical device batteries run out** with no plan for the next step.
- **Unsafe formula.** Powdered formula mixed with unsafe water can make infants very sick. Use safe water and follow the label exactly; never water down formula to stretch it.
- **Unsafe sleep for warmth.** Adults sleeping with infants in piles of blankets can smother them. Warm the room and the baby's clothing instead.
- **People refusing to leave because of pets.** Many shelters now accept pets or work with nearby pet shelters; ask early.
- **Livestock without water.** Animals need much more water than people; a well pump without power stops it. Plan hauling or a hand pump. See [[W-12]].
- **Group homes and senior communities** losing staff or heat. Families and neighbors should check on residents.

## Gear tiers
| Need | Minimum | Better | Serious |
|---|---|---|---|
| Older adults | Daily check, extra blankets, medication list | Thermometer in their room, fall-proofed paths | Warm-room setup and backup plan to move them |
| Medical devices | Spare batteries, list of device watts | Power station sized for device | Generator or solar backup, utility registry, written evacuation threshold |
| Infants | Diapers, ready-to-feed or formula and safe water | Cloth diaper backup, safe sleep space | Birth plan, midwife or doctor contact, newborn kit |
| Pets/livestock | 2 weeks food and water | 90 days food, carrier, leash, vet records | Stored feed and bedding, water hauling setup, livestock shelter plan |

## Day-90 check
- Is each vulnerable person in the household and on the block in the same or better health than at day 30?
- Are medical devices still powered, or has the person moved somewhere safer?
- Are pets and animals healthy, fed and watered?
- Is the infant's supply of diapers, formula and safe water steady?

## Michigan notes
Consumers Energy and other Michigan utilities offer medical certification or critical-care programs that can give notice and payment protections; they do not guarantee power. Ask your utility now. In winter, animals need wind shelter and unfrozen water; heated waterers will stop working, so break ice at least twice a day. Area Agency on Aging of Western Michigan serves older adults in the region.

> **Renters:** Many leases limit pets; know your building's rules for emergencies. If you care for an older parent in an apartment, find the nearest warming center and accessible shelter now.

**Who's most at risk:** Adults over 65, especially living alone; people on oxygen, CPAP, dialysis or powered mobility devices; infants and pregnant people; people with dementia or disabilities; residents of group settings; and animals left without water.
