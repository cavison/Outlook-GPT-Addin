After disasters, disease from poor sanitation and dirty hands often harms more people than the event itself. Diarrhea, skin infections and stomach bugs spread fast when toilets don't flush and water is short. This chapter is not glamorous, but it keeps people alive.

**What's happening now:** toilets may still flush with a bucket of water, but water is precious. Garbage is piling up. Hands are dirty.

### When toilets stop flushing

If city sewer or water is down, or your septic system has an electric pump, stop flushing normally. You can pour a bucket of non-drinking water into the bowl to flush only if the sewer is working; if sewers are backing up, stop entirely.

**The two-bucket system** ([[S-01]]):

- One bucket for urine (can be diluted and poured away from water sources, or into a lined area).
- One bucket for solids, lined with a heavy trash bag, with a seat. Cover every use with sawdust, kitty litter, peat or shredded leaves.
- Keeping them separate cuts smell a great deal.

### Disposal

Tie off bags when half full, double-bag, and store in a covered can away from the house until collection resumes ([[S-02]]). If no collection is coming, bury waste at least 200 feet from wells, streams and lakes, and below the topsoil, or use a properly dug latrine ([[S-03]]). Follow any county guidance — the Ottawa County Department of Public Health may set up disposal points.

### Handwashing with little water

Wash with soap after the toilet, before food, and after caring for sick people. A tippy-tap — a jug on a string with a foot pedal — washes hands with a cup of water ([[S-04]]). Hand sanitizer helps but does not replace soap on dirty hands.

### Bathing, laundry and dishes

- **Bathing:** a sponge bath with a quart of warm water ([[S-05]]).
- **Laundry:** bucket and plunger; wash only what matters (underwear, socks, towels for sick people) ([[S-06]]).
- **Dishes:** three tubs — wash, rinse, sanitize with a little bleach ([[S-07]]). Wipe plates first to save water.

### Garbage and pests

No trash pickup means rats, flies and raccoons. Keep food waste sealed, burn nothing plastic, compost plant scraps away from the house, and keep garbage in metal or heavy plastic cans with tight lids ([[S-08]]).

### Menstrual, infant and incontinence care

Stock pads, cloth pads or a menstrual cup; diapers or cloth diapers with covers; adult incontinence products. Plan how to wash or dispose of them ([[S-09]]).

### Sickness in the house

If someone has diarrhea or vomiting, give them their own bucket and towel if possible, wash hands every time, and disinfect surfaces with bleach solution ([[S-10]], [[M-08]]).

## Priorities
1. Stop normal flushing if sewer or water is down; set up the two-bucket toilet.
2. Handwashing station with soap by the toilet and kitchen.
3. Bag, store and dispose of waste safely.
4. Control garbage and pests.
5. Plan bathing, laundry and dishes on a water budget.

## What goes wrong
- Flushing into a backed-up sewer and flooding the basement.
- Waste buried near a well or creek.
- Skipping handwashing because water is short.
- Garbage left open, drawing rats.
- One sick person infects the whole house.

> **Who's most at risk:** infants and toddlers, older adults, people with weak immune systems, and anyone caring for sick family members.

> **Renters:** No yard or wood stove? You cannot dig a latrine, so the two-bucket system and double-bagging are your plan. Store sealed bags in a covered bin on a balcony or in a cold storage room, and talk with building management about where waste will go.

Is waste being handled the same safe way every day? Are you running low on bags, soap or cover material? Collect leaves and sawdust now for the months ahead.
