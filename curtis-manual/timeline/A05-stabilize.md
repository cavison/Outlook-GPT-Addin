The power just went out. In the first few hours you do not know if it will last one hour or one month. Act as if it might be long — the steps cost almost nothing and protect you if it is.

**What's happening now:** lights are off, the furnace is silent, phones still work for now. Stay calm and work through the list.

### The first hour

- **Account for everyone.** Who is home, who is at work or school? Use the communication plan ([[A04]]).
- **Check anyone on medical devices.** How much battery is left on oxygen or CPAP? Start that clock now ([[K-09]]).
- **Get safe light.** Flashlights and headlamps, not candles if you can avoid them.
- **Check on neighbors,** especially older people living alone ([[Q-07]]).
- **Look for hazards:** downed lines (stay 30+ feet back and call the utility), gas smell, fire.

### Protect your appliances

Turn off or unplug major appliances and electronics: furnace, computers, TVs, microwave, well pump if you can. When power returns it can surge or flicker. **Leave one light switched on** so you know when power is back.

### Fill water now

If you are on city water, pressure may last for a while, then drop. Fill the bathtub (for flushing and washing), and fill clean jugs and pots for drinking ([[Q-03]], [[W-03]]). On a well, you have only what is in the pressure tank — use it carefully.

### Protect the cold chain

Keep the fridge and freezer closed. Per USDA, a closed fridge keeps food safe about **4 hours**; a full freezer about **48 hours**, half full about **24 hours**. In winter you may be able to move food outside or into a cold garage, but watch for thawing in sun and for animals ([[Q-04]], [[F-13]]).

### Short or long?

Signs that point to a long outage:

- Your utility's outage map or line gives no restoration estimate, or estimates in days.
- Damage is widespread: ice on every tree, poles down on main roads, whole towns dark.
- Radio reports of transmission or substation damage, or a cause like a solar storm or attack.
- Neighboring counties and states are also out.

When in doubt, move to the long-outage plan by the end of day one ([[Q-09]]).

### Cash and fuel early

If it looks long, get cash and top off vehicle fuel early, before lines form and limits begin. Card systems and gas pumps need power ([[R-07]]).

### Stay or go?

Most people should shelter in place: your supplies are at home. Consider leaving if you cannot keep anyone warm or cool enough, a medical device cannot be powered, water is gone, or authorities order evacuation. Leave early, in daylight, with a destination ([[Q-10]], [[X-06]]).

## Priorities
1. Everyone accounted for, especially medically fragile people.
2. Safe light and hazard check.
3. Fill water.
4. Close the fridge and freezer.
5. Unplug appliances; leave one light on.
6. Decide short vs long.
7. Cash, fuel, and stay-or-go.

## What goes wrong
- Waiting too long to fill water, then losing pressure.
- Opening the fridge "just to check" again and again.
- Lighting candles everywhere and starting a fire.
- Approaching downed lines.
- Waiting until day four to decide it is a long outage.

> **Who's most at risk:** people on oxygen or CPAP, insulin users, older adults living alone, and households on wells (no water from minute one).

> **Renters:** No yard, no wood stove? Fill tubs and jugs right away — upper floors often lose pressure first. Check that your building's emergency lights and exits work, and find out from management whether there is a common warm room or generator.

## Gear tiers
| Minimum | Better | Serious |
|---|---|---|
| Flashlights, radio, water jugs, cooler | Bathtub water bladder, battery backup for medical device, cash on hand | Full first-hour kit in one bin, generator or power station ready, fuel stored |

## Day-90 check
If the outage turns long, the first-day actions set you up: water stored, food sorted, cash in hand, and a clear decision to stay or go.
