Before you buy anything, find out what you actually need. The audit is a calm afternoon at the kitchen table with a pencil. It tells you which gaps would hurt first, so your money and time go to the right places.

**What's happening now:** the power is on. Gather the household and walk through the house together.

### People

For every person, write down:

- Age, and any mobility limits (stairs, walking, lifting).
- Medical conditions: diabetes, heart or lung disease, asthma, seizures, dementia, pregnancy, mental health needs.
- Every prescription: name, dose, how many days are on hand, and whether it needs refrigeration (insulin, some eye drops, some liquid antibiotics).
- **Power-dependent devices**: oxygen concentrators, CPAP/BiPAP, home dialysis, powered wheelchairs, feeding pumps, suction machines. Note how long each battery lasts.
- Glasses, hearing aids, dentures, and their batteries or spares.

### Pets and livestock

Count animals, daily food and water, medications, and how they handle cold. A dog needs water every day; chickens need unfrozen water in January.

### The house

- **Heat:** gas forced-air (needs power for the blower), boiler, heat pump, electric baseboard, wood stove, fireplace? Any heat that works with no electricity?
- **Water:** city water or a private well? A well pump needs electricity.
- **Sewer:** city sewer or septic? Many septic systems drain by gravity; some have electric pumps or lift stations.
- **Cooking:** gas or electric range? A gas range burner can often be lit with a match when the power is out (never use the oven for heat).
- **Warm room:** which room is smallest, interior or south-facing, with few windows, near the bathroom? That is your winter room.
- **Shut-offs:** where are the main water valve, gas meter valve and electric panel? Can everyone over 12 find them?
- **Basement:** sump pump? A sump that stops in spring thaw or heavy rain can flood a basement.

### Skills inventory

Who in the house can cook from scratch, give first aid or CPR, fix plumbing or small engines, garden, can food, fish or hunt, sew, or teach? Who stays calm? Write it down honestly.

### Neighbors

Start a simple street sketch: who lives where, who is elderly or alone, who has a generator, a wood stove, a well, medical training, tools, or small kids. This becomes your block map ([[K-02]]).

### The weak-point list

From everything above, write the **five things that would hurt your household first** in a long outage. For most Michigan homes, the list looks like: no heat without power; a medical device or insulin; water (especially on a well); no way to cook; and no cash. These five drive your shopping in [[A03]] and your plan in [[A04]].

## Priorities
1. List every person's medicines and power-dependent devices.
2. Identify how you will heat, get water, cook, and flush with no power.
3. Pick the warm room.
4. Find and label shut-offs.
5. Write your top-5 weak points.

## What goes wrong
- A prescription runs out in week two because nobody counted pills.
- A family with a well discovers on day one they have no water at all.
- Nobody knew the sump pump was keeping the basement dry.
- People forget older neighbors living alone until it is too late.

> **Who's most at risk:** anyone on oxygen, CPAP, dialysis, or insulin; infants; older adults; people with dementia or limited mobility; homes that are all-electric or on a well.

> **Renters:** No yard or wood stove? Your audit still matters. Note which room in your unit holds heat best, where the building's water and gas shut-offs are, whether upper floors lose water pressure, and whether elevators would strand anyone. Ask the landlord for emergency contacts.

## Gear tiers
| Minimum | Better | Serious |
|---|---|---|
| Written audit, medication list, labeled shut-offs | Shut-off wrench at the gas meter, battery backup for key medical device, block sketch | Backup plan for every weak point, neighbor map shared with the street |

## Day-90 check
Could each person's medical needs be met for 90 days? If not, name what runs out first and when. Talk to your doctor now ([[M-10]]).
