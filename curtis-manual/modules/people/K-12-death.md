Handle a death respectfully, safely, and legally when emergency and funeral services are delayed.

## You need
- Gloves, masks, sheets or body bag, plastic sheeting
- Notebook, pen

## Steps
1. Call 911 or the police. Follow their instructions; they will involve the medical examiner when required.
2. Wear gloves; wash hands afterward. Cover the body with a clean sheet.
3. Keep the body in the coolest room or area, away from living and food spaces, out of sun; keep pets away.
4. Write down the person's name, time found, what happened, medicines, and who you notified. Keep ID documents with the body.

## Caution
Do not bury, burn, or move the body elsewhere on your own unless authorities direct it — it may break Michigan law and complicate death certificates and estates. WHO notes that bodies of people who died from injuries are rarely an infection risk, but follow basic infection control, especially with infectious illness.

## Field note
Grief is real even in a crisis — give children age-appropriate truth and a small ritual (a candle, words). Ask local clergy or funeral homes what help they can give.
