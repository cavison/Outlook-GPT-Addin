Get neighbors together early so the block can share work, skills, and information.

## You need
- Notebook and pens, clipboard, a printed agenda
- Block map template ([[K-02]])

## Steps
1. Pick a time and a visible outdoor spot (a driveway); knock on every door to invite.
2. Go around: names, house number, who lives there, needs, skills (nurse, mechanic, ham radio).
3. Quietly ask who is vulnerable: older adults, infants, power-dependent medical devices ([[K-09]]), people with no heat.
4. Give each volunteer 1–2 jobs: wellness checks, water, info runner, kids' activities.
5. Agree on the next meeting time and a bulletin spot for notes.

## Caution
Do not publicly announce who has stored food, fuel, guns, or generators. Collect sensitive info privately and share it on a need-to-know basis.

## Field note
Keep it under 30 minutes, standing up, in daylight — people come back to short meetings. Hudsonville-area churches and schools often become hubs; send a runner to find out what they offer.
