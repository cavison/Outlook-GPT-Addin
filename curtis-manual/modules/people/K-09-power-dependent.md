Plan for oxygen concentrators, CPAP, ventilators, powered wheelchairs, home dialysis, or refrigerated medicines when the power is out.

## You need
- Device manuals (watts, battery run time)
- Spare batteries, battery power station, generator/inverter
- Backup oxygen cylinders from your supplier; written plan

## Steps
1. Register: ask Consumers Energy about its Medical Certification program (a doctor's form can temporarily delay shutoff for nonpayment, and identifies you as a medical customer) — it does not guarantee faster restoration. Also tell your county emergency management and local fire department.
2. List each device's watts and how many hours your backup batteries last.
3. Plan two backup power sources (battery station, safe outdoor generator, car inverter) and backup supplies like oxygen cylinders.
4. Set an evacuation threshold in writing (for example, 'when backup drops below 12 hours, we go to the hospital or a shelter with power') and leave early, while roads and phones still work.

## Caution
Do not wait until batteries are empty to seek help. Never run a generator indoors or in a garage; keep oxygen away from flames, stoves, and smoking.

## Field note
Ask your oxygen or device supplier now what their outage plan is and how many backup cylinders they will provide. Many community shelters and fire stations let medical users charge devices — find out which ones in Ottawa County.
