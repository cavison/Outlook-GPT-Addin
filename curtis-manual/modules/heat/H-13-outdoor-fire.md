Build an outdoor fire for cooking, boiling water or warmth, and keep it under control.

## You need
- Tinder: dry grass, birch bark, dryer lint, cotton balls with petroleum jelly
- Kindling: pencil- to thumb-thick dry sticks
- Fuel wood: wrist-thick and larger
- Lighter, matches (in a waterproof container), ferro rod
- Bucket of water, shovel

## Steps
1. Check local rules. In southern Lower Michigan, burn rules are set by your city or township and fire department; northern Michigan uses DNR burn permits.
2. Pick a spot in a fire ring or on bare dirt, away from buildings, fences and overhanging branches. Clear a circle of leaves and grass.
3. Gather all three materials before you light anything: a double handful of tinder, an armful of kindling, a stack of fuel.
4. Put tinder in the center, then lean kindling over it like a teepee, leaving a gap on the wind side.
5. Light the tinder from the wind side, low down.
6. Blow gently at the base. Add bigger kindling, then fuel, as each layer catches.
7. Keep water and a shovel next to the fire. Never leave it.
8. To put it out: drown it, stir it, drown it again until it is cold to the back of your hand.

## Caution
Never use gasoline to start or revive a fire. Its vapor explodes. Do not build fires on windy, dry days.

## Field note
White birch bark burns even when damp because of the oils in it. Peel only loose bark from fallen birch; stripping bark from a living tree can kill it.
