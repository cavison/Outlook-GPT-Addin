Stay cool in a summer outage and spot heat exhaustion and heat stroke early.

## Signs
**Heat exhaustion:** heavy sweating; cold, pale, clammy skin; fast, weak pulse; nausea or vomiting; muscle cramps; tiredness or weakness; dizziness; headache; fainting.
**Heat stroke (911 emergency):** body temperature 103°F (39.4°C) or higher; hot, red, dry or damp skin; fast, strong pulse; headache; dizziness; nausea; confusion; passing out.

## You need
- Water (more than the 1 gal/person/day minimum in heat)
- Wet towels, spray bottle, cool water basin
- Light, loose, light-colored clothing
- Battery fan (helps when it is not extremely hot)

## Steps
1. Close blinds and curtains on sunny sides during the day.
2. Open windows at night when outside air is cooler; close them again in the morning.
3. Stay on the lowest floor. Basements are usually coolest.
4. Drink water regularly, before you feel thirsty. Avoid alcohol.
5. Cool skin with wet cloths, a spray bottle and a fan, or soak feet in cool water.
6. Do hard work early morning or evening. Rest in the shade.
7. Heat exhaustion: move to a cooler place, loosen clothes, apply cool wet cloths, sip water. Get help if vomiting or symptoms last over 1 hour.
8. Heat stroke: call 911, move the person somewhere cooler, cool them fast with cool cloths or a cool bath. Do not give drinks.

## Caution
CDC warns that fans alone will not prevent heat illness when it is very hot. If the house is dangerously hot, go to a cooling center, library or a friend with power. Never leave anyone, or a pet, in a parked car.

## Field note
Wet a bedsheet, wring it out and hang it in an open window or doorway at night. Breeze through it cools the air a little. It works best on dry nights; on humid Lake Michigan summer nights, spend your effort on cool skin instead.
