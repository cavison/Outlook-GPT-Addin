Stay warm indoors and out by wearing several loose layers instead of one heavy coat.

## You need
- Base layer: wool or synthetic long underwear (not cotton)
- Middle layer: fleece, wool sweater, down vest
- Outer layer: windproof, water-resistant jacket and pants
- Hat, gloves or mittens, scarf, wool socks, insulated boots

## Steps
1. Base layer next to skin: wool or synthetic that moves sweat away.
2. Middle layer for warmth: fleece, wool or down. Add or remove to stay just warm, not sweaty.
3. Outer layer to block wind and wet snow.
4. Cover your head, neck and hands. Mittens are warmer than gloves.
5. Wear dry wool socks. Change them as soon as feet are damp.
6. Take a layer off before hard work like shoveling, and put it back on when you stop.

## Caution
Cotton (jeans, sweatshirts, cotton socks) holds sweat and melted snow against the skin and pulls heat away fast. Wet clothes are a common path to hypothermia ([[H-11]]).

## Field note
Clothes should be loose. Tight boots or two pairs of socks crammed into one boot cut circulation and make feet colder. In a cold house, wear a hat even indoors and to bed — it is the easiest layer to add.
