Cut heat loss through glass, the weakest part of most walls.

## You need
- Heavy blankets, quilts or thick curtains
- Bubble wrap and a spray bottle of water
- Tape, thumbtacks
- Optional: shrink-film window kit, rigid foam board, rope caulk

## Steps
1. Feel for drafts with a damp hand or a candle-free method: a strip of tissue taped to a pencil.
2. Stuff gaps around the sash with rope caulk, rolled towels or tape.
3. Spray the glass lightly with water and press bubble wrap on, bubbles facing the glass. It sticks for weeks.
4. Add a shrink-film kit if you have one (hair dryer step needs power; tape it tight without if not).
5. Hang heavy blankets or curtains floor to ceiling and close them at night.
6. On sunny days open curtains on south-facing windows, then close them before sunset.
7. For windows you never need, cut rigid foam board to fit snug inside the frame.

## Caution
Do not block every window in a room you are heating with fuel. Keep at least one window you can open for fresh air and as an emergency exit, and keep bedroom escape windows usable.

## Field note
Bubble wrap on wet glass is cheap, reusable and still lets light in, which matters in short Michigan December days. Label each sheet with the window it fits and store it flat for next winter.
