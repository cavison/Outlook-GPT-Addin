Estimate, prepare and store enough dry wood to get through a Michigan winter outage.

## You need
- Firewood (hardwood like oak, maple, ash, beech if you can get it)
- Splitting maul or axe, wedge, chopping block
- Gloves, safety glasses, sturdy boots
- Pallets or rails to keep wood off the ground; a top cover (tarp or roof)

## Steps
1. Estimate need (see Table). Track how much you actually burn per week and adjust.
2. Buy or cut wood early. Most wood needs at least 6 months of drying after splitting; oak often longer.
3. Split rounds to wrist-to-calf thickness. Smaller splits dry and catch faster.
4. Stack off the ground on pallets or rails, bark side up, in single rows with air gaps.
5. Cover only the top. Leave the sides open so wind dries the wood.
6. Keep the main pile at least 30 feet from the house; bring a few days' worth near the door.
7. Check dryness: dry wood is lighter, has cracked ends and sounds hollow when knocked together. A moisture meter should read under 20%.

## Table
| Item | Figure |
|---|---|
| Full cord | 4 ft high × 4 ft deep × 8 ft long = 128 cubic feet (stacked) |
| Face cord ("rick") | 4 ft × 8 ft × one log length (often 16 in) — about 1/3 of a full cord |
| Seasoning time | 6+ months after splitting (oak often 1–2 years) |
| Dry wood moisture | Under 20% |
| Michigan winter, wood as main heat | Roughly 3–6 full cords per season |
| One warm room only, part of the day | Often far less; track your own use |

The cord estimate is a rough range. It depends heavily on your house size, insulation, stove efficiency, wood species and how cold the winter is. Your own weekly burn log is the real number.

## Caution
Splitting injuries happen when the maul glances off. Keep feet wide apart, split on a block (not the ground), and keep kids and pets well back. Do not stack wood against the house; it hides termites and is a fire path.

## Field note
In west Michigan, many sellers sell "face cords," not full cords. Ask the length of the pieces and do the math before you pay. Also: do not move firewood far from where it was cut; Michigan DNR asks people to buy it local to avoid spreading pests like emerald ash borer.
