Heat with wood without starting a chimney fire or filling the house with smoke.

## You need
- Dry, seasoned firewood ([[H-06]]), kindling, newspaper
- Fireplace screen or closed stove door
- Metal ash bucket with a tight lid, metal shovel
- Smoke and CO alarms, ABC extinguisher
- Optional: stove-top thermometer

## Steps
1. Before winter, have the chimney inspected and cleaned if needed (NFPA recommends yearly inspection).
2. Open the damper fully before lighting.
3. Build a small fire: paper, then kindling, then two or three split logs. Light from the top or bottom, but keep it small at first.
4. Keep a 3-foot clear zone around the stove or hearth.
5. Burn only dry wood. No trash, cardboard, treated or painted wood.
6. Keep the screen closed or the stove door shut. Never leave a fire burning when everyone sleeps in an open fireplace.
7. Shovel cold ashes into a metal bucket with a lid and set it outside, at least 10 feet from the house.

## Caution
Ashes can stay hot for days. Ashes dumped in a paper bag, a plastic bin or next to the garage start house fires every winter.

## Field note
An open fireplace can pull more warm air up the chimney than it puts back into the room. If you have a fireplace but no insert, use it to heat the room right in front of it, and close the damper only once the fire is fully out and cold.
