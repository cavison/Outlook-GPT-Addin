Heat one small room instead of the whole house when the furnace is down in winter.

## You need
- Blankets, quilts, sheets, towels
- Painter's tape, duct tape, thumbtacks or a staple gun
- Cardboard or rugs for the floor
- Battery CO alarm and smoke alarm
- Optional: window insulation kit, foam board

## Steps
1. Pick an interior room with few outside walls and windows, ideally south-facing, close to a bathroom.
2. Close off the rest of the house. Shut doors to unused rooms and close their vents.
3. Hang a blanket over the doorway (two layers if you can) and roll a towel along the bottom gap.
4. Cover windows at night with blankets or curtains; open south windows on sunny days. See [[H-02]].
5. Lay rugs, cardboard or foam on the floor where people sit and sleep.
6. Bring everyone (and pets) into the room. Body heat, cooking and a safe heat source all add up.
7. Put a battery CO alarm and smoke alarm in the room before using any fuel-burning heater.

## Caution
A sealed room plus a fuel-burning heater can build up carbon monoxide fast. Only use heaters rated for indoor use, follow [[H-04]], and keep a working CO alarm in the room.

## Field note
In lake-effect weather the sunniest room at noon is often not the warmest at 3 a.m. Pick the room with the fewest outside walls first, and use south windows only as a daytime bonus. A room above an attached garage usually loses heat through the floor.
