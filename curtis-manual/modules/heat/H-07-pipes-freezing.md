Protect water pipes for the first days of a winter outage, before you decide whether to drain the house.

## You need
- Pipe foam insulation, rags, old towels
- Duct tape
- Flashlight
- Bucket

## Steps
1. Find pipes on outside walls, in crawl spaces, garages and under sinks.
2. Wrap exposed pipes with foam sleeves, towels or rags, taped in place.
3. Open cabinet doors under sinks on outside walls so room heat reaches the pipes.
4. Let cold water drip from faucets fed by exposed pipes. Moving water freezes more slowly.
5. Disconnect garden hoses and shut off outside spigots from inside.
6. If a pipe freezes, open the faucet and warm the pipe gently with warm towels. Start near the faucet.
7. If the house will stay below freezing for days, drain it instead. See [[H-08]].

## Caution
Never thaw a pipe with a torch or open flame. It can start a fire inside the wall or burst the pipe. If a pipe has split, shut off the main water valve first.

## Field note
Know where your main shutoff is before the outage, and test that it turns. Many Hudsonville-area homes have it in the basement next to the meter; old gate valves can seize after years untouched.

## Michigan notes
**90-day outage that starts in January:** plan to drain the plumbing ([[H-08]]) unless you can hold one room and the pipe runs above freezing for months — dripping and wrapping only buy days. After an ice storm, stay away from trees and lines heavy with ice: limbs fall without warning for days, and any downed line may be live. Heavy wet snow on top of ice can overload roofs (garages, porches, flat or low-slope roofs first); clear from the ground with a roof rake, never climb an icy roof, and leave if you hear cracking or see sagging or doors that stick.
