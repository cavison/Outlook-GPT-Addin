Empty the water lines when the house will stay below freezing, so pipes do not burst.

## You need
- Buckets, garden hose
- Wrench for the water heater drain valve
- Sponge, towels
- RV/marine antifreeze (propylene glycol, labeled non-toxic) — not car antifreeze
- Clean containers to save water for drinking ([[W-01]], [[W-02]])

## Steps
1. Save drinkable water first: fill containers from taps and keep the water heater water.
2. Shut off the main water valve (or turn off the well pump at its breaker).
3. Turn off the water heater: gas to "pilot/off," or its electric breaker.
4. Open the highest faucets in the house, then the lowest ones (basement, outside spigots) so lines drain.
5. Drain the water heater with a hose to a floor drain or outside.
6. Flush toilets, then sponge out the water left in tanks and bowls.
7. Pour a cup of RV antifreeze into each sink, tub and floor drain trap, and into toilet bowls.
8. Tape a note on the main valve: "HOUSE DRAINED — close faucets before turning on."

## Caution
Do not turn a water heater back on until it is completely full again; an empty electric tank burns out its elements. Car antifreeze (ethylene glycol) is poisonous to people and pets — never use it in drains.

## Field note
If you have a well, open the drain on the pressure tank too. Hot-water baseboard systems and boilers need their own draining; if you do not know how, ask a plumber before winter and write the steps on the boiler.
