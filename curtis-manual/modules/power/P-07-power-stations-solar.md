Size a battery power station and solar panel with simple watt-hour math, so you know what it can really run.

## You need
- Device labels (watts)
- Power station spec sheet (Wh capacity, output watts)
- Solar panel spec (rated watts)
- Paper and pencil

## Steps
1. List each device and its watts. Multiply by hours of use per day. That is watt-hours (Wh): W × h = Wh.
2. Add up all the Wh for one day.
3. Plan on using only about 80–85% of the station's rated Wh; the inverter and electronics lose some.
4. Divide usable Wh by your daily Wh. That is how many days one charge lasts.
5. Solar: panel watts × peak sun hours × about 0.75 (real-world losses) = Wh per day.
6. Point the panel at the sun (south), tilt it steep in winter, and keep it clear of snow.
7. Run high-watt items (kettle, hair dryer, space heater) never, or only briefly.

## Table
**Example daily use:**

| Device | Watts | Hours/day | Wh/day |
|---|---|---|---|
| LED lantern | 5 | 5 | 25 |
| Phone charging (×2) | 10 | 3 | 30 |
| Radio | 5 | 4 | 20 |
| CPAP (no heated humidifier) | 40 | 8 | 320 |
| Laptop | 50 | 1 | 50 |
| **Total** | | | **445** |

**Station math:** a 1,000 Wh station × 0.85 = 850 Wh usable. 850 ÷ 445 = about 1.9 days per charge.

**Solar math (100 W panel):**

| Season (Michigan, rough) | Peak sun hours | 100 W × hours × 0.75 |
|---|---|---|
| December–January | about 1–2 | about 75–150 Wh/day |
| June–July | about 5–6 | about 375–450 Wh/day |

Check your own location's numbers with NREL's PVWatts calculator. CPAP draw varies a lot by model and settings; check your machine's manual.

## Caution
Do not run power stations or charge lithium batteries unattended near bedding or exits, and never charge a frozen battery; many lithium batteries should not be charged below freezing. Check the manual.

## Field note
Michigan's cloudy, short winter days are the hard case. Size solar for December, not June. Wipe snow off panels after every lake-effect band, and stand them nearly upright in winter so snow slides off and the low sun hits them square.
