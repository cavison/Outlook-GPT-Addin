Set up a portable generator so it powers your house without poisoning anyone.

## You need
- Portable generator and fuel
- Heavy-duty outdoor extension cords rated for the load
- Battery CO alarms (every level, outside sleeping areas)
- Dry level spot outside; a canopy made for generators if it rains

## Steps
1. Place it outside, at least 20 feet from the house, on dry ground. Never in a garage, basement, shed or porch, even with doors open.
2. Point the exhaust away from windows, doors and vents — yours and your neighbors'.
3. Put battery CO alarms on every floor inside.
4. Turn it off and let it cool before refueling. Never refuel a hot or running generator.
5. Plug appliances directly into the generator with outdoor cords, or use a transfer switch installed by a licensed electrician. Never backfeed a wall outlet ([[P-06]]).

## Caution
Carbon monoxide has no smell or color. If anyone feels dizzy, sick or weak, get everyone outside to fresh air and call 911 ([[Q-02]]).

## Field note
In Michigan winters, snow can drift over the generator and around the exhaust. Clear it every few hours and keep the air intake and exhaust open. Do not move it closer to the house to keep it out of the wind.
