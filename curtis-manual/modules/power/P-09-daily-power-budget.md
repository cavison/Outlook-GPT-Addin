Plan each day's electricity like a household budget, so the important things never go dark.

## You need
- Notebook or the budget sheet below
- Device watt numbers ([[P-05]], [[P-07]])
- Your power sources: generator, power station, solar, car

## Steps
1. List your power sources and how many Wh (or generator hours) you can get per day.
2. Rank devices: 1 = life and safety (medical devices, sump pump, radio, CO alarms' batteries), 2 = food (fridge/freezer cycles), 3 = comfort.
3. Give each device a daily Wh allowance (watts × hours).
4. Charge during the day, when solar works and someone is awake to watch.
5. Charge everything at one time, then shut the source off.
6. Each evening, write down what was used. Adjust tomorrow if you came up short.
7. Keep one full power bank or charged battery set in reserve, never spent.

## Caution
Medical devices come first. If someone depends on oxygen, CPAP or powered medicine storage, tell your utility and local emergency managers ahead of time and have a backup plan to reach power ([[M-09]]).

## Field note
Phones last far longer in airplane mode with screen brightness low, and texts get through on a weak network when calls do not. Check for news at fixed times (for example 8 a.m. and 6 p.m.) instead of leaving the phone on all day.
