Figure out what your generator can run at once, and keep a fuel log so you never run dry by surprise.

## You need
- Your appliances' nameplate labels (watts, or volts and amps)
- Generator manual (rated and starting watts, run time)
- Notebook and pen for the fuel log
- Approved fuel cans; fuel stabilizer

## Steps
1. Read each appliance label. If it shows amps, watts = volts × amps (e.g., 120 V × 5 A = 600 W).
2. List what you really need: fridge, freezer, sump pump, furnace blower, lights, phone charging.
3. Add up the running watts of what will run at the same time.
4. Add the single largest starting surge (motors take extra watts for a few seconds to start).
5. Keep that total under the generator's rated (running) watts. Leave some room to spare.
6. Stagger big loads: run the freezer, then the well pump — not all at once.
7. Log every refuel: date, time, gallons added, hours run. After a few days you know your real gallons per day.
8. Store fuel in approved cans away from the house, add stabilizer, and rotate it.

## Table
Typical ranges only. Your appliance label is the real number.

| Appliance | Running watts (typical) | Starting surge |
|---|---|---|
| Refrigerator | 100–800 | Up to about 3× running |
| Chest freezer | 100–500 | Up to about 3× running |
| Furnace blower (gas furnace) | 300–900 | Higher at start |
| Sump pump (1/3–1/2 hp) | 800–1,050 | About 2× running or more |
| Well pump (1/2 hp) | 1,000 | About 2× running or more |
| Microwave | 600–1,500 | — |
| Coffee maker | 600–1,200 | — |
| LED bulb | 5–15 | — |
| Phone charger | 5–20 | — |
| Laptop | 30–100 | — |
| Space heater | 1,500 | — |

**Example:** fridge 700 + furnace blower 600 + lights 50 + phones 40 = 1,390 W running. Add fridge surge (say 1,400 extra) = about 2,800 W peak. A 3,500 W generator covers it; a 2,000 W one does not.

**Fuel log columns:** Date | Time | Gallons added | Hour meter / hours run | Notes (oil checked?)

## Caution
Electric space heaters, water heaters and electric ranges draw very large loads; most portable generators cannot run them along with anything else. Overloading can damage the generator and appliances.

## Field note
A generator does not need to run 24/7. Running the fridge and freezer a few hours, several times a day, can stretch fuel a long way — keep doors shut between runs and check temperatures with a fridge thermometer ([[Q-04]]). Check the oil each refuel; many small engines need an oil change after their first several hours and then at intervals in the manual.
