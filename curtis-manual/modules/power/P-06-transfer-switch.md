Connect a generator to house circuits the legal, safe way, and never with a "suicide cord."

## You need
- Licensed electrician
- Electrical permit (required in Michigan for this work; your city or township issues it)
- Transfer switch or a listed interlock kit made for your panel, plus a generator inlet box
- Generator cord matched to the inlet

## Steps
1. Decide which circuits you need: furnace, fridge, sump pump, well, some lights and outlets.
2. Have a licensed electrician install a transfer switch or interlock with an outdoor inlet, with a permit and inspection.
3. In an outage, first turn off the main breaker (or set the transfer switch to "generator").
4. Start the generator outside, 20+ feet away ([[P-04]]), and plug its cord into the inlet.
5. Turn on circuits one at a time, largest first, staying under the generator's rating ([[P-05]]).
6. When power returns: turn off circuits, shut down and unplug the generator, then switch back to utility.

## Caution
Backfeeding — plugging a generator into a wall outlet with a male-to-male cord — sends power back out on the lines. It can electrocute utility line workers and neighbors, start fires, and destroy the generator when grid power returns. Never do it.

## Field note
An interlock kit is often much cheaper than a full transfer switch, but it must be listed for your exact panel brand and model. Bring a photo of your panel's label to the electrician so they can quote the right part.
