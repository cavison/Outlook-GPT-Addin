Keep the right batteries fresh, ready and safe for 90 days.

## You need
- Clear bins or a tackle box, labeled
- Marker for dates
- Rechargeable NiMH cells (low self-discharge type) and a charger
- Spare alkaline or lithium AA/AAA
- Tape for the ends of loose batteries

## Steps
1. Standardize: pick lights and radios that use the same size (AA is easiest to find).
2. Store batteries in a cool, dry room in their packages or a case, so ends cannot touch.
3. Write the purchase date on each pack. Use oldest first.
4. Take batteries out of devices you store for months; leaking cells ruin devices.
5. Do not mix old and new, or different types, in one device.
6. Keep lithium AA cells for cold-weather gear and outdoor use; they hold up better in the cold.
7. Charge NiMH cells during the day from solar or a power station ([[P-07]]).
8. Tape the ends of used batteries before throwing out or recycling.

## Caution
Button and coin batteries can cause serious internal burns within hours if a child swallows one. Keep them locked away; if swallowed, get emergency care right away. Do not charge lithium-ion packs unattended or on a bed.

## Field note
Cold makes batteries seem dead when they are not. In a Michigan winter, keep spare cells in an inside pocket and swap a "dead" cold battery for a warm one — the cold one often works again after it warms up.
