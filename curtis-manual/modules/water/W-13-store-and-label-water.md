Keep stored water safe for months and know at a glance what's drinkable and what isn't.

## You need
- Food-grade containers or store-bought bottled water
- Masking tape or labels and a permanent marker
- Unscented bleach (for treating stored tap water if needed)

## Steps
1. Label every container: DRINKING or NON-DRINKING, the date filled, and how it was treated (city tap, boiled, bleached).
2. Store in a cool, dark place, off bare concrete if you can (on wood or a shelf), away from sunlight.
3. Keep water far from gasoline, kerosene, paint, pesticides, and cleaning products.
4. Rotate: replace water you filled yourself every 6 months. Keep store-bought water unopened until its expiration or use-by date.
5. Once a container is opened, use it within a few days and keep it covered. Pour, don't dip, to avoid hands in the water.

## Caution
Never store water in containers that held chemicals, bleach, or antifreeze, and never put non-drinking water in a jug that looks like a drinking-water jug. Use a different color tape or container for non-drinking water.

## Field note
Colored tape solves the 3 a.m. problem: blue tape = drinking, red tape = flushing/washing only. In a cold Michigan basement or garage, leave headspace in jugs so freezing doesn't split them, and keep drinking water off the floor in basements that may flood from a dead sump pump.
