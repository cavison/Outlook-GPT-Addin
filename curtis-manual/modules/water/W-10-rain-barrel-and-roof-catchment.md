Catch rain from your roof for flushing, washing, gardens — and, after treatment, drinking.

## You need
- Food-grade barrel or trash can with a tight lid
- Window screen and bungee or tape
- Downspout diverter or hacksaw and elbow
- Spigot (optional)
- Bleach, filter, or fuel to boil

## Steps
1. Set the barrel on a stable, level base (cinder blocks) under a downspout. A full 55-gallon barrel weighs over 450 pounds.
2. Let the first rain after a dry spell wash the roof and run to the ground — that first flush carries the most bird droppings, dust, and grit. A simple pipe or bucket diverter does this automatically.
3. Cover the opening with window screen to keep out mosquitoes, leaves, and kids, and keep the lid on.
4. Use as-is for flushing and washing. For drinking, settle, filter, and disinfect it ([[W-06]], [[W-08]], [[W-04]] or [[W-05]]).

## Caution
Roof water can carry bird and animal droppings, and asphalt shingles, treated wood, and old metal or lead flashing can add chemicals that filters and bleach may not remove. Treat roof water as non-drinking unless there is no better source, and always disinfect it.

## Field note
West Michigan gets roughly 35–40 inches of precipitation a year, and about 0.6 gallon runs off each square foot of roof per inch of rain — so even a small roof can fill a barrel in one storm. Barrels freeze and crack in Michigan winters: drain them and disconnect the downspout before hard freezes, and catch snowmelt in buckets instead.
