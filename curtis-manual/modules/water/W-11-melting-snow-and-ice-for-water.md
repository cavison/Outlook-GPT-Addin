Turn a Michigan winter's snow and ice into usable water — with the right steps so you don't waste fuel or get sick.

## You need
- Large pot with lid
- Heat source (stove used safely, wood stove, or outdoor fire)
- Clean shovel or bucket for collecting
- Coffee filter or cloth

## Steps
1. Collect fresh, clean snow or clear ice from an undisturbed area — away from roads, plow piles, roofs, rooftop vents, pets, and yellow or gray snow.
2. Put a little liquid water in the pot first, then add snow a bit at a time. This melts faster and keeps the pot from scorching.
3. Keep adding snow as it melts. Snow is mostly air, so it takes a lot of snow to make a pot of water; ice gives much more water for the space.
4. Strain through a cloth, then bring to a rolling boil for 1 minute ([[W-05]]) or treat with bleach ([[W-04]]).

## Caution
Don't eat snow or ice to stay hydrated in cold weather — it lowers body temperature and can push someone toward hypothermia. Melt it first. Never melt snow on a camp stove or grill indoors.

## Field note
Set dark pots or buckets of snow near (not on) a wood stove, or in a sunny south window, to pre-melt without extra fuel. Lake ice and icicles from metal gutters can be used too, but icicles from asphalt-shingle roofs pick up grit and roof chemicals — treat them like roof water ([[W-10]]).
