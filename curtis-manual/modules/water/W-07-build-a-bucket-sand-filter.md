A simple gravity filter from buckets, gravel, and sand that clears large amounts of cloudy water for later disinfection.

## You need
- Food-grade 5-gallon bucket with lid, plus a second clean bucket
- Drill and a short piece of tubing or a bucket spigot
- Clean washed gravel (pea gravel) and fine sand (washed play sand or pool filter sand)
- Clean cotton cloth

## Steps
1. Drill a hole near the bottom and fit a spigot or tube. Put 2–3 inches of washed gravel in the bottom, covering the outlet.
2. Add a thick layer of fine sand (most of the bucket — about 12 inches or more works better than a thin layer). Rinse the sand first until runoff is fairly clear.
3. Lay a cloth on top of the sand so pouring water doesn't dig holes in it.
4. Pour settled water ([[W-06]]) gently onto the cloth.
5. Collect the water from the spigot into a clean bucket. Throw out the first few batches until it runs clear.
6. Always disinfect the filtered water by boiling or bleach ([[W-05]], [[W-04]]).

## Caution
A homemade sand filter is not a disinfecting filter. It reduces dirt and some germs, but not enough to make water safe to drink without boiling or bleach.

## Field note
Biosand filters used worldwide work better when the sand stays wet and is used daily, because a living layer forms on top that eats germs over a few weeks. That layer dies if the filter dries out or freezes — keep a long-term sand filter indoors in a Michigan winter. When flow slows, gently stir and scoop off only the top half-inch of sand.
