Your tank-type water heater holds a large reserve of clean water (typically 30–80 gallons) that you can draw out when taps stop working.

## You need
- Clean bucket or pot and clean jugs
- Short garden hose (optional)
- Flathead screwdriver if the drain valve has a slot
- Coffee filter or clean cloth

## Steps
1. Turn the heater OFF: switch off its breaker (electric) or turn the gas control to OFF or PILOT (gas). Let hot water cool for several hours if you can.
2. Close the cold-water inlet valve on top of the tank so dirty water can't flow in if city service is contaminated or restored.
3. Open a hot-water faucet somewhere in the house (or lift the tank's relief-valve lever) to let air in, so water will flow out.
4. Open the drain valve at the bottom into a clean container. The first water may carry rust and sediment — set it aside for flushing or let it settle.
5. Strain through a coffee filter or cloth. If you're unsure the water was clean when it entered the tank (for example a boil-water advisory), disinfect it ([[W-04]], [[W-05]]).
6. Close the drain valve when done. Leave the heater OFF until the tank is full again.

## Caution
Water and the tank can be scalding hot for hours after power loss. And never turn an electric heater back on until the tank is full — heating elements burn out in seconds without water around them.

## Field note
Sediment often clogs the plastic drain valve. If water trickles, gently opening and closing the valve a few times can clear it. Hard west Michigan well and city water builds sediment fast, so draining a few gallons once a year now (per the manufacturer's manual) makes this much easier in an emergency.
