Many homes in rural Ottawa County use private wells with electric pumps; here's how to get water when the pump stops.

## You need
- Your pump's voltage and horsepower (on the control box or from your well driller)
- Generator sized for the pump and a transfer switch or interlock installed by a licensed electrician
- Clean containers
- Bleach and a well test kit from your county health department

## Steps
1. Know your system before an outage: most deep wells in this area have a submersible pump, often 240 volts. Write down its voltage and horsepower.
2. When power fails, the pressure tank still holds a few gallons. Draw it from the lowest faucet into clean containers before it's gone.
3. Collect drinking water in containers; don't waste pressure flushing toilets — flush with a bucket instead.
4. To run the pump, use a generator large enough for the pump's starting load, connected through a transfer switch or interlock. Run it in short bursts to refill the pressure tank and your containers ([[P-04]]).
5. If the well may have been flooded or the water looks, smells, or tastes different, don't drink it. Use stored water and get it tested; shock chlorinate the well per health department instructions.

## Caution
Never backfeed house wiring by plugging a generator into an outlet (a "suicide cord") — it can kill you or a line worker. Pump wiring is 240 volts; have an electrician make the connection.

## Field note
Ottawa County Department of Public Health and Michigan EGLE have well water testing kits and guidance. After any flood, have the well tested for bacteria before drinking. A manual hand pump can be installed alongside many submersibles in the same well casing (depending on water depth) — ask a licensed Michigan well driller before the emergency.
