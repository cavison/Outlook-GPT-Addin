Use clear plastic bottles and strong sunlight to disinfect small amounts of clear water when you have no fuel or bleach.

## You need
- Clear, clean PET plastic bottles (soda or water bottles marked #1), 2 liters or smaller, labels removed
- A sunny, reflective spot (metal roof, foil, or a car hood)

## Steps
1. Use only clear water. If you can't read newspaper-size print through the full bottle, settle and filter first ([[W-06]]).
2. Fill the bottle three-quarters, cap and shake hard for 20 seconds to add air, then fill to the top and cap.
3. Lay bottles on their sides in full sun on a reflective surface.
4. Leave 6 hours in full sun. If the sky is more than half cloudy, leave 2 full days. Don't use it on days of steady rain.
5. Drink straight from the bottle. Don't pour into a dirty cup.

## Caution
SODIS does not work with cloudy water, glass or colored bottles, or containers over about 2 liters, and it does nothing against chemicals. It's a last resort when you can't boil or bleach.

## Field note
In west Michigan from about November through February, the sun is low and lake-effect clouds are common, so SODIS is unreliable in winter — save it for late spring through early fall. Scratched, old bottles let less UV through; swap them out.
