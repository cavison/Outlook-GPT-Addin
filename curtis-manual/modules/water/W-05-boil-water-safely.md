Boiling is the surest way to kill germs in water, including the ones bleach can miss.

## You need
- Pot with a lid
- Outdoor-safe heat: camp stove, grill, or wood fire used outside; or a gas range indoors if your gas still works
- Coffee filter or cloth
- Clean containers

## Steps
1. If the water is cloudy, settle and filter it first ([[W-06]]).
2. Bring it to a rolling boil — big bubbles that don't stop when you stir.
3. Keep it boiling for 1 minute. (3 minutes above 6,500 feet — not an issue anywhere in Michigan.)
4. Let it cool, covered, then pour into clean, covered containers. Pour between two containers to improve flat taste.

## Caution
Camp stoves, propane and charcoal grills, and outdoor burners make carbon monoxide — use them outside only, away from doors and windows. Boiling does not remove fuel, chemicals, or lead.

## Field note
Put a lid on the pot: water reaches a boil much faster and you burn less fuel. Most of west Michigan sits around 600–1,000 feet above sea level and Michigan's highest point is under 2,000 feet, so 1 minute is always the right boil time here.
