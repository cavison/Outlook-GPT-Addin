Store as much water as you can, in the right containers, before or right after the power fails.

## You need
- Food-grade water containers (marked HDPE #2 or sold for water)
- Clean 2-liter soda bottles
- Bathtub and optional tub liner bag
- Dish soap and unscented bleach for cleaning

## Steps
1. Bathtub: scrub, rinse, and fill. Use for flushing, washing, and dishes. Drink it only if you used a food-safe tub liner bag and disinfect it first.
2. Containers: use food-grade jugs or clean soda bottles. Wash with soap and water, rinse, then sanitize with 1 teaspoon unscented bleach per quart of water, swish, and rinse.
3. Don't use milk or juice jugs (they're hard to clean of milk protein and the plastic breaks down) or anything that held chemicals.
4. Fill, cap tightly, and store in a cool, dark place out of sunlight and away from heat.
5. Keep containers away from gasoline, paint, pesticides, and solvents — fumes can pass through plastic.

## Caution
A bathtub, bucket, or open cooler of water is a drowning hazard for small children. Cover buckets and keep the bathroom door shut.

## Field note
Stack 2-liter soda bottles of water in empty freezer space now. Frozen bottles keep the freezer cold longer in an outage and become drinking water as they thaw. Leave an inch of headspace so the bottle doesn't split.
