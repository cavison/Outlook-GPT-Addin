Support a possibly broken or sprained limb to reduce pain and further injury until care is available.

> This is not a substitute for training or professional care. Take an American Red Cross first aid/CPR class and a Stop the Bleed class before you need them. If 911 or any medical service is reachable, use it.

## You need
- Rigid padding: SAM-type splint, cardboard, rolled magazines, boards
- Padding (towels, clothing), triangular bandages, wide cloth strips or elastic wrap
- Cold pack or ice wrapped in cloth

## Steps
1. Splint the limb in the position you found it. Do not try to straighten it.
2. Pad it, then place rigid supports so they extend past the joint above and the joint below the injury.
3. Tie snug but not tight; keep knots off the injured spot.
4. Check fingers or toes below the splint every 15–30 minutes: warm, pink, can feel and move. If numb, cold, pale, or blue, loosen the ties. For sprains: rest, wrapped ice 20 minutes at a time, compression, elevation.

## Caution
An open fracture (bone through skin), a suspected neck/back/hip/thigh fracture, or a limb that is cold and pulseless needs emergency care — do not move a possible spine injury unless the person is in danger where they are.

## Field note
Ice is plentiful in a Michigan winter, but always put a cloth between ice and skin and limit to about 20 minutes. A pillow wrapped around an ankle and taped makes a surprisingly good splint.
