Prevent and handle tick and mosquito bites during warm-weather outages, when people spend far more time outdoors gardening, foraging and hauling.

> This is not a substitute for training or professional care. If any medical service is reachable, use it.

## You need
- Fine-tipped tweezers
- Rubbing alcohol or soap and water; small zip bag or tape
- Permethrin spray for clothing; EPA-registered skin repellent (such as DEET or picaridin)
- Long pants, light-colored clothes, socks to tuck pants into
- Pen and calendar to note bite dates

## Steps
1. **Dress and treat:** long sleeves and pants, tuck pants into socks. Treat clothing and boots (not skin) with permethrin, following the label.
2. **Check every day** after outdoor time: underarms, in and around ears, inside belly button, back of knees, hair, between legs, waist. Check kids and pets too.
3. **Remove a tick** with fine-tipped tweezers: grasp it as close to the skin as you can.
4. Pull **upward with steady, even pressure**. Don't twist or jerk. If mouthparts stay in and you can't remove them easily, leave them and let the skin heal.
5. **Clean** the bite and your hands with rubbing alcohol or soap and water. Put the tick in alcohol, a sealed bag or wrapped in tape; don't crush it with fingers.
6. **Write the date** and body spot on the calendar.
7. **Watch for weeks:** an expanding rash (sometimes bullseye-shaped), fever, chills, aches or fatigue mean seek medical care and mention the tick bite.
8. **Mosquitoes:** dump standing water at least weekly — buckets, tarps, tires, gutters, rain barrels without screens ([[W-10]]).

## Michigan notes
MDHHS reports that the blacklegged (deer) tick, which spreads Lyme disease, has been expanding in Michigan, including the west side of the Lower Peninsula along Lake Michigan. Check current MDHHS tick maps. Mosquitoes in Michigan can carry West Nile virus and, in some years, Eastern equine encephalitis (EEE); listen for MDHHS and county health alerts.

## Caution
Don't burn, paint with nail polish, or smother a tick with petroleum jelly; those folk methods delay removal (CDC). Never spray permethrin on skin, and keep wet permethrin away from cats.

## Field note
Blacklegged tick nymphs are about the size of a poppy seed, so most people never notice the bite. A daily full-body check is the real defense; CDC notes Lyme transmission generally takes many hours of attachment, so removing ticks the same day matters.
