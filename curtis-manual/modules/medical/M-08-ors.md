Replace water and salts lost to diarrhea or vomiting — dehydration from contaminated water is a leading outage risk.

> This is not a substitute for training or professional care. Take an American Red Cross first aid/CPR class and a Stop the Bleed class before you need them. If 911 or any medical service is reachable, use it.

## You need
- 1 liter (about 4¼ cups) safe drinking water
- Level teaspoon measure, sugar, table salt
- Clean container; commercial ORS packets are better if you have them

## Steps
1. Start with 1 liter of safe (boiled and cooled, or disinfected) water.
2. Add 6 level teaspoons of sugar.
3. Add 1/2 level teaspoon of salt.
4. Stir until dissolved. Sip small amounts often (a spoonful every minute or two if vomiting). Make fresh; discard after 24 hours. Keep breastfeeding infants.

## Caution
Measure exactly — too much salt can be dangerous, especially for children. If it tastes saltier than tears, remake it. Seek help for infants, bloody diarrhea, no urine for 8+ hours, confusion, or a person who can't keep anything down.

## Field note
Commercial ORS packets (WHO formula) are inexpensive, store for years, and remove the measuring risk — stock a box per person. Sports drinks are not a substitute for severe dehydration.
