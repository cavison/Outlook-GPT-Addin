Catch wound infections early, when they are easiest to treat.

> This is not a substitute for training or professional care. Take an American Red Cross first aid/CPR class and a Stop the Bleed class before you need them. If 911 or any medical service is reachable, use it.

## You need
- Good light, pen/marker, notebook
- Clean dressings, thermometer

## Steps
1. Check every wound daily in good light when you change the dressing.
2. If there is redness, trace its edge on the skin with a pen and write the date and time.
3. Signs of infection: increasing redness, warmth, swelling, pus, worsening pain, or bad smell.
4. Seek medical care quickly for: redness spreading past the pen line, red streaks moving up the limb, fever or chills, or the person seeming very ill or confused (possible sepsis).

## Caution
Sepsis can kill quickly. Fever, fast heartbeat, fast breathing, confusion, or extreme pain with an infection means get emergency help — do not wait.

## Field note
Pen-marking the red edge takes guesswork out of 'is it getting worse?' and gives a clinician useful information. Some redness right at a healing wound edge is normal; spreading redness is not.
