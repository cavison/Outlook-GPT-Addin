Clean cuts and scrapes well to prevent infection when you can't get to a clinic.

> This is not a substitute for training or professional care. Take an American Red Cross first aid/CPR class and a Stop the Bleed class before you need them. If 911 or any medical service is reachable, use it.

## You need
- Soap, clean (safe/disinfected) water, clean bowl or squeeze bottle
- Gloves, tweezers cleaned with rubbing alcohol
- Antibiotic ointment or petroleum jelly, sterile dressings, tape

## Steps
1. Wash your hands with soap and water; wear gloves if you have them.
2. Stop the bleeding first with direct pressure ([[M-02]]).
3. Rinse the wound under clean running or poured water for several minutes. Wash the skin around it with soap; keep soap out of the wound if you can.
4. Remove remaining dirt or splinters with clean tweezers. If debris is deep or won't come out, leave it and seek care.
5. Apply a thin layer of ointment and a clean dressing. Change it daily or whenever it is wet or dirty, and look for infection each time ([[M-12]]).

## Caution
Do not pour hydrogen peroxide or alcohol into a wound — it damages tissue and slows healing. Clean water and pressure from a squeeze bottle work better.

## Field note
A clean zip bag with a pinhole in a corner makes a good pressure-rinse syringe. Check tetanus status now: adults need a booster every 10 years, or after 5 years for a dirty wound — get it before an outage.
