Assemble a kit sized for three months without a pharmacy or ER, so small injuries stay small.

> This is not a substitute for training or professional care. Take an American Red Cross first aid/CPR class and a Stop the Bleed class before you need them. If 911 or any medical service is reachable, use it.

## You need
- Large plastic tote with lid, zip bags, marker
- Nitrile gloves (several boxes), gauze pads and rolls, adhesive bandages in many sizes
- Hemostatic or plain compressed gauze for packing, elastic wraps
- Commercial tourniquet (CAT or SOF-T style) from a reputable seller — at least one per adult
- Medical tape, wound-closure strips, saline or clean-water squeeze bottle, tweezers, trauma shears
- Antibiotic ointment, non-stick burn dressings, SAM-type splint, triangular bandages
- Digital thermometer, OTC pain/fever reducers (adult and child versions), antihistamine, anti-diarrheal, ORS packets
- Your household's prescriptions (see [[M-10]]) and a first-aid manual

## Steps
1. Start from a published list (Red Cross family kit) and multiply consumables for a 90-day, multi-person household.
2. Group supplies by job in labeled bags: BLEED, WOUND, MEDS, BREAK, TOOLS.
3. Put bleeding-control gear (gloves, packing gauze, pressure bandage, tourniquet) in one grab bag on top.
4. Add a 90-day supply of every family member's regular medicines, obtained legally ([[M-10]]).
5. Tape an inventory card inside the lid; check expiration dates and restock twice a year (e.g., at daylight-saving time changes).

## Caution
Cheap counterfeit tourniquets sold online can snap or fail to stop bleeding. Buy a recognized model from a trusted medical supplier, and practice with a trainer so you don't waste the real one.

## Field note
Gloves and gauze run out first in a long outage, not fancy gear. Buy gloves in boxes, and pack one small 'pocket kit' (gloves, gauze, tape) for each person's coat — Michigan winter injuries often happen outside shoveling or cutting wood.
