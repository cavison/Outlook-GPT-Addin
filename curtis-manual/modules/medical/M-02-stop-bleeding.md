Control life-threatening bleeding from an arm, leg, or body wound until help arrives — a person can bleed to death in minutes.

> This is not a substitute for training or professional care. Take an American Red Cross first aid/CPR class and a Stop the Bleed class before you need them. If 911 or any medical service is reachable, use it.

## You need
- Gloves (or a plastic bag over your hand)
- Gauze, clean cloth, or T-shirt
- Commercial tourniquet (CAT, SOF-T or similar); a pressure bandage

## Steps
1. Make sure the scene is safe. Call 911 or send someone to get help — any working phone or radio.
2. Find the bleeding source and press hard directly on it with gauze or cloth, using both hands and body weight. Do not lift to peek.
3. For a deep wound (especially thigh, armpit, groin, neck), pack it: stuff gauze or cloth tightly into the wound until full, then keep pressing hard for at least 3 minutes (longer for plain gauze).
4. If bleeding from an arm or leg won't stop with pressure, apply a tourniquet 2–3 inches above the wound (toward the body), never directly on a joint. If the wound is right at a joint, go above the joint.
5. Tighten the windlass until bleeding stops, lock it, and write the time on the tourniquet or skin. Do not loosen it; a second tourniquet above the first is used if one is not enough. Keep the person warm and get them to professional care.

## Caution
Tourniquets hurt — a lot — when applied correctly. A loose tourniquet is worse than none because it can increase bleeding. Do not put a tourniquet on the neck, chest, or abdomen; pack and press those instead.

## Field note
Improvised belt-and-stick tourniquets often fail; a commercial one is far more reliable. Blood soaking through? Don't remove the first dressing — add more on top and press harder. In cold Michigan weather, bleeding victims lose heat fast: get a blanket under them, not just over them.
