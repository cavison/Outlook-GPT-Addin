Handle toothaches, knocked-out teeth, and lost fillings until a dentist is available.

> This is not a substitute for training or professional care. Take an American Red Cross first aid/CPR class and a Stop the Bleed class before you need them. If 911 or any medical service is reachable, use it.

## You need
- Salt, warm water, cold pack
- Temporary dental filling kit (pharmacy), dental floss, gauze
- Small container of milk; OTC pain reliever

## Steps
1. Knocked-out adult tooth: pick it up by the crown (chewing part), not the root. Rinse briefly in milk or water if dirty; do not scrub.
2. Gently push it back into the socket and bite on gauze, or keep it in milk or the person's saliva. See a dentist as soon as possible — best within an hour. Do not re-implant baby teeth.
3. Lost filling or crown: clean the area and use a temporary dental filling material from a pharmacy kit.
4. Toothache: rinse with warm salt water, floss to remove trapped food, use a cold pack on the cheek and an OTC pain reliever per label.

## Caution
Swelling of the face, jaw, or neck, fever, or trouble swallowing or breathing can mean a spreading infection — this is an emergency.

## Field note
Don't put aspirin directly on the gum; it burns tissue. Daily brushing and flossing matter more in a long outage because dental care may be weeks away.
