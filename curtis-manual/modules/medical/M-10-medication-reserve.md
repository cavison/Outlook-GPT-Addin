Build up a 90-day supply of essential prescriptions the legal way, before an outage.

> This is not a substitute for training or professional care. Take an American Red Cross first aid/CPR class and a Stop the Bleed class before you need them. If 911 or any medical service is reachable, use it.

## You need
- Medication list for each person (name, dose, prescriber, pharmacy)
- Insurance card; prescriber contact

## Steps
1. Write a complete list of every medicine, dose, and prescriber for each person; keep copies in the go-bag and on paper.
2. Ask your doctor whether a 90-day prescription is appropriate for each maintenance medicine.
3. Use your insurer's 90-day or mail-order options and refill as early as your plan allows; ask the pharmacist about emergency refill rules. Michigan pharmacists may dispense limited emergency supplies in declared emergencies — ask what applies.
4. Store cool, dry, and dark; rotate oldest first so the reserve stays fresh.

## Caution
Never stockpile by taking less than prescribed, using someone else's medicine, or buying prescription drugs from unverified online sellers. Controlled substances have strict refill limits — talk with your prescriber.

## Field note
Expired is not always useless, but potency can drop — ask your pharmacist rather than guessing. Keep a printed list: pharmacy computers may be down, and a printed label speeds an emergency refill.
