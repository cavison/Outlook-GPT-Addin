First aid for burns from stoves, lanterns, hot water, and generators — common injuries in an outage.

> This is not a substitute for training or professional care. Take an American Red Cross first aid/CPR class and a Stop the Bleed class before you need them. If 911 or any medical service is reachable, use it.

## You need
- Cool, clean running water (or a container of it)
- Non-stick sterile dressings or clean plastic wrap
- OTC pain reliever

## Steps
1. Stop the burning: remove the person from the heat; remove rings, watches, and tight clothing near the burn before swelling (leave cloth that is stuck).
2. Cool the burn with cool — not ice-cold — running water for about 20 minutes.
3. Cover loosely with a sterile non-stick dressing or clean plastic wrap. Do not pop blisters.
4. Get medical help for burns that are large (bigger than the person's palm), deep, white/charred, or on the face, hands, feet, genitals, or major joints; for chemical/electrical burns; and for any burn in a young child or older adult.

## Caution
Never put ice, butter, oil, or toothpaste on a burn. Ice can cause more tissue damage. Cooling a large burn can chill the person — keep the rest of them warm.

## Field note
Kitchen plastic wrap laid (not wrapped tight) over a burn is a clean, non-stick cover many burn units recommend for transport. Most outage burns come from refilling hot lanterns or stoves — let them cool first.
