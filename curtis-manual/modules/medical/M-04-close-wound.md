Decide whether a cut should be taped closed or left open; closing the wrong wound traps infection.

> This is not a substitute for training or professional care. Take an American Red Cross first aid/CPR class and a Stop the Bleed class before you need them. If 911 or any medical service is reachable, use it.

## You need
- Wound-closure strips (Steri-Strip-type) or butterfly bandages
- Skin adhesive (optional), clean dressings, gloves

## Steps
1. Clean the wound thoroughly first ([[M-03]]).
2. Close only small, clean, fresh cuts with straight edges that come together easily.
3. Dry the skin around it, pull the edges together gently, and place strips across the cut about 1/8 inch apart; cover with a dressing.
4. Leave OPEN: animal or human bites, punctures, dirty or crushed wounds, wounds that are many hours old, and deep or gaping wounds, especially on the face, hands, feet, or over joints. Rinse, cover loosely, and get medical care.
5. Check every day for signs of infection ([[M-12]]); remove closures and seek help if they appear.

## Caution
Sealing a dirty wound or a bite traps bacteria and can cause a serious infection. When in doubt, leave it open and dressed. Do not attempt stitches without training.

## Field note
Most guidance suggests stitched-type closure works best when done within hours of injury; after that, open healing with daily cleaning is usually the safer home choice. Note the time of the injury so you know.
