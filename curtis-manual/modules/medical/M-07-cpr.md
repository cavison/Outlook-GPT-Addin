Keep blood moving to the brain when an adult collapses and is not breathing normally.

> This is not a substitute for training or professional care. Take an American Red Cross first aid/CPR class and a Stop the Bleed class before you need them. If 911 or any medical service is reachable, use it.

## You need
- Your hands; a firm, flat surface
- AED if anyone nearby has one; CPR face shield (optional)

## Steps
1. Check: tap and shout. Is the person responsive? Breathing normally (gasping does not count)?
2. Call 911 (put phone on speaker) and send someone for an AED.
3. Kneel beside them. Put the heel of one hand in the center of the chest, other hand on top, arms straight, shoulders over hands.
4. Push hard and fast: about 2 inches deep for adults, 100–120 pushes per minute. Let the chest fully rise after each push. If trained, give 2 rescue breaths after every 30 compressions; if not, keep doing hands-only CPR.
5. Do not stop until an AED is ready, help takes over, the person starts breathing, or you are too exhausted to continue. Switch rescuers about every 2 minutes.

## Caution
Stopping or pausing too much is the most common error. Children, infants, and drowning victims benefit from rescue breaths — take a Red Cross or AHA class to learn those differences.

## Field note
Push to the beat of a song around 100–120 beats per minute ("Stayin' Alive" is the classic). In a long outage, an unresponsive person in a closed house with a generator or fuel heater may be a CO victim — get everyone out to fresh air first.
