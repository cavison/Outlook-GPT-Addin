What to do right away after a bite or scratch from any mammal, and why rabies exposure needs medical care even when the grid is down.

> This is not a substitute for training or professional care. Rabies is almost always fatal once symptoms start; getting to care for an exposure is worth a long trip.

## You need
- Soap and plenty of clean water ([[W-04]], [[W-05]])
- Clean dressings ([[M-01]])
- Paper and pencil to record the animal, place and time
- Pet vaccination records

## Steps
1. **Wash the wound right away** with soap and running or poured water for **15 minutes** (WHO, CDC). This greatly lowers rabies risk.
2. Then clean and cover it as in [[M-03]]. Control bleeding first if it is heavy ([[M-02]]).
3. **Write down** the animal type, what it was doing, where it went, and the date and time.
4. **Contact** Ottawa County Department of Public Health (or any doctor or ER) as soon as possible. They decide whether you need rabies post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP).
5. **PEP is urgent.** It works when started before symptoms; don't wait to "see what happens."
6. If a pet dog, cat or ferret bit someone, **keep the animal confined** and observed as the health department directs; don't let it run off.
7. **Bat found in a room where someone was sleeping**, or with a child or impaired person: call public health even if no bite is seen. If safe, trap the bat under a container for testing; don't touch it bare-handed.
8. Watch the wound for infection ([[M-12]]).

## Michigan notes
MDHHS reports bats and skunks as the animals that most often test positive for rabies in Michigan. Raccoons, foxes and other wild mammals can also carry it. Never handle a wild animal that acts strangely — unusually tame, aggressive, staggering, or out in daylight when it normally isn't. Keep dogs, cats and ferrets up to date on rabies shots (required for dogs in Michigan); an outage brings more garbage and more wildlife near houses ([[S-08]]).

## Caution
Never try to catch, feed or "rescue" a strange-acting wild animal, and never let kids handle bats. Bat bites can be too small to see.

## Field note
In a long outage people trap and clean more small game ([[G-14]], [[G-15]]). Wear gloves when handling any carcass, and don't take an animal that looked sick or acted oddly before it was caught.
