Care for sick family members at home and recognize red flags that need professional care.

> This is not a substitute for training or professional care. Take an American Red Cross first aid/CPR class and a Stop the Bleed class before you need them. If 911 or any medical service is reachable, use it.

## You need
- Digital thermometer, log notebook
- Fever reducers (acetaminophen, ibuprofen) for adults and children
- ORS ([[M-08]]), soap, masks, separate bedding/towels

## Steps
1. Take temperature and write it down with the time and any medicine given.
2. Give fluids and rest. Dose fever reducers exactly by label (by weight for children). Never give aspirin to children or teens.
3. Keep the sick person in a separate room if possible; everyone washes hands often; clean shared surfaces.
4. Get help immediately for: a baby under 3 months with a fever of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher; trouble breathing; stiff neck; confusion or hard to wake; seizure; no urine for 8+ hours; signs of severe dehydration; a rash that doesn't fade when pressed; or chest pain.

## Caution
Double-dosing happens when two caregivers give the same medicine. One written log, by the bed, prevents it.

## Field note
In an outage, ask your block's wellness network ([[K-04]]) who has a working phone or radio to reach 911 — decide this before anyone is sick.
