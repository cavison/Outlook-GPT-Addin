Keep generators, chainsaws and snowblowers starting by checking the few things that stop most small engines: fuel, spark and air.

## You need
- Owner's manual
- Spark plug socket, feeler gauge, spare plug
- Fresh fuel with stabilizer, clean funnel
- Correct oil, rag, air filter

## Steps
1. Check oil level on a level surface; top up with the type the manual names.
2. Use fresh, stabilized fuel; old fuel is the most common cause of no-start.
3. Pull the spark plug, check for wet, black or worn tips; clean, gap per manual, or replace.
4. Remove and clean or replace the air filter.
5. Open the fuel valve and choke (cold engine), then pull or start.
6. Before storing, run the carburetor dry or drain it as the manual says.

## Caution
Let the engine cool before refueling or touching the muffler. Spilled gas on a hot engine starts fires.

## Field note
Run your generator under load for about 15–20 minutes once a month before the outage, and log it. Engines that sit for a season with untreated fuel often need a carburetor cleaning, which is hard to do mid-outage.
