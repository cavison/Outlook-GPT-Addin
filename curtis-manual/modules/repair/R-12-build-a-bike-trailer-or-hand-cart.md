Turn a hand truck, wheelbarrow, garden cart or old kids' bike trailer into a hauler for water, firewood and supplies when fuel is scarce.

## You need
- Base: garden cart, wheelbarrow, hand truck, or used kids' bike trailer
- Straight 2x4 or metal tube for a tongue, carriage bolts, washers, nuts
- Store-bought bike hitch (fits rear axle or seat post) or a hitch from an old trailer
- Backup safety strap or chain, zip ties, ratchet straps
- Reflectors, red rear light, drill, wrenches, tape measure

## Steps
1. **Pick a base:** a kids' bike trailer is easiest; a two-wheel garden cart is next best. Inflate tires and grease wheels.
2. **Tongue:** bolt a straight 2x4 or tube to the frame so the hitch point is level with the bike's hitch and the cart rides level.
3. **Hitch:** use a real bike hitch on the rear axle or chainstay; add a backup strap so the trailer can't come loose if the hitch fails.
4. **Load limit:** never exceed the lowest rating among the trailer, hitch and tires. If there's no rating, start light and test.
5. **Load right:** heavy items (water jugs) low, centered, slightly ahead of the axle; strap everything.
6. **Ride smart:** slow on hills and turns, walk steep grades, and use lights and reflectors in low light.

## Caution
A heavy trailer pushes the bike downhill and can jackknife or tip in turns — loaded braking distances are much longer. Don't carry children in a homemade trailer, and don't exceed the manufacturer's rated load.

## Field note
A gallon of water weighs about 8.3 lb, so five 5-gallon jugs is over 200 lb — more than most kids' trailers are rated for. On West Michigan's snowy roads, a plastic sled or the cart without the bike (pulled on foot) is often safer than riding.
