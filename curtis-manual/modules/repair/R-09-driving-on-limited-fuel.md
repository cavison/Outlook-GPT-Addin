Make each gallon go further when stations can't pump without power and every trip has to count.

## You need
- Paper map
- Tire pressure gauge
- Trip log notebook
- Winter car kit (blanket, shovel, traction aid)

## Steps
1. Keep the tank at least half full at all times, before and during an outage.
2. Plan and combine trips; ask neighbors to share rides and errands.
3. Set tires to the pressure on the driver's door sticker.
4. Remove roof racks and heavy cargo you don't need.
5. Drive smoothly; ease off the gas early and avoid hard acceleration.
6. Don't idle to warm up longer than needed; turn the engine off when parked.
7. Log miles and fuel so you know your real range.

## Caution
Never let a car idle with the tailpipe blocked by snow, and never idle in a garage. Carbon monoxide can kill people sitting inside the car.

## Field note
Gas stations need electricity to pump. Some stations have backup power; local news and county emergency management often list which are open after a big storm, so check before driving around looking.
