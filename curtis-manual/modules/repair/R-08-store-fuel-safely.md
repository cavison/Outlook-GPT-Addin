Store gasoline, diesel, kerosene and propane so they're ready to use without starting a fire or poisoning anyone.

## You need
- Approved fuel containers (UL or ASTM marked; red = gasoline, yellow = diesel, blue = kerosene)
- Fuel stabilizer
- Marker and tape for date labels
- Fire extinguisher (ABC)
- Detached shed or well-ventilated area away from the house

## Steps
1. Check your local fire code and homeowner's insurance for how much fuel you may store and where.
2. Use only approved containers; never milk jugs or glass.
3. Fill containers on the ground, not in a truck bed or car trunk, to prevent static sparks.
4. Leave room for expansion; don't fill to the brim.
5. Add stabilizer as the label directs and write the date on the can.
6. Store away from the living space, heaters, pilot lights, sun and kids, ideally in a detached shed.
7. Rotate: use the oldest fuel in your car or mower and refill on schedule.
8. Keep propane cylinders outdoors, upright, valves closed; never store them indoors or in a basement.

## Caution
Gasoline vapors are heavier than air and can travel to a water heater or furnace pilot light. Never store gas in the house or basement, and never refuel a hot or running engine.

## Field note
Static is a real ignition source: always set the can on the ground before filling, and keep the nozzle touching the can opening. Plastic truck-bed liners and carpeted trunks insulate the can and let a static charge build up.
