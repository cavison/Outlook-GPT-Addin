Give children a steady daily learning routine during a long outage, which helps their mood as much as their schooling.

## You need
- Books, workbooks, library books
- Paper, pencils, crayons, a clipboard
- Board and card games, a deck of cards
- Printed grade-level worksheets (download before outages)

## Steps
1. Set a short school time each morning in daylight, at the same time each day.
2. Read aloud together every day; let older kids read to younger ones.
3. Use real outage jobs as lessons: measuring water, tracking fuel, weather logs, radio check-in times.
4. Do math with cards and games; write daily journals or letters to relatives.
5. Add hands-on skills: sewing ([[R-04]]), bike repair ([[R-03]]), gardening, cooking.
6. Keep a simple record of what they did to share with the school later.

## Caution
Don't let study time come at the cost of safety: kids should never handle fuel, generators, or candles as "projects", and learning by flashlight beats candles.

## Field note
A daily journal turns stress into something kids can control, and becomes a real record of the outage. Many children cope better with routine and a job to do; give each one a daily task that matters.
