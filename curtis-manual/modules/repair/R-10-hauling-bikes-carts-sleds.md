Move water, firewood and supplies without fuel using bikes, wagons, garden carts and snow sleds.

## You need
- Bike with rack or child/cargo trailer
- Garden cart or wagon
- Plastic snow sled (ice-fishing sled is ideal)
- Rope, ratchet straps, bungee cords
- Reflective vest and lights

## Steps
1. Pick the right carrier: sled on snow, cart or wagon on pavement, bike trailer for longer trips.
2. Load heavy items low and centered, over or just ahead of the wheels.
3. Strap the load down so it can't shift.
4. For a sled, tie a waist or shoulder harness so your legs pull, not your arms.
5. Walk or ride slowly on hills and ice; on downhills, walk behind the sled or cart to control it.
6. Wear reflective gear and lights; drivers aren't expecting you without streetlights.

## Caution
A gallon of water weighs about 8.3 pounds, so loads get heavy fast. Overloaded carts tip and sleds run away on hills; make two trips instead of one injury.

## Field note
Plastic ice-fishing sleds sold in Michigan sporting goods stores are tough, deep, and slide well on packed snow; they carry water jugs or firewood much better than kids' sleds and work in summer on grass, too.
