Use clear, short, legal radio procedure so your call is understood the first time and you don't waste battery or block emergency traffic.

## You need
- Programmed radio ([[C-03]]) and frequency card ([[C-04]])
- Pencil and log sheet
- Your callsign (licensed operators)

## Steps
1. Listen first for at least 30 seconds; never talk over someone. Emergency traffic always has priority.
2. Plan what you'll say before pressing the button. Keep it under about 20 seconds.
3. Press, wait one second, then speak across (not into) the mic from a few inches away.
4. Format: their callsign, "this is," your callsign. Example: "KD8ABC, this is KE8XYZ."
5. Say "over" when you want a reply. Release the button and listen.
6. Give your callsign at least every 10 minutes and at the end of the contact (FCC §97.119).
7. For a real emergency say "Break, break" or "Emergency" when the channel is clear, then state location and problem.
8. Write time, station and message on your log sheet.

## Check-in schedule
Radios burn little power listening but a lot transmitting, and "scan" eats battery. Agree on fixed windows: for example, the top of every hour for 5 minutes at 8 am, noon, 6 pm and 9 pm. Radios off or on low volume in between. If someone misses two windows in a row, follow the family plan in [[C-08]].

## Caution
Without a license you may transmit on ham frequencies only when there is an immediate threat to life or property and normal communications (phone, 911) are not available (FCC §97.403). "Just checking in" is not an emergency.

## Field note
Use phonetics for callsigns and street names (K = Kilo, D = Delta). For numbers, say each digit ("one-four-six point five-two"). Local nets run by ARES/RACES often welcome listeners; just listening to one net teaches more procedure than any book.
