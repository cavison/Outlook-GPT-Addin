Earn an FCC Technician license so you can legally talk on your Baofeng, use local repeaters and join emergency nets.

## You need
- Free question pool and study tools (ARRL, HamStudy.org, or a printed study guide)
- Photo ID
- FCC Registration Number (FRN) from the FCC CORES website
- Exam fee for the volunteer team, and the FCC application fee (check current amounts)

## Steps
1. Get an FRN on the FCC CORES website before the exam; you'll write it on your application.
2. Study the current Technician question pool. The exam is 35 multiple-choice questions taken from that public pool; no Morse code is required.
3. Take practice tests until you pass them consistently.
4. Find a Volunteer Examiner (VE) session near Ottawa County or online with the ARRL exam search or HamStudy.org.
5. Take the exam; the VE team grades it right away.
6. Pay the FCC application fee when the FCC emails you, then watch the FCC ULS database for your callsign.
7. Join a local club or Ottawa County ARES/RACES and check in to a weekly net.

## Caution
Don't transmit until your callsign actually appears in the FCC ULS database. Passing the exam is not the same as being licensed.

## Field note
One license usually covers a lot: a Technician license lets you use 2 m and 70 cm (most Baofeng channels). Many families study together; each person needs their own license, but kids can pass too, and there is no minimum age.
