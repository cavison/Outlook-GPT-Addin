Get much more range from a handheld radio by improving the antenna and its height, not by turning up power.

## You need
- Aftermarket whip for your radio's connector (Nagoya-style, check SMA-male vs SMA-female)
- Roll-up J-pole (ladder-line type) with coax and adapter
- String or rope, and a tree branch or high window
- AA battery pack (or spare battery) for the radio

## Steps
1. Check your radio's antenna connector type before buying; Baofeng UV-5R class radios usually need SMA-female antennas.
2. Replace the stock rubber antenna with a longer, better whip; this alone often helps.
3. Hold the radio vertical and get up high: second-floor window, porch roof edge, or a hill.
4. For home use, hang a roll-up J-pole from a tree or window frame as high as you can, with the coax going to the radio.
5. Start transmissions on low power; switch to high only if you aren't heard.
6. Carry an AA battery pack so you can run on store-bought or rechargeable AAs when the battery dies.

## Caution
Never hang or throw an antenna or rope anywhere near power lines, including downed lines. Lines can re-energize without warning when crews restore power or someone backfeeds a generator.

## Field note
At 2 m and 70 cm, height matters more than watts: signals travel mostly line-of-sight. Moving a J-pole from ground level to 20–30 feet up a tree can make a bigger difference than high power, and low power stretches your battery.
