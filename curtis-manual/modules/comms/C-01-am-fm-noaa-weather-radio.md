Use a battery or hand-crank radio to hear official news and weather alerts when TV, internet and cell service are down.

## You need
- Battery AM/FM radio (any car radio also works)
- Spare batteries that fit it
- NOAA Weather Radio with SAME alerting (Public Alert logo is a good sign)
- Hand-crank/solar radio as backup

## Steps
1. Write the local AM and FM news stations on a card and tape it to the radio before an outage.
2. Program the NOAA Weather Radio to your local station (Grand Rapids area: KIG63, 162.475 MHz; confirm on the NWS station list).
3. Enter the SAME code for your county (Ottawa County, MI: 026139) so it alarms only for your area.
4. Set alert mode on, put it where you sleep, and test it on the weekly test (NWS schedules these; often Wednesday midday).
5. At night, tune AM for distant stations; AM signals travel much farther after dark.
6. Listen on a schedule (for example, on the hour) to save batteries, then turn it off.

## Caution
A weather radio left on "alert" with dead batteries gives no warning. Use fresh batteries plus the wall adapter while you still have power, and check them monthly.

## Field note
If KIG63 is weak in your spot, NOAA transmitters in neighboring areas often overlap; the NWS station listing for Michigan shows each transmitter and the counties it covers. Ottawa County's SAME code works on any of them that broadcast for Ottawa.
