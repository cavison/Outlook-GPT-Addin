A simple daily schedule for listening to weather, local news, emergency nets and distant shortwave news so your household stays informed without wasting batteries. No license is needed to listen.

## You need
- NOAA weather radio and AM/FM radio ([[C-01]])
- Baofeng or scanner programmed with weather and local repeaters ([[C-03]], [[C-04]]) — listen only unless licensed
- Optional: shortwave receiver (battery or crank)
- Pencil, the log template below, and fresh batteries ([[P-03]])

## Steps
1. **Before an outage**, fill in the station table below. Look up current repeaters and net times on RepeaterBook (repeaterbook.com or its offline app) and ask Ottawa County ARES or a local radio club; write them in pencil.
2. Pick **fixed check times** (example: morning, midday, evening) and stick to them so the radio isn't on all day.
3. **Every check:** NOAA weather first (watches, warnings, cold or heat), then local AM/FM news.
4. **When nets run:** listen to the ARES/SKYWARN net or local repeater for road, shelter and damage reports.
5. **After dark:** AM and shortwave travel farther at night; scan for distant news if local stations are off the air.
6. **Log it**: time, station, what you heard, and whether it is confirmed or rumor ([[C-09]]).
7. Share the log at the street meeting or message board ([[K-01]], [[C-10]]).

## Caution
Listening is legal for anyone; transmitting on ham frequencies is not without a license ([[C-07]]), except in a true emergency (FCC §97.403). A Baofeng cannot hear Michigan's digital public-safety radio (MPSCS) — see [[C-11]].

## Field note
Nets often have a fixed weekly practice time even in good weather. Listening to a few of them before an outage teaches you the voices, the procedure and which repeater actually reaches your house, which is worth more than any printed frequency list.
