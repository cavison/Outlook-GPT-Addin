Decide whether to shelter at home or leave for a relative's home, shelter, or warming center.

## You need
- Radio or phone for official notices
- Go-bag with medicines, IDs, chargers, warm clothes
- Full or half-full gas tank
- Your paper plan with a destination ([[A04]])

## Steps
1. If officials order an evacuation, go.
2. If someone depends on power for oxygen, dialysis, or other medical needs, or needs care you can't give, go early — before roads or the person get worse.
3. If you cannot keep one room safely warm (see [[Q-05]], [[H-11]]), or you have no safe water or way to deal with toilet waste, go to a relative, shelter, or warming center. Dial 211 to find one.
4. Otherwise, stay. Recheck these questions every morning and write down your answer.

## Caution
Most deadly mistakes come from staying and heating unsafely — grills, generators, or ovens indoors. Leaving is safer than heating with something that makes carbon monoxide.

## Field note
Decide your "go" point and destination in advance and write it on your plan (for example: "If the warm room drops below a temperature we agree on and we can't fix it, we go to Grandma's"). A decision made calmly beforehand is easier to follow at 3 a.m. Before leaving in winter, protect your plumbing ([[H-08]]) so you don't come home to burst pipes.
