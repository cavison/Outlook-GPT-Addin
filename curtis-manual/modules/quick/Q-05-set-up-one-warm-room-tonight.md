Keep everyone warm through a cold night without power by heating people, not the whole house.

## You need
- Blankets, comforters, sleeping bags
- Towels or rolled rugs for door gaps
- Tape or thumbtacks
- Hats, socks, layers for everyone

## Steps
1. Pick one small room, ideally on the south side, away from outside walls if possible, with few windows. Close its door and stuff a towel along the bottom.
2. Cover windows with blankets or comforters after dark; open them on the south side during sunny days.
3. Put bedding on the floor away from windows, or build a blanket tent over a bed or table ([[H-03]]).
4. Bring everyone — and pets — into the room. Body heat adds up in a small space.
5. Dress in dry layers with hats and socks. Eat a snack and drink water before bed.

## Caution
Never heat a room with a gas oven, charcoal or gas grill, camp stove, or generator. These make carbon monoxide that can kill a sleeping family. Use only heaters rated for indoor use, with a working CO alarm ([[H-04]]).

## Field note
Close off upstairs rooms and the door to the basement stairway; cold air sinks and warm air rises, so a ground-floor room with the stairway closed off stays noticeably warmer. In older west Michigan homes, the bathroom is often the easiest room to seal — but avoid rooms with plumbing on outside walls if you plan to drain pipes ([[H-08]]).
