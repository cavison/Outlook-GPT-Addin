Find out what is happening and how long it may last, without draining your phone.

## You need
- Battery or hand-crank AM/FM radio, ideally with NOAA Weather Radio
- Spare batteries
- Car radio (as a backup)
- Pen and paper

## Steps
1. Turn on a battery or crank radio. Scan AM news stations from Grand Rapids and NOAA Weather Radio for warnings.
2. Write down what you hear: time, source, restoration estimates, warming centers, boil-water notices.
3. Use your phone for short texts, not calls or video. Texts get through when networks are busy.
4. Check utility outage maps once or twice a day, not constantly. Save battery ([[C-02]]).
5. Believe official sources (county emergency management, utility, health department) over rumors ([[C-09]]).

## Caution
Do not run a car in a garage to listen to the radio or charge a phone — even with the door open. Run it outside with the tailpipe clear of snow.

## Field note
AM signals travel farther at night than in the day, so after dark you may pick up Chicago, Detroit, or other distant stations — useful if local stations are off the air. Michigan 2-1-1 is a free number that lists shelters and warming centers; dial 211.
