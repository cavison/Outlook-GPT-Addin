Find neighbors who are in danger — elderly, disabled, sick, or alone — and get them help early.

## You need
- Flashlight
- Notepad and pen
- Your block map if you made one ([[K-02]])

## Steps
1. Visit in daylight in pairs. Knock and call out your name.
2. Ask: Are you warm enough? Do you have food, water, and your medicines?
3. Ask about anything that needs power: oxygen, CPAP, home dialysis, power wheelchair, medicine that must stay cold.
4. Look for danger: generator or grill indoors, headache or confusion (CO), shivering or slurred speech (cold).
5. Write down what each household needs and agree on a time to check back.

## Caution
If someone is confused, very cold, or can't be woken, call 911. If you smell gas or suspect carbon monoxide, get them out and call from outside — don't stay in to investigate.

## Field note
People who use oxygen concentrators often have only a few hours on backup tanks. Ask now, while phones still work, if they have a plan; many oxygen suppliers will deliver tanks if called early, and Consumers Energy has a medical-certification program for customers on life-support equipment.
