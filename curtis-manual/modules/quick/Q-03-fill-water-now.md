If the taps still run, fill every clean container now — city water pressure can drop once pumps and towers run dry.

## You need
- Clean food-grade jugs, pots, pitchers
- Bathtub (scrubbed and rinsed) or a tub liner bag
- Buckets for flushing water
- Masking tape and marker

## Steps
1. Scrub and rinse the bathtub, plug it, and fill it. Use tub water for flushing and washing unless you used a food-safe tub liner.
2. Fill clean food-grade jugs and pots with drinking water. Cap or cover them.
3. Fill buckets for toilet flushing — about one bucket per flush.
4. Label drinking water with the date ([[W-13]]).
5. Listen for a boil-water advisory. If one is issued, treat stored water before drinking ([[W-04]], [[W-05]]).

## Caution
An open tub of water can drown a toddler in seconds. Keep the bathroom door shut and never leave small children alone near it.

## Field note
Your water heater already holds about 30–80 gallons of clean water ([[W-01]]). If you're short on containers, close the water heater's cold inlet valve after the tub fills — that keeps it from back-draining if the system loses pressure.
