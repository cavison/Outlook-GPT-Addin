Quick signs that tell you whether to wait it out or start your multi-day plan.

## You need
- Radio
- Phone (for the utility outage map)
- Notepad

## Steps
1. Look outside. If only your house is dark, check your main breaker and call the utility. If the whole street is dark, it's a wider outage.
2. Note the cause if you know it. Ice storms, wind storms with many downed trees, and tornadoes damage lines across wide areas and often take days to repair.
3. Check the utility's outage map or radio for an estimated restoration time. Hours means wait; "assessing" or multi-day means start the 72-hour plan ([[A05]]).
4. If you still have no estimate after 24 hours, or the estimate keeps slipping, plan for a long outage: water, warm room, food rotation, sanitation.

## Caution
Do not wait to act just because you hope power will come back soon. Filling water and protecting pipes costs little if power returns, but is too late once taps are dry or pipes freeze.

## Field note
Utilities restore in a set order: power plants and transmission lines first, then substations and lines serving hospitals and many customers, then neighborhood lines, and last of all single homes. If your street has one damaged service drop, you may wait after the rest of the block is back — report it again once neighbors have power.
