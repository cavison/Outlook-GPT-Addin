What to do when a CO alarm sounds or people get headaches, dizziness, or nausea while a fuel-burning device is running.

## You need
- Working battery CO alarm on every level and near sleeping areas
- Phone

## Steps
1. Get everyone out to fresh air right now, including pets. Do not stop to open windows or find the source.
2. Call 911 from outside. Say "carbon monoxide" and how many people have symptoms.
3. Do not go back in until firefighters say the air is safe, even if the alarm stops.
4. Anyone who is confused, unconscious, pregnant, or very young or old needs medical care now. Tell responders about the symptoms.
5. Find and fix the cause before using it again: generator too close, blocked furnace vent, grill or heater indoors.

## Caution
CO has no smell or color. People who are asleep or drunk can die before they feel anything. Symptoms — headache, dizziness, weakness, upset stomach, vomiting, chest pain, confusion — feel like the flu; if several people in the house feel sick at once, think CO.

## Field note
CO alarms wear out. Most have a "replace by" date on the back and many chirp at end-of-life even with a fresh battery. Check the date now while you can still buy one. After lake-effect snow, clear drifts away from furnace and water-heater vents on the outside wall — a buried vent can push CO into the house.
