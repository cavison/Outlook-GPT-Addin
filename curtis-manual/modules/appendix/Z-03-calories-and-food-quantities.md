How much food does a family of four actually need for 90 days? This page works the numbers so you can shop with a target instead of a guess. Use [[F-03]] to fit it to your household.

## Assumptions (change these to match your family)

| Person | Daily calories | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Adult man, moderately active | 2,400 | Middle of the 2,000–3,000 range (Dietary Guidelines) |
| Adult woman, moderately active | 2,000 | Middle of the 1,600–2,400 range |
| Teen (about 13) | 2,000 | Growing; varies a lot by sex and activity |
| Child (about 8) | 1,600 | Range for ages 2–8 is about 1,000–2,000 |
| **Household total** | **8,000 per day** | |

- Outage lasts **90 days**, no grocery resupply at all (worst case).
- Michigan winter: hauling water and wood and living in a cold house raises needs. Add 10–20% if you can.
- Calorie values below are rounded from USDA FoodData Central for dry/uncooked foods. Brands differ; read your labels.

**Total needed:** 8,000 × 90 = **720,000 calories**. With a 10% cold-weather buffer: about **800,000 calories**.

| Food (dry, as stored) | Calories per lb | Notes |

| White rice | ~1,650 | Stores for years if sealed and dry |

| Rolled oats | ~1,700 | Quick to cook; less fuel |
| Pasta | ~1,650 | Needs more water to cook |
| All-purpose flour | ~1,650 | For flatbread ([[F-10]]) |
| White sugar / honey | ~1,750 / ~1,380 | |
| Peanut butter | ~2,650 | Dense calories and protein |
| Vegetable oil | ~4,000 | Most calories per pound; rotate (goes rancid) |
| Nonfat dry milk | ~1,630 | |
| Canned meat/fish | varies — read label | Protein, ready to eat |
| Canned vegetables/fruit | low (often 100–400 per lb) | For vitamins and morale, not calories |

## A sample 90-day plan for 4 (about 800,000 calories)

| Food | Amount | Calories (approx.) |
|---|---|---|
| White rice | 150 lb | 247,500 |
| Dry beans | 75 lb | 116,250 |
| Rolled oats | 50 lb | 85,000 |
| Flour and pasta | 50 lb | 82,500 |
| Vegetable oil | 3 gallons (~23 lb) | 92,000 |
| Peanut butter | 25 lb | 66,250 |
| Sugar / honey | 25 lb | 42,000 |
| Nonfat dry milk | 15 lb | 24,450 |
| Canned meat/fish | about 90 cans | (read labels; often 25,000–40,000 total) |
| Canned vegetables/fruit | about 180 cans | (about 2 cans/day; vitamins, variety) |
| Salt, spices, bouillon, vitamins, coffee/tea | as needed | morale and nutrition |
| **Total of staples (not counting cans)** | | **about 756,000** |

Add the cans and you reach roughly 800,000. This is a *bulk staples* plan — it keeps you alive. Mix in foods your family already eats, and don't buy 150 lb of rice if nobody will eat it ([[F-16]]).

## Things the numbers don't show

- **Cooking fuel and water**: rice and beans need both. Count fuel in [[P-05]] and water in [[W-14]].
- **Vitamins**: a 90-day bulk diet can run short of vitamin C and others. A daily multivitamin and sprouts ([[F-12]]) help.
- **Special diets**: infants ([[K-10]]), diabetics, allergies, and older adults ([[K-08]]) need their own plan.
- **Pets**: add their food separately ([[K-11]]).
- **Storage**: keep dry, cool, sealed against pests ([[F-15]]); rotate first in, first out ([[F-04]]).
