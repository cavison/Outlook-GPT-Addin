Plain-language meanings of terms used in this manual.

**Amp-hour (Ah)** — How much charge a battery holds. Multiply by voltage to get watt-hours.

**Backfeeding** — Plugging a generator into a house outlet to power the wiring. Illegal and deadly; can electrocute line workers. See [[P-06]].

**Barter** — Trading goods or services without money. See [[R-06]].

**Boil water advisory** — Official notice that tap water may be unsafe; boil it 1 minute before drinking. See [[V-04]].

**Brownout** — Low voltage from the grid; lights dim. Can damage motors.

**Call sign** — Unique ID issued by the FCC to a licensed ham radio operator. See [[C-07]].

**Carbon monoxide (CO)** — Invisible, odorless poison gas from burning fuel. Leading killer after outages. See [[Q-02]].

**Cat-hole** — Small hole dug to bury human waste outdoors. See [[S-03]].

**CHIRP** — Free software to program many radios from a computer. See [[C-03]].

**Cross-contamination** — Spreading germs from raw food or waste to clean food, hands, or surfaces.

**Dehydration** — Losing more water than you take in. Dark urine, dizziness, dry mouth.

**FEMA** — Federal Emergency Management Agency; runs disaster aid after a federal declaration. See [[V-05]].

**FIFO** — First in, first out: use oldest stock first. See [[F-04]].

**Frostbite** — Frozen skin and tissue, usually fingers, toes, ears, nose. See [[H-11]].

**FRS / GMRS** — Family Radio Service (no license) and General Mobile Radio Service (FCC license, no test). See [[C-08]].

**Fuel stabilizer** — Additive that slows gasoline from going stale in storage. See [[R-08]].

**Grid-down** — Long, widespread loss of utility power.

**Ham radio (amateur radio)** — Licensed two-way radio service with long range. See [[C-07]].

**Haybox** — Insulated box that finishes cooking food with stored heat, saving fuel. See [[F-06]].

**Heat exhaustion / heat stroke** — Overheating illness; heat stroke is a 911 emergency. See [[H-12]].

**Hypothermia** — Body core temperature falls too low. See [[H-11]].

**Inverter** — Device that turns battery DC power into household AC power.

**Lake-effect snow** — Heavy snow bands formed when cold air crosses Lake Michigan; common in Ottawa County.

**Latrine** — Dug pit toilet. See [[S-03]].

**Low-acid food** — Vegetables, meat, beans; must be pressure-canned to be safe. See [[G-19]].

**NOAA Weather Radio** — Government radio network broadcasting weather alerts 24/7. See [[C-01]].

**ORS** — Oral rehydration solution: water, sugar, and salt to treat diarrhea dehydration. See [[M-08]].

**Perishable** — Food that spoils without refrigeration (meat, dairy, eggs, leftovers).

**Pilot light** — Small flame that lights a gas appliance burner.

**Power station** — Portable battery pack with built-in inverter and outlets. See [[P-07]].

**Pressure canning** — Canning at higher temperature under pressure; required for low-acid foods. See [[G-19]].

**Repeater** — Hilltop/tower radio station that rebroadcasts signals to extend range. See [[C-06]].

**Rocket stove** — Efficient small stove burning sticks with a strong draft. See [[F-05]].

**Shelter-in-place** — Staying in your home instead of evacuating. See [[A11]].

**Shock chlorination** — Disinfecting a well by adding a strong chlorine dose. See [[V-04]].

**SODIS** — Solar water disinfection in clear bottles. See [[W-09]].

**Sodium hypochlorite** — Active chemical in bleach.

**Sump pump** — Pump that keeps basements from flooding; stops when the power goes out.

**Tourniquet** — Tight band to stop severe limb bleeding. See [[M-02]].

**Transfer switch** — Device that safely connects a generator to house circuits and isolates the grid. See [[P-06]].

**Triage** — Sorting who needs care first by how serious it is.

**Watt / watt-hour (Wh)** — Watt = power used right now; watt-hour = energy over time. See [[P-09]].

**Water-bath canning** — Canning high-acid foods in boiling water. See [[G-18]].
