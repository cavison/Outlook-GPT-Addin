Bring your house back onto grid power in a safe order so you don't trip breakers, damage electronics, or shock a line worker.

## You need
- Flashlight or headlamp
- Your breaker panel map (or a pencil to make one)
- Dry shoes; dry hands
- Surge protectors for computers and TVs

## Steps
1. If you used a generator, shut it off and disconnect it first. Set the transfer switch back to UTILITY (or unplug all cords from the generator).
2. Before power returns, unplug sensitive electronics and turn off big loads: furnace, well pump, water heater, AC, electric range, dryer.
3. Leave one lamp switched on so you know when power is back.
4. When the lamp comes on, wait 10–15 minutes. Grids often flicker or drop again while crews switch circuits.
5. Turn big loads back on one at a time, a few minutes apart: furnace or AC first, then fridge/freezer, then well pump, then water heater.
6. Check the water heater has water in it before turning it on (open a hot tap until water flows). An empty electric heater burns out its elements.
7. Plug electronics back in through surge protectors last. Reset clocks, thermostats, and the sump pump if it has a reset.

## Caution
Never touch a breaker panel or wiring that got wet in flooding — call an electrician and Consumers Energy first. A generator still connected to house wiring when the grid returns can start a fire or kill a line worker.

## Field note
If only half your house works or lights are oddly dim/bright, you may have lost one leg of the 240-volt service or a damaged neutral. Turn off the main breaker and report it to Consumers Energy — this can destroy appliances fast.
