Document losses and file claims with your insurer, the utility, and (if a federal disaster is declared) FEMA, so you recover money you are owed.

## You need
- Phone or camera; notebook
- Copies of insurance policies (kept in your paper plan / go-bag)
- Receipts for repairs, generator fuel, hotel, spoiled food
- Folder or large envelope to keep everything

## Steps
1. Photograph and video all damage before cleaning up. Say the date out loud on video. Keep damaged items until the adjuster sees them, if safe.
2. Make an itemized list: what, when bought, approximate cost. Include spoiled food (your policy may or may not cover it).
3. Make only temporary repairs needed to prevent more damage (tarp a roof, shut off water). Keep every receipt.
4. Call your insurance company soon; write down the claim number, adjuster name, and every call date.
5. Ask Consumers Energy and the Michigan Public Service Commission whether you qualify for an outage credit.
6. If the President declares a major disaster for Ottawa County, apply for FEMA assistance at DisasterAssistance.gov or a Disaster Recovery Center. Apply even if insured — FEMA needs your insurance decision.
7. Keep copies of everything in one folder. Never pay a contractor in full up front.

## Caution
Disaster scams spike after outages: fake FEMA inspectors, door-to-door roofers, "tree crews" wanting cash. FEMA never charges to apply. Check contractor licenses with the State of Michigan (LARA) before hiring.

## Field note
Standard homeowners policies usually exclude flood (that's separate NFIP insurance) and often limit sump-pump backup unless you added a rider. Read your policy *before* the next storm — Ottawa County basements flood when sump pumps lose power.
