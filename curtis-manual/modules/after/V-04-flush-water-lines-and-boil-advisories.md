Get safe tap water back after an outage: follow any boil advisory, flush stale water from your pipes, and test a private well.

## You need
- Local news radio or city/township notices
- Pot and stove for boiling (until the advisory lifts)
- New fridge/faucet filter cartridges
- Well owners: unscented bleach and a bacteria test kit from the health department

## Steps
1. Check whether your city or township water system has a boil water advisory. If yes, boil all drinking and cooking water at a rolling boil for 1 minute (or use bottled water) until it is lifted.
2. When the advisory is lifted, run cold water from every tap for several minutes, starting with the faucet closest to where water enters the house, until it runs clear and cold.
3. Run hot taps until the water heater has refilled. Drain a few gallons from the water heater valve to clear sediment if water looks rusty.
4. Dump ice from the icemaker (first 2–3 batches) and replace fridge and faucet filters.
5. Private well: if the wellhead was flooded or the water looks, smells, or tastes different, do not drink it. Shock-chlorinate the well and test for bacteria (Ottawa County Department of Public Health can advise and supply test kits).
6. Keep boiling or disinfecting well water until a lab test comes back clean.

## Caution
Boiling does not remove chemicals or fuel. If floodwater, fuel, or chemicals may have entered your water, use bottled water and ask the health department.

## Field note
After pipes freeze and thaw, check for slow leaks: read your water meter, use no water for an hour, then read it again. If the number moved, you have a leak somewhere.
