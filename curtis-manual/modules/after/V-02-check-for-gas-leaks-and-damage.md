Walk the house and yard after a long outage or storm to find gas leaks, CO risks, and damage before you settle back in.

## You need
- Flashlight (battery, never a match or candle)
- Phone (to call from outside) and camera for photos
- Work gloves, sturdy shoes
- Notepad for damage list

## Steps
1. Sniff at the door before going in. If you smell rotten eggs, hear hissing, or see dead plants near the gas line: do not go in.
2. If you smell gas inside: do NOT flip switches, use phones, or start anything. Leave the door open, get everyone out, and walk well away.
3. From a safe distance, call 911 and Consumers Energy's gas emergency line. Do not go back until they say it is safe.
4. No gas smell? Check that every CO alarm works (press test). Replace batteries.
5. Look at the gas meter, furnace and water heater vents, and dryer vent outside. Clear snow, ice, or debris from all vents.
6. Look for downed or sagging lines, damaged meter base, and wet or scorched outlets. Stay 30+ feet from any downed line and report it.
7. Check the roof, chimney, and pipes (especially if pipes froze). Photograph all damage before cleanup for insurance.

## Caution
Do not relight pilot lights or turn a gas valve back on yourself if the gas was shut off at the meter — the utility or a licensed technician must do it. Treat every downed wire as live.

## Field note
Natural gas has odorant (mercaptan) added so you can smell it, but people with a cold or reduced sense of smell may miss it. Keep a battery CO alarm on every level; a combustible-gas detector is a cheap extra.
