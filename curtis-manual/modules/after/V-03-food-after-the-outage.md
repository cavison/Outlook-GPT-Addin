Decide what in the fridge, freezer, and pantry is safe to keep once power returns — and clean up so the appliances are safe again.

## You need
- Appliance thermometer or food thermometer
- Heavy trash bags, gloves
- Baking soda, unscented bleach, rags
- Cooler and ice if sorting takes a while

## Steps
1. Check the thermometer in the fridge and freezer before you open them wide.
2. Fridge: if power was off more than 4 hours with the door closed, throw out perishables (meat, dairy, leftovers, cut fruit) that were above 40°F for 2+ hours.
3. Freezer: food that still has ice crystals or reads 40°F or below can be refrozen. Quality may drop; safety is fine.
4. Anything that thawed and sat above 40°F for over 2 hours: throw it out. Never taste to test.
5. Check pantry cans: throw out bulging, leaking, deeply dented, or rusted cans, and anything touched by floodwater (unless in sealed commercial cans you can sanitize).
6. Clean the empty fridge and freezer: wash with hot soapy water, rinse, wipe with 1 tablespoon unscented bleach per gallon of water. Leave open to air out. An open box of baking soda helps with odor.
7. Restart the appliance and wait until it reads 40°F or below (fridge) and 0°F (freezer) before restocking.

## Table
| Item | Keep? |
|---|---|
| Hard cheese, butter, fresh whole fruit & vegetables, jams, mustard, ketchup | Usually keep (USDA) |
| Meat, poultry, fish, milk, soft cheese, eggs, cooked leftovers above 40°F 2+ h | Throw out |
| Thawed frozen food still with ice crystals / ≤ 40°F | Refreeze |
| Opened mayonnaise or creamy dressings above 50°F 8+ h | Throw out (USDA) |

## Caution
"When in doubt, throw it out." Spoiled food can look and smell fine; reheating does not destroy all bacterial toxins.

## Field note
A coin on top of a cup of frozen water in the freezer tells you later if it thawed and refroze while you were away — if the coin sank, the freezer warmed up.
