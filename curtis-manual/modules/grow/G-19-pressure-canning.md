Safely can low-acid foods — vegetables, beans, meat, fish, broth — with a pressure canner and USDA-tested times.

## You need
- A real pressure canner (not an electric multicooker or "pressure cooker")
- USDA Complete Guide to Home Canning / NCHFP tested recipe
- Mason jars, new lids, bands, jar lifter
- Steady heat source (outdoor propane burner in an outage; check your canner's heat limits)
- Dial gauge tested for accuracy (MSU Extension offices can often help) or a weighted gauge

## Steps
1. **Use a tested recipe** for the exact food, jar size, and pack style.
2. **Prepare:** put the rack and the amount of water your canner manual says (often 2–3 inches) in the canner; keep it hot.
3. **Fill jars** with the right headspace, remove bubbles, wipe rims, add lids and bands.
4. **Vent:** lock the lid, heat until steam flows from the vent, and let it vent for 10 minutes before adding the weight or closing the petcock.
5. **Pressurize:** for 0–1,000 ft, use 10 lb on a weighted gauge or 11 lb on a dial gauge. Start timing when the correct pressure is reached.
6. **Hold pressure** for the full time. If it drops below, bring it back up and restart the timing from zero.
7. **Cool naturally:** turn off heat and let the pressure fall to zero on its own. Don't force-cool. Wait 10 minutes, then open the lid away from your face.
8. **Check seals** after 12–24 hours; refrigerate unsealed jars and use soon.

## Caution
Botulism toxin can form in improperly canned low-acid food and can kill — it may have no smell, taste or bulge. Never water-bath or oven-can low-acid food, never shorten times, and never use untested family recipes. Throw out any bulging, leaking, or spurting jar without tasting.

## Field note
Fish canned at home must follow USDA's specific fish procedure (pint jars only, long processing time); don't adapt a meat recipe. Wood stoves are hard to keep at steady pressure — a propane burner outdoors makes holding pressure much easier.
