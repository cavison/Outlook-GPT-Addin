Meat rabbits are quiet, small, and fast: a doe kindles about a month after breeding, and the young reach fryer size in about 8–12 weeks.

## You need
- 1 buck and 2–3 does of a meat breed (e.g. New Zealand, Californian)
- Hutch or wire cage per adult, roughly 30 × 36 inches (larger is better), raised off the ground
- Nest box with straw for each doe
- Water bottle or crock, feeder; hay, rabbit pellets, garden greens
- Shade, wind and rain cover; predator-proof latches

## Steps
1. **House:** one adult per cage, raised to waist height, with a solid roof, shade, and wind protection; wire floor or a resting board to keep feet healthy.
2. **Feed and water:** unlimited clean water every day (the most important item), hay, and pellets; add greens slowly to avoid gut upset.
3. **Breed:** always take the doe to the buck's cage, not the other way around. Note the date.
4. **Nest box:** add a nest box with straw about 27–28 days after breeding. Gestation is about 31 days.
5. **Kits:** check the nest daily and remove any dead kits. Kits leave the box at about 3 weeks.
6. **Wean** at about 6–8 weeks; the doe can be rebred.
7. **Harvest fryers** at about 8–12 weeks old, then clean as in [[G-15]].

## Start in the BEFORE phase
A meat rabbit setup started after the grid goes down will not feed you for 3–4 months, and pellets, hay and breeding stock may be impossible to buy. Get stock, cages and a 90-day feed supply now, and breed at least once to learn. **Check ordinances:** Hudsonville city, Georgetown Township and other township zoning may limit livestock, numbers or setbacks — call your city or township zoning office before buying.

## Caution
Rabbit meat is very lean. People who eat almost nothing but lean meat (like rabbit) for a long time can get sick from "rabbit starvation" (protein poisoning) — nausea, diarrhea, and hunger despite eating. Always eat rabbit with fat (lard, oil, nuts) and carbohydrates (potatoes, grain, beans).

## Field note
Rabbits handle Michigan cold well if dry and out of the wind, but heat is dangerous: above about 85°F they can die of heat stress. In summer, freeze water jugs and put one in each cage for the rabbit to lie against. Rabbit droppings can go straight onto the garden without composting first — a fast fertilizer source.
