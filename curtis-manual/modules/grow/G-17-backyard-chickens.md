A few hens turn kitchen scraps, bugs and feed into eggs; meat birds reach butcher size in weeks.

## You need
- Check Hudsonville/township ordinances first (many limit numbers, ban roosters, set setbacks)
- Coop: a commonly used rule is about 4 sq ft per bird inside, plus a run
- Roost bar, nest boxes (about 1 per 4–5 hens), feeder, waterer
- Layer feed, oyster shell or crushed eggshell, grit
- Hardware cloth (not chicken wire), latches that a raccoon can't open

## Steps
1. **Predator-proof:** cover run walls and roof with hardware cloth; bury or apron it along the ground. Lock birds in at dusk every night.
2. **Feed and water** daily; water freezes in winter — swap in a thawed container morning and afternoon.
3. **Calcium:** keep crushed shell available so eggs have strong shells.
4. **Collect eggs** at least daily; don't wash until just before use, and store cool.
5. **Meat birds:** broiler breeds reach butcher size in about 6–8 weeks; dual-purpose breeds take months but also lay eggs.
6. **Retired hens:** older hens make good stewing birds — cook long and slow.
7. **Butcher** cleanly with gloves, cool the carcass fast, and cook chicken to 165°F (74°C).

## Caution
Live poultry often carry Salmonella even when they look healthy. Wash hands after touching birds, eggs or the coop, keep birds out of the kitchen, and don't let small children kiss or snuggle chicks. Report sudden deaths of several birds to MDARD (possible avian flu).

## Field note
Hens slow or stop laying in short winter days. That's normal; it isn't a feed problem. Store eggs in summer for winter: unwashed, clean eggs keep for weeks in a cold spot, and you can freeze beaten eggs in portions. Crack older eggs into a separate cup first; any off smell or odd color means throw it out.
