Add fresh greens and fruit from common plants — but only ones you can identify with 100% certainty.

## You need
- A regional field guide with photos (bring it outside)
- Gloves, bags, scissors or knife
- Clean water to wash finds

## Steps
1. **100% sure or leave it.** Match every feature (leaf, stem, flower, smell, habitat) — not just one.
2. **Avoid the carrot family entirely** (white umbrella-shaped flower clusters, fern-like leaves). Poison hemlock and water hemlock grow in Michigan and can kill.
3. **Pick clean sites:** not roadsides, sprayed lawns, railroad tracks, or floodwater areas.
4. **Start small:** eat a small amount of a new plant and wait a day before eating more.
5. **Wash and cook** greens when in doubt; cooking also softens tough leaves.
6. **Know the law:** foraging rules differ on state, county and private land — ask permission on private land and check DNR rules for state land.

## Table
| Plant | Eat | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Dandelion | leaves, flowers, roots | sprayed lawns |
| Common plantain | young leaves | roadsides |
| Lamb's quarters | leaves (cook) | contains oxalates; eat in moderation |
| Purslane | stems, leaves | look-alike spurge has milky sap — no milky sap on purslane |
| Clover (red/white) | flowers, leaves in small amounts | moldy clover |
| Garlic mustard | leaves, roots (invasive — pick freely) | cook first; bitter raw |
| Cattail | young shoots, roots | iris looks similar but has no cattail "hot dog" head and is toxic |
| Wild raspberry / blackberry | berries | thorns; ID is easy |
| Staghorn sumac | red fuzzy berry clusters soaked in cold water for a drink | poison sumac has white berries and grows in wet swamps |
| Black walnut | nuts in fall | hulls stain heavily |

## Caution
Most foraging deaths come from the carrot family (water hemlock, poison hemlock) and from eating something "close enough." No folk test (watching animals, the "universal edibility test") proves a plant is safe.

## Field note
Garlic mustard is an invasive species that MSU and the DNR encourage people to pull. It grows thick in west Michigan woods in spring — you help the forest and get greens. Pull the whole plant so it can't drop seed.
