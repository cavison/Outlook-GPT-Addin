Hunt squirrels and rabbits safely and legally for fresh meat, using a .22 or air rifle where the law allows.

## You need
- Michigan hunter safety certificate (required for most hunters born on or after Jan. 1, 1960) and a base license
- Current Michigan Hunting Digest (printed)
- .22 rifle, shotgun, or air rifle legal for small game
- Hunter orange (when required), knife, gloves, game bag

## Steps
1. **Licensed and trained:** take hunter safety, buy the license, and read the current Hunting Digest for squirrel and rabbit seasons and bag limits.
2. **Check where you can shoot:** firearm discharge is generally prohibited inside city limits (including Hudsonville) and restricted near homes. Call the city or your township and read its ordinance; get landowner permission in writing.
3. **Legal gear:** use a firearm or air gun the Digest allows for that species and season.
4. **Identify:** be 100% sure of the animal and what is behind it before your finger touches the trigger.
5. **Backstop:** shoot only when a dirt bank, hillside or ground is behind the target. Never shoot at a squirrel against the sky or at water.
6. **Clean:** wear gloves (rabbits can carry tularemia), cool the meat and cook thoroughly ([[G-15]]).

## Caution
A .22 bullet can travel over a mile and skip off water or frozen ground. Treat every gun as loaded, keep the muzzle pointed safely, and never shoot toward houses, roads or people you can't see.

## Field note
Squirrels are easiest to hunt early morning in oak and hickory stands — sit still 15 minutes and they come back out. Many hunters wait until after a few hard frosts to take rabbits, partly because of tularemia concerns in warm months; whatever the season, skip any rabbit that acts sluggish or has spotted liver.
