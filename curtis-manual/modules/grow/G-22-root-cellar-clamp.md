Store potatoes, carrots, beets, cabbage and apples for months using cold, damp, dark storage instead of a fridge.

## You need
- A cool basement corner, unheated garage, crawlspace, or a spot in the yard
- Bins, crates, buckets; damp sand or sawdust
- Thermometer (and a hygrometer if you have one)
- For a clamp: straw or leaves, a shovel, and a well-drained spot

## Steps
1. **Choose the coldest spot** that won't freeze — most root crops keep best near 32–40°F with high humidity.
2. **Cure first:** potatoes cure 1–2 weeks in a dark, airy place; winter squash cure in warmth before storage.
3. **Store only perfect produce:** no cuts, bruises or rot; don't wash — brush off soil.
4. **Pack roots** (carrots, beets) in damp sand or sawdust in buckets, not touching.
5. **Clamp outdoors:** on a high, well-drained spot, lay 6+ inches of straw, pile vegetables in a cone, cover with 6–12 inches of straw, then 4–6 inches of soil. Add a straw "chimney" at the top for airflow, and dig a ditch around it for drainage.
6. **Check weekly:** remove anything soft or rotting — one bad potato spoils its neighbors.

## Caution
Keep apples and pears apart from potatoes and carrots — they give off ethylene gas that makes potatoes sprout and carrots bitter. Potatoes must be kept dark; green ones contain solanine ([[G-05]]).

## Field note
In west Michigan, soil a few feet down stays above freezing all winter even when the air is far below zero, which is why a buried clamp works. Open it on a mild day, take out several weeks' worth at once, and reseal it fast so frost doesn't get in.
