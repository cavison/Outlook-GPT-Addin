Save seed from this year's crop so you can plant again next year without a store.

## You need
- Plants grown from open-pollinated or heirloom seed (not "F1 hybrid")
- Paper plates or screens, paper envelopes
- Jar with lid, labels, pencil

## Steps
1. Choose your best, healthiest plants to save from — not the first ones to bolt.
2. **Beans and peas:** let pods dry brown on the plant, then shell.
3. **Tomatoes:** squeeze seeds and pulp into a jar with a little water; let ferment 2–4 days, stirring daily; rinse the seeds that sink.
4. **Lettuce, radish, greens:** let a few plants go to flower and seed; shake dry heads into a bag.
5. Spread seeds on a plate in a dry, airy spot out of sun for 1–2 weeks.
6. Label each envelope with crop, variety and year.
7. Store cool, dark and dry — a sealed jar in a cool room.

## Caution
Seeds from F1 hybrids do not grow true to the parent. Squash and pumpkins cross with each other easily, so their saved seed may give odd fruit unless you hand-pollinate.

## Field note
Beans, peas, lettuce and tomatoes mostly pollinate themselves — the easiest crops for beginners. Put stored bean seed in the freezer for a few days after drying to kill bean weevil eggs before long-term storage.
