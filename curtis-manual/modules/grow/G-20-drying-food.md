Dry fruit, vegetables, herbs and lean meat with sun, air and gentle heat so they keep for months.

## You need
- Window screens or racks (clean, not galvanized or vinyl-coated), cheesecloth for a cover
- Sharp knife, cutting board
- Lemon juice (for fruit), salt
- Jars or bags for storage
- Food thermometer (for jerky)

## Steps
1. **Slice thin and even:** about ¼ inch for fruit and vegetables so they dry before spoiling.
2. **Pretreat:** dip apples and pears in lemon-juice water to slow browning; blanch most vegetables briefly in boiling water.
3. **Spread** in one layer on screens, pieces not touching.
4. **Cover** with cheesecloth to keep insects off; raise racks for airflow.
5. **Dry in the sun** on hot, dry, breezy days and bring racks inside at night so dew doesn't rewet the food. Herbs dry best hung in bunches in the shade.
6. **Jerky:** heat meat strips to 160°F (poultry 165°F) before or after drying, as USDA advises, then dry until it bends and cracks but doesn't snap.
7. **Test dryness:** fruit should be leathery with no moisture when squeezed; vegetables brittle.
8. **Condition and store:** pack loosely in a jar for a week, shaking daily; if moisture beads appear, dry more. Then seal and store cool and dark.

## Caution
Raw meat dried at low temperature can carry Salmonella and E. coli — do not skip the 160°F heating step. West Michigan summers are often too humid for sun drying meat; dry fruit and herbs outdoors, and dry meat only with controlled heat.

## Field note
A closed car parked in full sun gets very hot inside — racks of fruit or herbs on a sheet on the back deck window, with windows cracked an inch for moisture to escape, can work as a solar dryer on clear days. Food is ready faster than on an open rack, but check often so it doesn't scorch.
