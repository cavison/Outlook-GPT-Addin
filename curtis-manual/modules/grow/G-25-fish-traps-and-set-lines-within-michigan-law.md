Use extra lines, jugs, minnow traps and dip nets to catch more food with less effort — but only the ways Michigan allows for that species, water and season.

## You need
- The current Michigan Fishing Guide (print it, or the DNR app downloaded before the outage)
- Michigan fishing license (age 17+)
- Plastic jugs with caps, line, hooks, sinkers
- Wire or plastic minnow trap, bread or crackers for bait
- Dip net
- Waterproof marker and tags if gear marking is required

## Steps
1. **Read the rules first:** the Fishing Guide sets the number of lines and hooks, gear allowed, seasons, size and bag limits by species and by water. Rules on trout streams and Great Lakes tributaries are different from inland lakes.
2. **Jug or set lines:** use only where and when the guide allows them. A jug line is a hook and weight hanging from a floating jug; set depth just above bottom or weeds.
3. **Count your lines:** each jug or set line counts toward your legal line limit.
4. **Minnow trap:** bait with bread, set in shallow water near weeds, and check within hours. Use minnows for bait as the guide allows — don't move bait fish between waters.
5. **Dip net:** legal only for certain species, waters and dates (for example some smelt or sucker runs). Check before you dip.
6. **Stay with your gear:** watch or check it as the law requires, and never leave lines out where unattended lines are prohibited. Pull everything when you leave.

## Caution
Legality depends on species, water and season — a method legal on one lake can be illegal on the next creek. Unattended set lines are prohibited in many places and hook ducks, turtles and pets. Read the current Michigan Fishing Guide before every season.

## Field note
In a grid-down, the cheapest win is the minnow trap: a few minnows on hooks catch far more perch and pike than worms. Wire traps can be emptied and re-baited in a minute. Keep caught minnows alive in a bucket with frequent water changes (a lid with holes keeps them from jumping out).
