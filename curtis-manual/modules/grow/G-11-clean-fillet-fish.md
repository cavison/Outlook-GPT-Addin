Turn a fresh catch into clean food quickly, either whole (gutted) or as fillets.

## You need
- Sharp thin knife (fillet knife is best), cutting board
- Spoon or butter knife for scaling
- Cooler with ice or snow, or a bucket of cold clean water
- Bag or hole for scraps; clean water to rinse

## Steps
1. **Keep it cold:** put fish on ice or in a stringer in cold water until cleaning; clean within a few hours.
2. **Scale (if cooking whole):** scrape from tail to head with a spoon until smooth.
3. **Gut:** insert the knife tip in the vent and slit forward to the gills; pull out all guts and scrape the dark blood line along the spine.
4. **Fillet:** cut behind the gill plate down to the backbone; turn the blade toward the tail and slide along the backbone.
5. **Remove the skin:** lay the fillet skin down, hold the tail, and slide the blade between meat and skin.
6. **Rinse and chill:** wash in clean water, pat dry, keep cold, and cook soon.
7. **Dispose of scraps:** bury deeply or use as garden fertilizer, away from your house to avoid animals.

## Caution
Disinfect your water before using it to rinse fish ([[W-04]]). Fish spines and gill plates cut hands easily; a puncture from fish or scraps can get badly infected — clean it right away and watch for redness ([[M-03]]). Selling or bartering wild game or sport-caught fish is illegal in Michigan (Michigan DNR; Wildlife Conservation Order 4.3 and the fishing regulations). Share it as a gift with your own household if the rules allow, but never trade it.

## Field note
Skinning and trimming the fat — the belly flap, the fatty strip along the sides and the dark line under the skin — lowers many chemicals (like PCBs) that collect in fat. MDHHS recommends this for fish from waters with advisories. It does not remove mercury, which is in the meat.
