Get eggs and meat from a small, quiet bird that fits in a garage or shed corner and starts laying in about 6–8 weeks.

## You need
- Permission under your city/township ordinance (and confirm any state permit)
- Cage or hutch with a fine wire floor (some sloped so eggs roll out)
- Brooder box, heat lamp or heat plate, thermometer
- Shallow water dish with marbles or pebbles
- High-protein game-bird starter feed
- Incubator (still-air or forced-air) and fertile eggs, or day-old chicks

## Steps
1. **Check rules:** read Hudsonville or your township's animal ordinance. Ask MDARD/DNR whether any permit applies to quail where you live.
2. **Hatch:** incubate fertile Coturnix eggs about 99.5°F (forced-air) for roughly 17–18 days. Stop turning eggs about 3 days before hatch.
3. **Brood:** start chicks around 95°F and lower roughly 5°F a week until feathered (standard poultry practice). Use shallow water with marbles so chicks can't drown.
4. **Feed:** high-protein game-bird starter, then game-bird or layer feed as the bag directs. Add calcium (crushed oyster shell) once hens lay.
5. **Eggs:** hens usually start laying at about 6–8 weeks old and need about 14+ hours of light to lay well; laying slows in winter without light.
6. **Keep clean:** remove droppings often — quail are small, but ammonia builds fast in a closed garage.

## Caution
Chicks drown in ordinary water dishes and chill quickly — brooder heat must be steady and safe from fire (secure heat lamps two ways). Wash hands after handling birds or eggs; poultry can carry Salmonella (CDC).

## Field note
About 4–5 quail eggs equal one chicken egg. Quail don't need a big run, are quieter than chickens, and a single trio (1 male, 2–3 hens) plus a cheap incubator can rebuild a flock in a few months — keep a hand-turning incubator or a broody plan if power is down. Store extra fertile eggs pointy end down and set them within about a week for best hatch.
