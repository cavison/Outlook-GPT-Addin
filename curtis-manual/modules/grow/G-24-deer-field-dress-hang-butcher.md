Legally take, clean, cool and cut up a white-tailed deer — from a hunt or a car-killed deer — without wasting meat or spreading disease.

## You need
- Michigan deer license + kill tag, or a DNR salvage permit for a road-killed deer
- Long nitrile or rubber gloves (several pairs)
- Sharp knife with 3–4 in. blade, sharpener, bone saw (optional)
- Rope, gambrel or stout stick, a tree limb or garage beam
- Clean bags, cheesecloth or game bags, bucket of water, paper towels
- Probe thermometer

## Steps
1. **Be legal:** hunt only in open season with the right license and tag the deer the way the current Michigan Hunting Digest says. For a car-killed deer, get the free DNR salvage permit (DNR online system) before or promptly after taking it — check the current procedure.
2. **Protect yourself:** wear gloves. Avoid cutting through the spine, brain or lymph nodes; use separate knives for gutting and meat if you can.
3. **Field-dress:** deer on its back, cut from the pelvis to the breastbone with the blade edge up so you don't puncture the guts. Cut the windpipe, roll out the organs, and cut around the anus. Keep the heart and liver in a clean bag.
4. **Cool fast:** prop the cavity open with a stick, wipe out blood and hair, and get it off the ground into shade and air.
5. **Hang:** hang in a place that stays about 34–40°F (below 40°F, not frozen solid). Skin when ready — skin comes off easiest while warm, but the hide protects meat from drying and dirt. Above 40°F, butcher the same day ([[F-17]]).
6. **Butcher:** take off the front shoulders (no ball joint — they lift off), then the backstraps along the spine and tenderloins inside, then the hindquarters (hams) at the hip joint. Bone the meat out rather than sawing through bone; trim fat, hair and bloodshot meat; neck and rib meat go to grind or stew.

## Caution
Chronic wasting disease (CWD) is present in Michigan deer, including in counties near Ottawa. Don't shoot or salvage a deer that looks sick or acts strangely, wear gloves, don't saw through brain or spinal cord, and don't eat meat from a deer that tests positive (CDC/DNR). Get it tested where the DNR offers testing.

## Field note
Debone instead of sawing: it keeps spinal tissue (where CWD prions concentrate) off the meat and lets cuts cool faster. Many Michigan hunters hang deer in an unheated garage — use a cheap thermometer on the wall and a probe in the hindquarter; if the garage tops 40°F in a warm November spell, quarter the deer and pack it in coolers with ice or snow in sealed bags.

## Michigan notes
Seasons, deer management units, antler rules and CWD testing locations change yearly — read the current Michigan Hunting Digest and DNR CWD pages. Hunter safety education is required for most new hunters ([[G-27]]).
