Pick crops by how fast they feed you, and plant them inside west Michigan's frost window.

## You need
- Seeds (check the packet's "days to maturity")
- Any sunny spot: bed, bucket, or windowsill
- Water, a trowel or spoon, labels
- A calendar to track frost dates

## Steps
1. **Days, not weeks:** Start sprouts today ([[F-12]]) — edible in a few days, no soil.
2. **1–2 weeks:** Grow microgreens on a sunny windowsill, about 7–14 days ([[G-02]]).
3. **About a month:** Sow radishes (about 25–30 days) and leaf lettuce or other greens (about 30–45 days; pick outer leaves early).
4. **About two months:** Plant bush beans after frost danger passes and soil is warm (about 50–60 days).
5. **Two to four months:** Plant potatoes for calories (about 70–120 days, depending on variety) ([[G-05]]).
6. **Plant in waves:** Sow a short row of radishes and greens every 1–2 weeks so food keeps coming.

## Table
| Crop | Approx. days to eat | Frost-hardy? |
|---|---|---|
| Sprouts | a few days | indoors |
| Microgreens | 7–14 | indoors |
| Radishes | 25–30 | yes, light frost |
| Lettuce / greens | 30–45 | yes, light frost |
| Bush beans | 50–60 | no |
| Potatoes | 70–120 | plant early, protect shoots |

## Michigan notes
For Ottawa County, last spring frost is roughly mid-May and first fall frost is roughly early to mid-October. These are approximate averages — frost can come earlier or later. Radishes, lettuce, peas and spinach can go in several weeks before the last frost; beans, squash and tomatoes wait until after it.

## Caution
Do not count on a garden to feed you in a short outage — it supplements stored food. Seeds planted in cold, wet spring soil (especially beans) often rot instead of sprouting.

## Field note
Lake Michigan moderates nearby temperatures, but low spots inland frost first because cold air drains downhill. Plant tender crops on slightly higher ground or against a south-facing wall, and cover them with an old sheet on clear, still nights.
