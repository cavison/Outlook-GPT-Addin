Fish are great protein, but only if they are cooked thoroughly, fresh, and from water that is safe to eat from.

## You need
- MDHHS "Eat Safe Fish" guide for your area (print it now; it is free)
- Cooking pot or pan and fuel
- Food thermometer if you have one

## Steps
1. **Check the guide:** look up the lake or river and species in the MDHHS Eat Safe Fish guide for how many servings per month are safe.
2. **Avoid bad water:** do not eat fish from floodwater, water near sewage overflows, or water with oil sheens or dead fish.
3. **Smell and look:** fresh fish smell mild, have firm flesh that springs back, clear eyes and red gills.
4. **Reject spoiled fish:** strong sour or ammonia smell, slimy or mushy flesh, dull sunken eyes, brown or gray gills — throw it out.
5. **Cook thoroughly:** to 145°F (63°C), until the flesh is opaque and flakes with a fork. Do not eat freshwater fish raw.
6. **Eat smaller fish:** panfish and younger fish usually carry fewer contaminants than large, old predators.

## Signs
Get help if, after eating fish, someone has: tingling or numbness in lips or mouth, trouble breathing, or severe vomiting and diarrhea ([[M-13]]).

## Caution
Freshwater fish can carry parasites (such as tapeworm) that are killed by thorough cooking. Children and anyone pregnant or nursing should follow the stricter MDHHS limits because mercury and PCBs affect developing brains.

## Field note
During and after a long outage, sewage plants may lose power and overflow into rivers like the Grand. Fish from downstream of an overflow can be contaminated even if the water looks normal — fish upstream, or in a separate lake, until things settle.
