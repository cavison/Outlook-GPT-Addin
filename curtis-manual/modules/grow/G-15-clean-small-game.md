Skin and gut a rabbit or squirrel cleanly while protecting yourself from germs the animal may carry.

## You need
- Disposable or rubber gloves (essential)
- Sharp knife, game shears or strong scissors
- Bucket of disinfected cold water, clean bowl, plastic bags
- Bleach solution for tools and surfaces

## Steps
1. **Glove up:** wear gloves the whole time; don't touch your face.
2. **Look first:** skip any animal that was acting sick, sluggish or tame before you caught it.
3. **Skin:** pinch skin on the back, cut a small slit, then pull the skin apart toward head and feet, like taking off a sweater.
4. **Remove head and feet** with shears.
5. **Gut:** cut carefully up the belly without puncturing the intestines or bladder; pull all organs out.
6. **Inspect the liver:** a healthy liver is smooth and dark red. White or yellow spots mean throw the whole animal away (bury it), and wash up.
7. **Rinse and chill:** rinse in disinfected cold water and keep cold; cook thoroughly (to at least 160°F / 71°C for small game).
8. **Clean up:** bleach tools and surfaces; bury or bag scraps; wash hands well.

## Caution
Rabbits and rodents can carry tularemia, which can infect you through cuts, splashes in the eye, or undercooked meat. Gloves and thorough cooking are the main protection. Do not eat squirrel or other animal brains.

## Field note
Rabbits can carry warbles (bot fly larvae) under the skin in late summer; they look nasty but the meat underneath is usually fine once the area is trimmed. White spots on the liver are the warning sign that matters most.
