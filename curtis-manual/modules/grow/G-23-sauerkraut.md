Turn cabbage and salt into sauerkraut — a long-keeping, vitamin C–rich food that needs no power or canning.

## You need
- 5 lb fresh cabbage
- 3 tablespoons canning or pickling salt (USDA ratio; non-iodized)
- Food-grade crock, bucket, or large glass jars
- Plate that fits inside, plus a weight (water-filled jar or a food-safe bag of brine)
- Clean cloth, knife, large bowl

## Steps
1. **Shred:** remove outer leaves, quarter, core, and slice thin.
2. **Salt:** mix 3 tablespoons salt with 5 lb shredded cabbage; let sit a few minutes.
3. **Pack:** press firmly into the crock in layers until juice rises above the cabbage.
4. **Weigh it down:** cover with a plate and weight so all cabbage stays under the brine; cover the crock with a cloth.
5. **Ferment at 70–75°F** for about 3–4 weeks; at 60–65°F expect about 5–6 weeks. Below about 60°F it may not ferment properly.
6. **Check a few times a week:** skim any surface scum; keep cabbage under brine.
7. **Store:** when it tastes sour and bubbling stops, keep it cold (cool basement or fridge), or can it by USDA directions.

## Caution
Throw it out if the kraut is pink, slimy, soft, or has a bad smell, or if fuzzy mold reaches below the surface. Too little salt or warm temperatures (above about 75°F) make soft, spoiled kraut.

## Field note
Sauerkraut was carried on long sea voyages to help prevent scurvy. A small daily serving of raw (unheated) kraut adds vitamin C to a canned-food diet — heating reduces it, so stir it into food after cooking.
