Quickly identify common edible fish around Ottawa County so you keep the right ones, release the protected ones, and know how to cook them.

## You need
- This card, a measuring tape, pliers
- The current Michigan Fishing Guide and MDHHS Eat Safe Fish guide

## Steps
1. **Shape and mouth:** round and flat (panfish), long and torpedo (pike/walleye), whiskers (catfish), small sucking mouth underneath (suckers, carp).
2. **Marks:** count bars and spots, check fin shape and color.
3. **Dangers:** watch for teeth (pike, walleye, gar, bowfin) and catfish/bullhead fin spines.
4. **Rules:** check the season, size and bag limit for that species and water.
5. **Eat safe:** look up the river or lake in Eat Safe Fish (MDHHS) — the Grand River has advisories for some species.
6. **Sturgeon:** release lake sturgeon immediately.
7. **Gar and bowfin:** handle with pliers; both have sharp teeth.

## Table
| Fish | Key ID marks | Eating notes |
|---|---|---|
| Bluegill / sunfish | Deep, flat oval body; dark "ear" flap (bluegill) | Excellent; fry whole or fillet |
| Yellow perch | Yellow-gold, 6–8 dark vertical bars, orange lower fins | Excellent, mild |
| Crappie (black/white) | Speckled silver; big dorsal and anal fins; thin mouth | Excellent; mouth tears — lift gently |
| Largemouth bass | Upper jaw extends past eye; dark side stripe | Good; smaller ones taste best |
| Smallmouth bass | Bronze with vertical bars; jaw ends below eye | Good |
| Walleye | Big glassy eye, sharp teeth, white tip on lower tail | Prized; mild white fillets |
| Northern pike | Long body, duck-bill jaw, light bean-shaped spots | Good, but has Y-bones |
| Channel catfish | Forked tail, whiskers, often spotted | Good; skin it; spines sting |
| Bullhead | Square/rounded tail, whiskers | Good in cold water; skin it |
| Suckers | Small, down-pointing fleshy mouth | Bony; best ground, scored or canned |
| Common carp | Big scales, two whisker pairs on each side of mouth | Edible; score or can for bones; check advisories |
| Salmon / steelhead | Grand River fall/spring runs; silver, black spots | Good; special river rules and advisories |
| Gar / bowfin | Gar: long bony snout; bowfin: long back fin | Edible but bony; sharp teeth |
| Lake sturgeon | Bony plates, shark-like tail, whiskers | **Protected — never keep. Release at once.** |

## Caution
Lake sturgeon are protected in Michigan: release immediately with minimal handling. Larger, older and fattier fish (catfish, carp, salmon) often have stricter Eat Safe Fish limits — check before eating, especially for kids and pregnant women.

## Field note
Skin-on vs skinless matters: removing skin, belly fat and the dark lateral line cuts many fat-stored chemicals like PCBs, which is why MDHHS recommends trimming fat and not frying in the fish's own fat. Suckers from cold spring water are better eating than their reputation — score the fillets every 1/4 inch and fry hard so small bones soften.
