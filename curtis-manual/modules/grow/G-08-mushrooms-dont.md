This is a warning, not an identification guide: in a grid-down emergency, do not eat wild mushrooms.

## You need
- Nothing. Leave them alone.
- Poison center number written down: 1-800-222-1222

## Steps
1. **Do not eat** any mushroom you picked yourself, or one a neighbor "is pretty sure" about.
2. **Do not trust tests:** silver spoons, peeling caps, animals eating them, cooking, and "smells fine" prove nothing.
3. **Do not trust apps or photos** for eating decisions.
4. **Teach kids** not to touch or taste mushrooms in the yard.
5. **If someone ate one:** call Poison Control (1-800-222-1222) right away, even if they feel fine. Save a piece of the mushroom in a paper bag.

## Caution
Deadly amanitas grow in Michigan. Their poison often causes no symptoms for 6 or more hours, then vomiting and diarrhea that may seem to get better — while the liver is failing. In an outage, a hospital and transplant care may be out of reach. The calories in mushrooms are tiny; the risk is your life.

## Field note
Mushrooms are mostly water and provide very few calories, so they do almost nothing for a food shortage. Spend foraging time on berries, nuts, greens and fish ([[G-07]], [[G-09]]) instead.
