Turn kitchen and garden waste into fishing bait and extra feed for chickens, quail and rabbits.

## You need
- 2 plastic totes with lids, drill for air holes
- Shredded newspaper or cardboard, a handful of garden soil
- Red wiggler worms (bait shop, garden store, or a friend's bin)
- A separate bucket/tote for black soldier fly larvae (BSFL), with a ramp
- Wide tub or kiddie pool for duckweed (start from a pond)

## Steps
1. **Worm bin:** drill air holes, fill with damp shredded paper (like a wrung-out sponge) and a handful of soil; add worms.
2. **Feed worms:** vegetable scraps, coffee grounds, eggshells. No meat, dairy or oily food. Keep the bin between about 55–77°F — indoors or a basement in winter.
3. **BSF bin:** wet kitchen scraps attract wild black soldier flies in Michigan's warm months (they don't survive outdoor winters).
4. **Self-harvest:** mature larvae crawl up a ramp into a collection bucket — feed them to chickens or quail, or use for bait.
5. **Duckweed:** float a start in a tub of water in partial shade; it can double fast in summer. Skim half every few days, rinse, and feed fresh to poultry.
6. **Garden scraps:** feed each animal only what's safe for it — for rabbits: grass hay, dandelion, plantain, carrot tops, small amounts of leafy greens; for chickens: most veggie scraps, weeds, and bugs.

## Caution
Moldy or spoiled feed sickens animals. Never feed avocado (toxic to birds and rabbits), and don't give rabbits large amounts of sudden new greens. Don't release red wigglers or bait into Michigan waters or woods.

## Field note
Nightcrawlers come up on wet spring nights — use a red-light flashlight on a mowed lawn and grab quickly. A worm bin of red wigglers gives smaller but steady bait all winter; keep it in the basement so it doesn't freeze.
