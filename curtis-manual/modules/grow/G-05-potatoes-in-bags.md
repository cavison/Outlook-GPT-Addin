Potatoes give the most calories for the space of any easy crop; grow them in layers in a bag or bucket.

## You need
- Seed potatoes (certified, or sprouted grocery potatoes as a backup)
- Grow bag, trash can, or 5-gallon bucket with drain holes
- Soil, compost, straw or leaves
- Knife

## Steps
1. Cut large seed potatoes into chunks with at least 1–2 "eyes" each; let cut sides dry for a day or two.
2. Put 4–6 inches of soil in the bottom of the container.
3. Set 2–3 pieces (bucket) or 4–6 (large bag) on the soil, eyes up; cover with 3–4 inches of soil.
4. When shoots are 6–8 inches tall, add soil or straw until only the top few leaves show.
5. Repeat "hilling" until the container is nearly full.
6. Keep evenly moist; do not let it dry out and then flood.
7. Dig "new potatoes" after flowering; harvest the rest after the vines die back (about 70–120 days by variety).
8. Cure in a dark, cool, airy place for about 1–2 weeks before storing.

## Caution
Green skin on potatoes means solanine, a natural toxin. Cut away all green parts and sprouts, and throw out potatoes that are very green or taste bitter. Keep tubers covered with soil so they don't turn green while growing.

## Field note
In Ottawa County, plant potatoes a few weeks before the last frost (roughly mid-May, approximate); if frost threatens after shoots appear, cover them with more soil or straw. Grocery potatoes are sometimes treated with sprout inhibitor, so certified seed potatoes sprout more reliably and avoid bringing in disease.
