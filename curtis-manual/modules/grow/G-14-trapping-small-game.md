Trapping can add meat, but in Michigan it is tightly regulated — know the law before you set anything.

## You need
- Correct Michigan DNR license (fur harvester and/or small game, as the species requires)
- Current Michigan DNR Hunting and Trapping Digest
- Cage (live) trap sized for the animal
- Bait: apple, carrot, corn, peanut butter (rabbits, squirrels); follow digest rules
- Gloves, ID tag with your name/address or DNR ID (if required)

## Steps
1. **Check the law:** look up the species, season, license, and allowed trap types in the current DNR digest. Many species and methods have closed seasons.
2. **Get permission:** trap only on your own land or with landowner permission; follow state-land rules.
3. **Scout:** find runs, tracks, droppings and chewed plants along fence lines, brush piles and field edges.
4. **Set:** place the cage trap on a flat run, door open, bait at the far end; level it so it doesn't rock.
5. **Hide:** brush around the sides lightly so the animal must enter from the front.
6. **Check daily:** check traps at least as often as the law requires (once per day is a common rule) — sooner in extreme cold or heat.
7. **Release** non-target animals, pets and protected species immediately and gently.

## Caution
Snares and body-gripping traps are heavily restricted in Michigan and can kill pets, raptors and protected animals — do not improvise them. Illegal trapping can bring fines and loss of hunting rights, and it takes food from the whole community's future.

## Field note
In a residential area, a cage trap catches the same backyard rabbits and squirrels that eat your garden — protecting crops and producing meat. Michigan allows landowners to take some nuisance animals under specific rules; check the DNR's "nuisance animal" guidance before acting.
