Fish hold where there is food, cover and comfortable water; find those spots and fish the low-light hours.

## You need
- Rig from [[G-09]]
- Polarized sunglasses if you have them
- Notebook to log what worked
- Current Michigan DNR Fishing Guide (seasons, sizes, limits)

## Steps
1. **Inflows:** fish where a creek, culvert or ditch enters a lake or river — current carries food and oxygen.
2. **Structure:** cast next to fallen trees, docks, bridge pilings, rock piles and weed edges.
3. **Drop-offs:** look for where shallow water suddenly gets deeper; fish move along that edge.
4. **Shade:** on bright days fish move under overhanging trees, docks and lily pads.
5. **Time:** fish the first and last hour of daylight (dawn and dusk); also before storms.
6. **Move:** if no bites in 15–20 minutes, try a new spot or depth.

## Michigan notes
- **Small lakes and ponds:** bluegill, sunfish, perch, bass near weed edges and docks.
- **Grand River:** catfish, suckers, panfish and more; fish slower water behind obstacles and below dams (obey posted closures).
- **Pigeon Creek and small creeks:** check DNR stream type — some are trout streams with special seasons and rules.
- **Lake Michigan piers:** perch and other species, but piers are dangerous in wind and waves.
Rules change yearly — check the DNR Fishing Guide.

## Caution
Do not fish from breakwalls or piers when waves are breaking over them; people are swept off every year. Stay off river banks undercut by high water, and wear a life jacket in a boat or near fast current.

## Field note
In spring, bluegill and sunfish nest in shallow water in plain sight — look for pale, plate-sized circles on the bottom. A worm dropped near those beds often gets hit fast. Spring and early summer are the easiest times for beginners.
