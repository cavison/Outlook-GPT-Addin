Fish hold where there is food, cover and comfortable water; find those spots and fish the low-light hours.

## You need
- Rig from [[G-09]]
- Polarized sunglasses if you have them
- Notebook to log what worked
- Current Michigan DNR Fishing Guide (seasons, sizes, limits)

## Steps
1. **Inflows:** fish where a creek, culvert or ditch enters a lake or river — current carries food and oxygen.
2. **Structure:** cast next to fallen trees, docks, bridge pilings, rock piles and weed edges.
3. **Drop-offs:** look for where shallow water suddenly gets deeper; fish move along that edge.
4. **Shade:** on bright days fish move under overhanging trees, docks and lily pads.
5. **Time:** fish the first and last hour of daylight (dawn and dusk); also before storms.
6. **Move:** if no bites in 15–20 minutes, try a new spot or depth.

## Michigan notes
- **Small lakes and ponds:** bluegill, sunfish, perch, bass near weed edges and docks.
- **Grand River:** catfish, suckers, panfish and more; fish slower water behind obstacles and below dams (obey posted closures).
- **Pigeon Creek and small creeks:** check DNR stream type — some are trout streams with special seasons and rules.
- **Lake Michigan piers:** perch and other species, but piers are dangerous in wind and waves.
Rules change yearly — check the DNR Fishing Guide.

**Public spots near Hudsonville** (verify access, hours and closures before you go):
- **Grand River Park** (Ottawa County Parks, 28th Ave., Georgetown Township near Jenison): Grand River access, boat launch, and a fishing dock on a small lake.
- **Grand Ravines** (Ottawa County Parks): about half a mile of Grand River frontage.
- **Eastmanville Bayou** (Grand River, Ottawa County): on the DNR's family-friendly fishing waters list.
- **Bass River Recreation Area** (state land, Grand River and Max Lake between Grand Rapids and Grand Haven).
- **Riverside Park** (Ottawa County Parks, near Grand Haven): barrier-free fishing dock on the Grand River.
- **Sixth Street Dam / Fish Ladder Park** (downtown Grand Rapids): steelhead and salmon stack up below the dam in spring and fall. A river-restoration plan may remove this dam — check current status and DNR closures.
- **Grand Haven pier and channel; Lake Macatawa** (Holland): perch, panfish and seasonal salmon/trout — piers are deadly in waves (see Caution).

## Table
Typical west Michigan pattern; runs shift 2–3 weeks with weather. Check seasons in the DNR Fishing Guide.

| Month | Best bets |
|---|---|
| Jan–Feb | Ice fishing for bluegill, perch, crappie on small lakes — only on solid clear ice |
| Mar | Suckers start running up creeks and rivers; spring steelhead below Grand River dams (often mid-to-late March) |
| Apr | Steelhead and suckers; crappie move shallow as ice leaves |
| May | Bluegill and sunfish move shallow; catfish start on the Grand |
| Jun | Panfish on beds — easiest month for beginners; bass (check season opener) |
| Jul–Aug | Panfish and bass at dawn/dusk and deeper weed edges; catfish at night; perch off piers |
| Sep | Chinook and coho salmon begin running up the Grand (mid-September) |
| Oct | Salmon peak; fall steelhead start late October |
| Nov | Steelhead in rivers; perch; panfish before freeze-up |
| Dec | Steelhead in rivers; first ice is the most dangerous ice |

## Caution
Do not fish from breakwalls or piers when waves are breaking over them; people are swept off every year. Stay off river banks undercut by high water, and wear a life jacket in a boat or near fast current.

## Field note
In spring, bluegill and sunfish nest in shallow water in plain sight — look for pale, plate-sized circles on the bottom. A worm dropped near those beds often gets hit fast. Spring and early summer are the easiest times for beginners.
