Turn a patch of lawn into one productive 4×8-foot bed you can reach from both sides.

## You need
- Shovel or spade, garden fork, rake
- Cardboard (tape and labels removed)
- Compost, leaves, grass clippings
- Optional: boards or logs for an edge
- String and stakes

## Steps
1. Pick the sunniest flat spot (6–8+ hours of sun), away from downspouts that carry roof runoff.
2. Mark a 4×8-foot rectangle with stakes and string — 4 feet wide so you never step in it.
3. Either dig and turn the sod, or lay cardboard over the grass and wet it ("no-dig").
4. Pile 4–8 inches of compost and soil on top; add an edge if you have boards.
5. Divide into 1-foot squares (32 squares) with string.
6. Plant by spacing: e.g. 16 radishes, 4 lettuce, or 9 bush beans per square; 1 potato or tomato takes a square or more.
7. Mulch around plants with leaves or straw to hold water and block weeds.

## Caution
Soil near older houses, driveways and busy roads can hold lead. Keep beds away from old painted siding, wash produce, peel root crops, and have soil tested by MSU Extension when you can.

## Field note
Cardboard laid over lawn in fall, covered with leaves, will smother the grass by spring — the easiest way to make a bed with no tiller. Ottawa County has a lot of sandy soil, which drains fast and needs more compost and more frequent watering than clay.
