Grow fresh, crunchy vitamin-rich food in a jar on the counter, with no soil or sun.

## You need
- Seeds sold for sprouting (lentils, mung beans, alfalfa, radish, broccoli)
- Quart glass jar with mesh lid, screen or cheesecloth and rubber band
- Safe water (disinfected or boiled and cooled)

## Steps
1. Put 2–3 tablespoons of seeds (or 1/2 cup lentils/mung beans) in the jar. Rinse.
2. Cover with safe water and soak 8–12 hours.
3. Drain well. Tip the jar upside down at an angle in a bowl so water runs out and air gets in.
4. Rinse and drain 2–3 times a day with safe water.
5. Keep out of direct sun at room temperature. Most sprouts are ready in 3–5 days.
6. Throw out any batch that smells sour, looks slimy, or grows fuzzy mold.
7. Cook sprouts before eating if anyone is young, old, pregnant, or ill.

## Caution
Raw sprouts have caused many Salmonella and E. coli outbreaks because the warm, wet conditions that grow sprouts also grow bacteria (FDA). Cooking sprouts until steaming hot greatly reduces the risk. Never use garden seeds: many are coated with fungicide.

## Field note
Lentils and mung beans from your pantry stock usually sprout well and give you fresh food in February when nothing grows outside. Kidney and other large beans should not be eaten raw even as sprouts; cook them.
