Keep stored food fresh by always eating the oldest item first and putting new items behind it.

## You need
- Permanent marker or masking tape
- Shelves you can reach from the front
- Inventory sheet and pencil

## Steps
1. Write the purchase month and year on every can and bag in large letters.
2. Put new items at the back of the shelf.
3. Take items from the front; that is always the oldest.
4. Keep an inventory sheet on the shelf and mark items off as you use them.
5. Once a month, look for swollen or leaking cans, pests, and torn bags.
6. Store in a cool, dry, dark place; heat and moisture shorten shelf life.

## Caution
Most "best by" dates on shelf-stable food are about quality, not safety (USDA). But bulging, leaking or badly dented cans are unsafe at any date. Never store food next to fuel, paint or chemicals.

## Field note
A Michigan garage swings from below freezing in January to hot in July. That ruins canned-food quality and can split cans. A closet on an inside wall or a dry basement shelf keeps food much longer.
