Use Michigan winter cold to keep food cold, safely, without letting sun, thaw or animals ruin it.

## You need
- Hard-sided cooler or plastic tote with a latching lid
- Appliance thermometer (one inside each cooler)
- Bungee cords or a strap; heavy object for the lid
- Water jugs or ice-cube trays

## Steps
1. Pick a shaded spot on the north side of the house, off the ground (porch, table). Out of the sun all day.
2. Put a thermometer inside the cooler. Pack food in sealed bags or containers.
3. Latch and strap the lid closed; set something heavy on top. Animals open loose lids.
4. Check the thermometer at least morning and afternoon. Refrigerator food must stay at 40°F or below.
5. On thaw days or sunny afternoons, bring the cooler inside to the coldest room and add ice.
6. Make ice: fill jugs and trays with water and set them outside overnight. Use them in your indoor cooler or fridge.

## Caution
USDA warns NOT to bury food in snow: outdoor temperatures swing, sun can warm food even on a cold day, and animals or contamination can reach it. Use a closed cooler with a thermometer, and treat any food above 40°F for 2+ hours as unsafe.

## Field note
West Michigan winters often thaw for a day or two between cold snaps (and lake-effect sun breaks can warm a dark cooler fast). A white or light-colored cooler on the north side stays far steadier. Raccoons, skunks, opossums and neighborhood dogs will find food smells; a latched hard cooler and strap is the minimum.
