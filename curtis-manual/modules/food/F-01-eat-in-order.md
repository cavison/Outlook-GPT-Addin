Eat the food that spoils first, so nothing goes to waste and the pantry lasts longest.

## You need
- Fridge thermometer and freezer thermometer
- Cooler and ice (or snow-cooled containers in winter)
- Marker and paper for a door log

## Steps
1. Day 1: eat fresh and refrigerated food first. A closed fridge keeps food cold about 4 hours (USDA).
2. Keep the fridge and freezer doors shut. Write the time power went out on the door.
3. Next, eat freezer food as it thaws. A full freezer holds about 48 hours, half-full about 24 hours, if kept closed (USDA).
4. Cook thawed meat completely and eat it the same day, or share it with neighbors.
5. Then move to sturdy fresh produce: apples, carrots, potatoes, onions, cabbage.
6. Last, open shelf-stable pantry food: canned goods, rice, beans, flour.
7. Check temperatures before eating anything from the fridge. Discard perishables above 40°F for 2+ hours.

## Caution
Thawed meat that has sat above 40°F for more than 2 hours can make you very sick even if it smells fine. Cooking does not destroy every toxin bacteria leave behind. When in doubt, throw it out.

## Field note
Group freezer food into one tight block (or one freezer) on day 1. Packed food stays cold longer than scattered items. In winter, set jugs of water outside overnight to freeze, then put them in the fridge as free ice blocks.
