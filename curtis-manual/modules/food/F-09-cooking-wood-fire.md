Cook safely over an outdoor wood fire using coals, not flames, for even heat and less burned food.

## You need
- Fire pit, ring of stones, or bare dirt area
- Dry hardwood and kindling
- Grate or bricks to hold the pot
- Cast iron or heavy pot, long tongs, leather gloves
- Bucket of water and a shovel

## Steps
1. Check local burn rules (township or fire department) and wind. Clear a 10-foot circle of anything that burns.
2. Build the fire on one side of the pit. Let it burn down to glowing coals (about 30–45 minutes).
3. Rake coals to the cooking side. Keep a small fire on the other side to make more coals.
4. Set the grate or pot on bricks 4–6 inches above the coals.
5. Control heat by moving the pot or adding/removing coals, not by adding big logs.
6. Use a lid to cook faster and keep ash out.
7. When done, drown the fire with water, stir, and drown again until it is cold to the touch.

## Caution
Cooking over high flames chars the outside and leaves the inside raw. Keep long hair, loose sleeves and synthetic jackets away from fire; synthetics melt onto skin. Never use gasoline to start or revive a fire.

## Field note
Use hardwoods common in west Michigan (oak, maple, ash, cherry) for long-lasting coals. Pine and other softwoods flare fast and coat pots with sticky soot. Rub dish soap on the outside of a pot before cooking: the soot washes off far more easily afterward.
