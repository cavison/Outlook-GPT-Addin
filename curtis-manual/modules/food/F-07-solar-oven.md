Cook or heat food with sunshine on clear days using a cardboard box, foil and a clear cover.

## You need
- Two cardboard boxes, one fitting inside the other with 1–2 in gap
- Aluminum foil and glue or tape
- Newspaper for insulation
- Clear oven bag, glass pane, or clear plastic sheet
- Black paint or black paper; dark pot with lid
- Stick to prop the reflector; oven thermometer

## Steps
1. Set the small box inside the big box. Stuff crumpled newspaper in the gap on all sides and bottom.
2. Line the inside of the small box with foil, shiny side out. Put black paper or a black tray on the floor.
3. Cut a reflector flap from the big box's lid; cover it with smooth foil.
4. Cover the opening with a clear oven bag or glass, sealed with tape, to trap heat.
5. Face the oven at the sun and prop the reflector so it bounces light into the box.
6. Put food in a dark, lidded pot inside. Turn the box every 30–60 minutes to follow the sun.
7. Check with a thermometer. Use for foods that are safe warm (rice, oats, lentils, reheating) unless you can confirm meat reaches safe temperatures.

## Caution
A cardboard oven can fail to get hot enough when clouds come in, leaving food in the danger zone. Do not cook raw meat, poultry or eggs unless a food thermometer shows the safe temperature (USDA). Reflected sun can burn eyes and hands; use sunglasses and oven mitts.

## Field note
In west Michigan solar ovens work best on clear days from about late spring to early fall, near midday. Lake-effect cloud cover makes them unreliable most of the winter. Pre-heat the empty oven for 30 minutes, and use a thin dark pot: a heavy cast-iron pot wastes the heat.
