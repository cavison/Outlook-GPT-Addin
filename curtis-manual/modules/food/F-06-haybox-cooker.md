Finish cooking rice, beans, oats and stews with stored heat in an insulated box, saving most of your fuel.

## You need
- Pot with a tight lid (heavier pots hold heat better)
- Cooler, box, or laundry basket
- Insulation: blankets, pillows, towels, sleeping bag, or crumpled newspaper
- Instant-read thermometer

## Steps
1. Line the box with at least 3–4 inches of insulation on the bottom and sides, leaving a nest the size of the pot.
2. Bring food to a full boil on the stove. Simmer a few minutes so the whole pot is hot through (dry beans: soaked, then hard boil at least 10 minutes first).
3. Lid on tight. Move the pot straight into the nest with no delay.
4. Pack insulation on top and around so no air gaps remain. Close the box.
5. Leave it alone: rice and oats are often done in 1–2 hours, soaked beans several hours. Do not open to peek.
6. Before eating, check it is still above 140°F. If not, bring it back to a boil.

## Caution
Food that cools into the 40–140°F danger zone for hours can grow bacteria. Check the temperature, and re-boil anything that has cooled before serving. Do not use a haybox for raw meat unless you bring it to a boil first and confirm 140°F+ when you open it.

## Field note
Red and white kidney beans contain a toxin that slow, low cooking does not destroy (FDA). Always hard-boil soaked beans for at least 10 minutes BEFORE they go in the haybox. A wrapped sleeping bag rated for cold is one of the best haybox insulators a Michigan household already owns.
