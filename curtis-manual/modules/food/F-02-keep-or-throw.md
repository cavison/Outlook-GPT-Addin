Decide quickly and safely whether food from a powerless fridge, freezer or damaged can is still safe to eat.

## You need
- Instant-read food thermometer
- Appliance thermometers in fridge and freezer
- Trash bags

## Steps
1. Check the fridge thermometer. If perishables (meat, dairy, eggs, leftovers, cut fruit) sat above 40°F for 2+ hours, throw them out.
2. Check the freezer. Food with ice crystals, or at 40°F or below, can be refrozen or cooked (USDA).
3. Hard cheeses, butter, whole fresh fruit and vegetables, bread, jams, peanut butter and most condiments usually keep without power. Check the USDA chart.
4. Inspect cans: throw out cans that bulge, leak, spurt when opened, are deeply dented on a seam, or rusted through.
5. Throw out any food that touched floodwater unless it is in an undamaged commercial can (then wash and sanitize the can first).
6. Never taste food to test it. Bacteria that cause illness often do not change smell or look.

## Caution
Botulism can come from bulging or leaking cans and from badly home-canned low-acid food. Do not open or taste a suspect can; bag it and throw it out. Botulism is a medical emergency.

## Field note
Put a coin on top of a cup of frozen water in your freezer before an outage. If you return and the coin has sunk to the bottom, everything thawed and refroze, and the meat and dairy are unsafe even if they feel frozen now.
