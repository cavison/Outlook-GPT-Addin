Build an outdoor cookstove from 16 bricks with no mortar that boils water on a few handfuls of sticks.

## You need
- 16 solid clay bricks (about 8 x 4 x 2 1/4 in) — not hollow decorative pavers
- Flat, level, non-flammable ground (patio, gravel, bare dirt)
- 3 small stones, bolts or brick pieces as pot rests
- Dry sticks thumb-thick or smaller, and tinder
- Pot with a lid, heat-proof gloves

## Steps
1. Pick a spot outdoors, at least 10 ft from buildings, fences and anything that burns. Level the ground.
2. Layer 1: set 3 bricks on edge in a U shape. The open side is the fuel door, facing into the wind.
3. Layer 2: lay 4 bricks in a pinwheel square (each brick's end touches the next brick's side). It bridges over the fuel door and leaves a square chimney in the middle.
4. Layers 3 and 4: repeat the pinwheel, turning it the other way each layer so joints overlap. That uses 15 bricks.
5. Break the 16th brick (or use 3 stones or bolts) into 3 pot rests on top, leaving a finger-width gap under the pot for smoke to escape.
6. Light tinder inside the fuel door. Feed sticks in by their tips; push them in as they burn.
7. Set the pot on the rests with a lid on. Keep the fire small and hot; more sticks is not faster.
8. To finish, pull out unburned sticks and drown the coals with water. Let bricks cool before moving them.

## Caution
Use the stove outdoors only; it makes carbon monoxide. Wet or concrete blocks can crack or spall from heat, so use dry clay bricks and keep children and pets back — the top bricks get very hot.

## Field note
The gap under the pot matters more than anything else: if the pot sits flat on the chimney the fire chokes and smokes. A sheet of metal or a flat stone laid as a shelf in the fuel door (sticks on top, air underneath) makes it burn much cleaner. In winter, shovel down to bare ground; snowmelt under the stove kills the draft.
