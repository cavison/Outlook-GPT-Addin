Protect dry food from mice, rats, pantry moths and weevils so your stockpile lasts the full 90 days.

## You need
- Food-grade buckets with tight lids (gamma lids make opening easy)
- Glass jars, metal cans or tins with tight lids
- Freezer (before an outage) or winter cold
- Snap traps; flashlight

## Steps
1. Move flour, rice, oats, beans, pasta, cereal and pet food out of paper and thin plastic into hard containers.
2. Before an outage, freeze new bags of flour, grains and beans for several days to kill hidden insect eggs (extension services commonly advise about 4 days at 0°F).
3. Store containers off the floor and away from walls so you can see droppings.
4. Check monthly for droppings, webbing, holes, and small moths or beetles.
5. Throw out infested food (or feed to chickens if allowed); vacuum or wipe the shelf.
6. Set snap traps along walls behind food areas if you see mouse signs.

## Caution
Do not use poison baits or insect sprays near food or where children and pets can reach them. Mouse droppings can spread hantavirus: do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings; wet them with disinfectant first and wear gloves (CDC).

## Field note
Mice chew through plastic buckets’ thin spots and cardboard easily but not metal. A clean metal trash can with a tight lid is a cheap, mouse-proof bin for several bags. Michigan field mice move into houses in fall, right when winter outages come.
