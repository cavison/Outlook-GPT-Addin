Work out how much basic food your household needs to eat for 90 days without stores.

## You need
- This table, a pencil and paper
- A kitchen scale (optional)
- Your family's list of ages, allergies and favorite foods

## Steps
1. Count each household member as adult-shares (adult = 1, child = about 0.5–0.75 by age).
2. Multiply each row in the Table by your adult-shares.
3. Swap staples for foods your family already eats; keep the total calories about the same.
4. Subtract what you already have on the shelf.
5. Buy a little each grocery trip; store in sealed, pest-proof containers.
6. Label every container with contents and date, and rotate by eating it.

## Table
**Assumptions:** one adult at about 2,000 kcal/day for 90 days = about 180,000 kcal. Calorie values are rounded from USDA FoodData Central for dry/raw foods. Quantities are calculated from those values and rounded; adjust for your family's tastes and needs. Children: roughly half to three-quarters of an adult share by age; teens and hard-working adults may need more (see Dietary Guidelines for Americans). Water is NOT included (see [[W-14]]).

| Staple | Daily kcal share | Approx. kcal per lb | Per adult, 90 days |
|---|---|---|---|
| White rice (dry) | 400 | ~1,650 | 22 lb |
| All-purpose flour | 350 | ~1,650 | 19 lb |
| Rolled oats | 250 | ~1,700 | 13 lb |
| Dry beans/lentils | 250 | ~1,550 | 15 lb |
| Vegetable oil | 200 | ~4,000 | 4.5 lb (about 2.5 qt) |
| Sugar or honey | 150 | ~1,750 (sugar) | 8 lb |
| Peanut butter | 150 | ~2,650 | 5 lb |
| Nonfat dry milk | 100 | ~1,600 | 5.5 lb |
| Canned fruit/vegetables/meat | ~150 | varies | ~90 cans (1/day) |
| Salt, spices, baking powder, yeast | — | — | a few lb total (salt for cooking) |
| Multivitamin | — | — | 90 tablets |

Multiply the last column by the number of adults. Example: 2 adults + 2 kids (count as about 1.5 adults) = about 3.5 adult-shares.

## Caution
A pantry of only rice and beans will make kids, elders and sick people stop eating. Store foods they will actually eat, and plan for allergies, diabetes and infant feeding separately (see [[K-10]]).

## Field note
Dry food needs water and fuel. Every pound of rice or beans needs several quarts of water and real cooking time, so a haybox ([[F-06]]) and soaking beans overnight save fuel you will need for heat in a Michigan winter.
