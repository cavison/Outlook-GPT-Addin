Turn a freshly killed animal (deer, rabbits, chickens, a hog) into safe food in one day when there is no working fridge or freezer.

## You need
- Clean table, sharp knives, steel, cutting boards, buckets with lids
- Plenty of clean water, dish soap, bleach for sanitizing surfaces
- Probe thermometer (meat thermometer)
- Shade, a cool garage or porch, coolers, ice or snow if you have it
- Fire or stove; pressure canner + jars (if canning); drying racks or smoker
- Salt (non-iodized canning/pickling salt for curing)

## Steps
1. **Plan the day:** butcher on the coolest day you can get and start at first light. Have water, fire and jars ready *before* the kill.
2. **Cool fast:** open the body cavity, prop it open, and get the carcass into shade and moving air. Cut large pieces down so the center cools; packing quarters in clean bags in snow or ice water speeds this up.
3. **Cook today:** eat the liver, heart, kidneys, ground meat and trimmings first — these spoil fastest. Cook to USDA temperatures: ground meat 160°F, whole cuts of red meat 145°F plus a 3-minute rest, poultry 165°F.
4. **Can:** meat is low-acid — pressure-can only, following the USDA Complete Guide times for your jar size and altitude ([[G-19]]).
5. **Dry or cure:** slice lean meat thin (1/4 in.), heat it to 160°F (165°F for poultry) before or at the start of drying, then dry until it bends and cracks but doesn't snap ([[G-20]], [[G-21]]).
6. **Winter vs summer:** in steady cold (below 40°F day and night, but above freezing) a carcass can hang for days ([[F-13]]). In summer, everything not eaten must be canned, dried or cooked and kept hot by nightfall.

## Table
| Condition | What it means |
|---|---|
| Below 40°F | Safe holding (USDA). Michigan Nov–Mar nights often, but not always, qualify |
| 40–140°F | Danger zone: bacteria grow fast |
| Over 2 hours in danger zone | Discard (1 hour if air is above 90°F) |
| 160°F | Ground meat; jerky pre-heat |
| 165°F | Poultry and leftovers reheated |

## Caution
Meat sitting between 40°F and 140°F for more than 2 hours (1 hour on a hot day) is unsafe even if it smells fine. Never water-bath or oven-can meat — only a pressure canner kills botulism spores in low-acid food.

## Field note
In a west Michigan November, daytime highs can swing from the 20s to the 50s in one week. Check the meat temperature with a probe, not the air: a deer hanging in a sunny garage can sit above 40°F while it's freezing at night. Thick muscle like a hindquarter cools much more slowly than you think — breaking it into muscles speeds cooling more than anything else.
