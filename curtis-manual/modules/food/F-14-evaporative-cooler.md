Keep vegetables and fruit cooler and fresher in summer using two clay pots, wet sand and moving air.

## You need
- Two unglazed terra-cotta pots, one fitting inside the other with 1–2 in gap
- Clean sand
- Plug for drain holes (clay, cork, or tape)
- Wet cloth or towel for the top
- Water (non-drinking water is fine)

## Steps
1. Plug the drain hole of the inner pot (and the outer one if you want less dripping).
2. Put an inch of sand in the outer pot, set the inner pot on it.
3. Fill the gap between the pots with sand.
4. Pour water into the sand until it is soaked but not flooded.
5. Put produce in the inner pot and cover with a wet cloth.
6. Place in shade where a breeze moves. Re-wet sand once or twice a day.

## Caution
This cooler does NOT keep food at refrigerator-safe 40°F. Use it only for produce, not meat, dairy, cooked leftovers or medicine.

## Field note
Evaporation cools best in dry air. West Michigan summers are often humid, so the effect may be small on muggy days and better on dry, breezy ones. A basement floor is often cooler than any evaporative trick; use it first.
