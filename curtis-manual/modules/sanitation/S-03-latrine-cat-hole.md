Dispose of human waste in the ground when bucket toilets are full and no service is coming, without fouling water.

## You need
- Shovel or trowel
- Measuring tape or 75 paces
- Cover soil, ash or sawdust
- Toilet paper, hand sanitizer, a tarp for privacy

## Steps
1. Choose a spot at least 200 ft (about 70 adult steps) from wells, ponds, creeks, drains and food gardens, and downhill from any well.
2. Cat-hole (one use): dig 6–8 inches deep and 4–6 inches wide.
3. Latrine trench (many uses): dig about 1 ft wide and as deep as practical, but stop well above groundwater.
4. After each use, cover waste with a layer of soil (and ash or sawdust if you have it).
5. Fill the trench to within 1 ft of the top, then fill with soil and mound it. Mark the spot.
6. Set up handwashing right next to it ([[S-04]]).

## Caution
In much of Ottawa County the soil is sandy and the water table is shallow, so waste can reach groundwater and wells quickly. If you dig and hit wet soil or water, stop, refill, and choose another spot or keep using bagged buckets.

## Field note
In Michigan winters frozen ground makes digging nearly impossible. Plan on bagged bucket toilets ([[S-01]]) in winter and use a latrine only when the ground thaws. Ash from your wood stove is an excellent cover material.
