Keep trash from drawing rats, flies, raccoons and disease when pickup stops for weeks.

## You need
- Metal or heavy plastic trash cans with tight lids; bungee cords
- Heavy trash bags
- Separate bins: food scraps, recyclables, burnables, hazardous
- Snap traps; fly paper or fly traps

## Steps
1. Separate trash: wet food waste, dry paper/cardboard, cans/plastic, and hazardous (batteries, medicines, chemicals).
2. Rinse cans and containers with used dish water; crush them flat to save space.
3. Bag food waste tightly, squeeze out air, and put it in a lidded can strapped shut.
4. Keep cans outside, away from doors, on a hard surface, in shade.
5. Compost plant scraps (no meat, bones, dairy, grease) in a closed bin if you have one.
6. Look daily for droppings, gnawing, and maggots. Set snap traps along walls. Hang fly traps near cans.
7. Follow local instructions for drop-off sites or special pickup.

## Caution
Do not burn household trash: burning plastic, treated wood and painted materials releases toxic smoke, and open burning of household trash is restricted in Michigan (EGLE). Never set out rodent poison where children, pets or wildlife can reach it.

## Field note
Flies breed in wet food waste in about a week in warm weather, so the most important habit is sealing wet garbage within hours. Freezing winters help: in deep cold, food waste in a sealed outdoor can barely smells. In spring thaw, expect odors and flies to jump.
