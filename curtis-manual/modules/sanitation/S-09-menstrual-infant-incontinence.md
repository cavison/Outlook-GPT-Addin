Plan hygiene supplies for periods, diapers and incontinence so no one goes without for 90 days.

## You need
- Stock: pads/tampons, a menstrual cup or washable cloth pads
- Disposable diapers and wipes; or cloth diapers, covers and a lidded diaper pail
- Adult briefs, bed pads, barrier cream
- Gloves, plastic bags, unscented bleach, soap

## Steps
1. Count needs before an outage: estimate use per day or per cycle for each person and multiply for 90 days. Add extra.
2. Keep a reusable backup: menstrual cup or cloth pads, cloth diapers, washable bed pads.
3. Change diapers, pads and briefs often; clean skin front to back and let it air-dry before a new one.
4. Bag used disposables tightly and store with other waste ([[S-02]]).
5. For cloth items: rinse solids into the toilet bucket, soak in a lidded pail, then wash in hot soapy water and dry fully, in sun if possible.
6. Wash hands with soap after every change.

## Caution
Leave a tampon in no longer than the package directions (FDA) to lower the risk of toxic shock syndrome. Watch babies and bed-bound adults for diaper rash, sores and dehydration; fewer wet diapers is a warning sign.

## Field note
Washable cloth items need water, soap and drying space; plan them into your water budget. A diaper sprayer is not possible without pressure, but a squeeze bottle or cup of gray water over the toilet bucket works. Store supplies in sealed tubs; Michigan basements are damp.
