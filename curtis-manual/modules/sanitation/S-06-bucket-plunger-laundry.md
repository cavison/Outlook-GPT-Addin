Wash clothes by hand with two buckets and a clean plunger, using far less water than a machine.

## You need
- Two 5-gallon buckets (one wash, one rinse); a lid with a hole fits the plunger handle
- New, clean toilet plunger used only for laundry (or a mobile washer)
- Laundry detergent (a small amount)
- Clothesline, clothespins; rubber gloves

## Steps
1. Fill the wash bucket about half full with water and a little detergent.
2. Add a small load: a few shirts, socks and underwear. Do not pack it tight.
3. Plunge up and down for 2–5 minutes. Let soak, then plunge again.
4. Wring each item and move it into the rinse bucket of clean water; plunge to rinse.
5. Wring hard (twist with a partner, or roll in a towel).
6. Hang to dry. Indoors in winter, hang near (not on) the heat source.

## Caution
Keep wet clothes and lines well away from stoves, heaters, candles and lanterns; drying clothes are a common fire start. Wash clothes soiled with stool, vomit or blood separately with gloves on, and use hot water and bleach where the fabric allows (CDC).

## Field note
Wash underwear and socks most often; outer layers can go much longer. In below-freezing weather, clothes hung outside freeze-dry slowly (the ice sublimates) but it takes days; bring them in to finish.
