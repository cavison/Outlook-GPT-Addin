Build a foot-operated handwashing station from a jug and sticks that uses very little water and keeps dirty hands off the jug.

## You need
- 1-gallon plastic jug with handle and cap
- Two forked sticks or posts about 5 ft long, and one cross stick
- String or cord
- Nail heated to make a hole
- Bar of soap (in old sock or mesh bag) or soapy-water bottle
- Gravel or stones; safe water

## Steps
1. Push the two forked posts into the ground about 3 ft apart; lay the cross stick across the forks.
2. Make a small hole in the jug about 2 inches below the cap (heat the nail, push through).
3. Fill the jug with safe water and cap it. Tie the jug's handle to the cross stick so it hangs and can tilt.
4. Tie string from the jug neck down to a short foot-pedal stick on the ground.
5. Hang soap on another string from the cross stick.
6. Put gravel under the jug so water soaks in instead of making mud.
7. Use: step on the pedal, jug tips, wet hands, release. Lather 20 seconds, step again to rinse.

## Caution
Use only clean or disinfected water in the jug. Hand sanitizer does not work well on visibly dirty or greasy hands; soap and water is better (CDC). Wash after the toilet, before cooking and eating, and after caring for sick people.

## Field note
Loosening the cap slightly controls the flow: tight cap, a thin stream; loose cap, more water. In freezing Michigan weather, set the tippy-tap up indoors in a tub or over a bucket, or refill with warm water each time, because the jug and hole freeze solid.
