Make a safe, low-odor indoor toilet when toilets can't flush, by keeping pee and poop in separate buckets.

## You need
- Two 5-gallon buckets with tight lids (label PEE and POO)
- Toilet seat that fits a bucket, or a seat cut from a pool noodle
- Heavy-duty trash bags (contractor bags) to line the POO bucket
- Cover material: sawdust, wood shavings, peat moss, shredded paper, or clumping cat litter
- Toilet paper, hand sanitizer, gloves

## Steps
1. Pick a private spot with some airflow (bathroom, closet, garage corner). Set the two buckets side by side.
2. Line the POO bucket with two bags. Pour about an inch of cover material in the bottom.
3. PEE goes in the PEE bucket (use a funnel or cup to pee into it). Keep it lidded.
4. POO goes in the POO bucket. Toilet paper goes in the POO bucket too.
5. After each use, sprinkle a cup or so of cover material over the poop until it is hidden. Lid on.
6. Empty the PEE bucket daily: dilute and pour on the ground away from wells, gardens and water, or follow local guidance.
7. When the POO bag is about half full, tie it, tie the second bag, and store it (see [[S-02]]). Re-line.
8. Wash or sanitize hands every time ([[S-04]]).

## Caution
Never add bleach to the PEE bucket: bleach and urine can release irritating chloramine gas. Keep lids on so kids and pets cannot get in, and do not overfill bags; heavy bags tear.

## Field note
Keeping pee out of the poop bucket is the whole trick: it cuts odor dramatically and makes bags much lighter. Urine from healthy people is usually low-risk; feces are what spread disease. Before an outage, ask a sawmill, lumber yard or pet store for sawdust or pine-pellet litter; a 40 lb bag of pine pellets lasts a long time.
