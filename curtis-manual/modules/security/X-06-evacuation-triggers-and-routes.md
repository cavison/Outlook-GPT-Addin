Decide ahead of time exactly what will make you leave and where you'll go, so you don't have to decide while scared.

## You need
- Paper road map of Ottawa and Kent counties
- Highlighters
- Go-bags ([[X-05]])
- List of possible destinations (relatives, warming centers, shelters)

## Steps
1. Write your triggers: e.g., an official evacuation order, indoor temperature you can't keep safe, no water, a gas leak, fire, or a medical need you can't meet.
2. Pick two destinations in different directions and a backup meeting place nearby.
3. Highlight two routes to each on a paper map; avoid low areas near rivers that flood.
4. Keep the car's tank at least half full; know how far you can go on it.
5. Leave a dated note inside saying where you went and when.
6. Drill once: everyone out with bags, pets and meds in a set time.

## Caution
Always follow an official evacuation order. Waiting until roads are icy, flooded or jammed is how families get stranded. Never drive through water over the road.

## Field note
Set a temperature trigger for cold: if you can't keep at least one room safely warm and people are showing early signs of hypothermia, it's time to go to a warming center or a relative's. Decide the number now with your family rather than at 3 a.m.
