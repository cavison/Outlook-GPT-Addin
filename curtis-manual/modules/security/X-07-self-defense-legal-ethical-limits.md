Understand the general legal and moral limits on using force so you don't turn a bad situation into a tragedy or a prison sentence. This is general information, not legal advice.

## You need
- A Michigan CPL / pistol-safety class or a self-defense law class from a qualified instructor
- Locked storage (safe, lockbox, cable locks) for any firearms
- A household agreement on avoidance and retreat

## Steps
1. Avoid, leave or lock out first; the best fight is the one that never happens.
2. In general US and Michigan law, force must be proportional; deadly force is generally limited to an honest, reasonable belief that it's necessary to prevent imminent death, great bodily harm or sexual assault.
3. Protecting property alone generally does not justify deadly force.
4. Michigan law gives specific rules for your home and for duty to retreat; learn them from a class or attorney, not the internet.
5. Store firearms locked and unloaded where children can access the home; Michigan has a safe-storage law.
6. After any incident: get safe, call 911, give basic facts, and talk to a lawyer before detailed statements.

## Caution
During an outage, people knocking or wandering are usually neighbors, lost or desperate, not attackers. Identify your target and what's beyond it; fear and darkness cause tragic mistakes.

## Field note
Laws on self-defense, castle doctrine and firearm storage change and depend on exact facts. A Michigan CPL class must cover state law, and many instructors teach a separate legal-use-of-force course; take one with every adult who might handle a firearm in the home.
