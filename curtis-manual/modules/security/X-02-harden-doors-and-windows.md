Make exterior doors and ground-floor windows much harder to force with cheap, quick upgrades.

## You need
- Screwdriver or drill
- 3-inch (or longer) wood screws
- Heavy-duty strike plate
- Door security bar or wedge
- Wooden dowel or bar for sliding doors
- Window locks or pins

## Steps
1. Replace the short screws in the deadbolt strike plate with 3-inch screws that reach the wall stud.
2. Replace at least one screw in each door hinge with a 3-inch screw.
3. Install a heavy-duty strike plate if yours is thin.
4. Use a door bar or wedge at night on doors that open inward.
5. Lay a dowel in sliding-door tracks so the door can't slide open.
6. Add pin locks to double-hung windows; trim shrubs that hide windows.
7. Make sure every person can still unlock and get out fast in a fire.

## Caution
Never nail doors or windows shut or block every exit. In a fire you must get out in seconds, and Michigan winters mean more candle, heater and stove fires during outages.

## Field note
Most kicked-in doors fail at the frame, not the lock. The short screws that come with strike plates often only reach the thin door jamb; long screws into the stud behind it are among the cheapest security upgrades you can make.
