How to read a rank
1–150 is where the sources agree and where your draft is actually won. 151–300 is role-based inference. 301–500 is deep-league and dynasty stash territory — treat teams there as a starting point, not gospel.
The honest caveat
This is a consensus snapshot, not a projection engine, and preseason moves fast. Anything carrying a status flag below should be re-checked the morning of your draft.

Where the draft is won

position by position

Running Back

Two elite backs, then a cliff you can see from the first round.

  • Rounds 1–2Gibbs and Bijan are the 1A/1B. Roughly the top 12 backs are gone by the end of round two, and more than a third of NFL backfields are running two- or three-man committees — scarcity is real this year.
  • ValueChase Brown, Bhayshul Tuten and Jadarian Price are the mid-round backs whose roles outrun their cost.
  • HandcuffBlake Corum first, then Tank Bigsby and Tyrone Tracy. These are the three whose starters going down changes a league.

Wide Receiver

Deep enough that the position is sliding — so let it.

  • Tier 1Chase, Nacua and Smith-Njigba are the only three projected for 255+ half-PPR points. Everything after them is one large, flat tier.
  • MiddleRounds 5–8 are where receivers are being underdrafted. Egbuka, McConkey, Pickens and Waddle all sit below where the projections put them.
  • LateParker Washington, Adonai Mitchell, Rashid Shaheed and De'Zhaun Stribling are the late-round names with a real path to volume.

Tight End

Pay for one of the top four or wait until round twelve.

  • The fourBowers and McBride are a coin flip at the top; the sophomores Loveland and Warren close the tier. After Kyle Pitts and Harold Fannin the position flattens hard.
  • Skip zoneRounds 7–10 at TE buy you a fringe starter. Goedert, Ferguson and Kincaid are the same player at different prices.
  • StreamersGunnar Helm, Mason Taylor and Terrance Ferguson are the last-round shots worth taking.

Quarterback

Josh Allen, a gap, and then eighteen guys you can wait for.

  • Tier 1Allen alone — projected nearly 24 fantasy points clear of every other passer and the only QB inside the overall top 30.
  • Tier 2Jayden Daniels, Lamar Jackson, Drake Maye, Jalen Hurts. Taking one of these in round six is the efficient move.
  • WaitBaker Mayfield in round 12 or 13, and Cam Ward as the QB2-upside dart nobody is paying for.
  • LegsRushing is the cheapest floor at the position — look for the Rush badge in the table. Jaxson Dart, Justin Herbert and Malik Willis all carry real ground volume well below where it is normally priced.

Sleepers

grouped by why the market is missing them

A sleeper is not just a cheap player — it is a player whose price is wrong for a reason you can name. These thirty are sorted by that reason, because the reason is what tells you how much risk you are actually buying. Every one of them is on the board above; switch the table to Sleepers to see them in draft order.

Post-hype

The market already tried them once and got bored. Boredom is not evidence.

Jonathon Brooks RB32 · CAR · Ovr 82 ADP ~125 · RB29

A second ACL tear cost him all of 2025, so the market moved on. Carolina did the opposite: they let Rico Dowdle walk and ignored running back entirely in the draft. Dave Canales says he “looks like the guy that I remember.” He climbed nine spots on the 8/25 update and still costs a bench pick for a three-down RB1 outcome.

Michael Wilson WR39 · ARI · Ovr 83 Value past pick 100

Jumped nearly eight spots after the first preseason game and is still cheap. The knock is fair — 2025 was top-heavy in the games Marvin Harrison missed. The counter is that Arizona's target tree is still thin behind Harrison.

Xavier Worthy WR46 · KC · Ovr 96 WR46

Kansas City moved on from Marquise Brown and Smith-Schuster and replaced them with a fifth-round rookie. That is a roster telling you who it trusts. Third year, fastest man out of the 2024 class, and the last starter standing.

Rashod Bateman WR66 · BAL · Ovr 141 Late-round / waiver

Five years of waiting has trained everyone to stop looking. He is still the perimeter starter on an offense that scores, and the cost is now nothing.

Role changed, price didn't

An offseason move or a depth-chart shift that ADP hasn't caught up to.

Harold Fannin Jr. TE5 · CLE · Ovr 55 TE6-ish cost

72-731-6 and a 22% target share as a 21-year-old rookie, good for TE6. Then David Njoku left for the Chargers and handed him the entire room. The Cleveland quarterback situation is the only reason he is not priced higher.

David Montgomery RB24 · HOU · Ovr 56 PPR RB24

Traded to Houston and has already established himself on top of that backfield — RB24 in ADP against RB43 for Woody Marks. The name is familiar enough that nobody is excited, which is the whole point.

Stefon Diggs WR29 · WAS · Ovr 62 ~Round 10

Led the entire NFL in catch rate over expected at 15.4%, caught 83.3% of his targets, and ran 2.4 yards per route — fifth among everyone with 300+ routes. He has risen more than a round twice since signing in Washington and is still a round-10 pick.

Parker Washington WR33 · JAX · Ovr 73 ADP 88.4 Yahoo / 102.7 ESPN

From Week 12 on he led all Jacksonville receivers with a 25% target share and 14.2 PPR points per game. Over the final four games, playoffs included, that became a 31% target share and 18.5 per game on a 26-454-3 line. He is being drafted as though that stretch did not happen.

Kenny Gainwell RB31 · TB · Ovr 79 RB31

The exact reason Bucky Irving's price fell is the reason Gainwell has standalone value. Tampa is running a real committee and only one half of it is being drafted.

Josh Downs WR49 · IND · Ovr 107 WR43

Moving out of the slot into Indianapolis's No. 2 job. That makes him Daniel Jones' primary underneath target working opposite Tyler Warren, which is a volume role at a WR4 price.

The second-year leap

Rookie-year efficiency without the volume. The volume is the part that arrives.

Emeka Egbuka WR17 · TB · Ovr 35 WR18

A rookie star through five weeks before leveling off, which drafters have read as the ceiling rather than the calibration period. Priced as a WR2 for a player with WR1 usage in front of him.

Mason Taylor TE15 · NYJ · Ovr 132 TE14-ish

Led the Jets in both catches and targets as a rookie despite playing 13 games. Aaron Glenn named him a breakout candidate in mid-August, which is usually a coach telling you the target share is going up.

Tyler Shough QB15 · NO · Ovr 157 QB17

Strong down the stretch as a rookie, and New Orleans spent the offseason propping the offense up around him — Travis Etienne in, Jordyn Tyson drafted eighth overall. Projection models keep flagging him as a steal.

Gunnar Helm TE25 · TEN · Ovr 193 TE28 — essentially free

As a rookie he posted a 22.4% target-per-route-run rate and 1.45 yards per route, both better than Chig Okonkwo, on an 80% catch rate. Cam Ward gets more comfortable in year two and Helm becomes the security blanket. You can take him with your last pick.

Terrance Ferguson TE26 · LAR · Ovr 199 TE20s

The buzziest name in Rams camp all summer, getting extended first-team reps from Sean McVay. Only 25 targets as a rookie but 21 yards per catch, and the Rams are visibly expanding his route tree.

Somebody else's injury

A door opened in the last three weeks and the price is still catching up.

Tyrone Tracy Jr. RB43 · NYG · Ovr 112 RB50

Averaged 16.9 touches and 12.9 PPR points across the 10 games Cam Skattebo was not the clear lead back. Skattebo is coming back from a major injury. This is the cheapest handcuff with a starter's outcome attached.

Tank Bigsby RB47 · PHI · Ovr 126 RB47

Not a sleeper if Saquon plays 17 games. The full Barkley workload is one of maybe three handcuff outcomes in football that wins a league outright.

Jonah Coleman RB49 · DEN · Ovr 144 Late-round

Sits behind J.K. Dobbins and R.J. Harvey in Denver, and Dobbins' health is a perpetual red flag. Touchdown-dependent as an RB3, but he could lead that team in rushing scores.

De'Zhaun Stribling WR70 · SF · Ovr 147 Up ~27 spots to ~pick 100

The single biggest riser on the board over the last week, and it still may not be enough. Ricky Pearsall's season-ending knee surgery cleared the room and Stribling — a second-round rookie, sixth receiver drafted — has been San Francisco's best camp receiver.

Buried by a bigger name

Drafters stop reading a depth chart after the first line.

Jayden Reed WR45 · GB · Ovr 94 WR45

Green Bay's slot receiver in a room where the market is busy arguing about Golden and Watson. Availability is the real question, not talent.

Jalen Nailor WR50 · LV · Ovr 111 WR67 — going very late

Las Vegas's big offseason signing and their camp WR1. Brock Bowers will lead that team in receiving, but Nailor is the receiver to own there and he costs a WR5 pick.

Rashid Shaheed WR53 · SEA · Ovr 119 WR56-ish

Was breaking out in New Orleans before the midseason trade to Seattle. Now he is Sam Darnold's No. 2 and the deep threat working opposite Smith-Njigba, and he is being drafted like a change-of-pace piece.

Adonai Mitchell WR56 · NYJ · Ovr 125 WR55-ish

Set to start opposite Garrett Wilson for a team that will be playing from behind most weeks. Third-year receivers with a defined starting job and negative game script are how late-round WR rooms get won.

Isaac TeSlaa WR73 · DET · Ovr 154 Waiver-adjacent

Locked in as Detroit's No. 3 and a genuine red-zone body on one of the league's best offenses. One step forward makes him relevant.

Free in the last two rounds

Zero cost, real path to targets. Take the shot.

Nick Singleton RB48 · TEN · Ovr 138 Last round

One of the best late stashes on the board — the talent and the opening to become a difference-maker in exactly the weeks the fantasy playoffs happen.

Jalen Coker WR72 · CAR · Ovr 152 Last round / undrafted

An undrafted free agent who built a genuine connection with Bryce Young. Large frame, hard worker, and a third-year breakout is live if Carolina's passing game takes any step at all.

Emmett Johnson RB50 · KC · Ovr 153 Undrafted

The rookie behind Kenneth Walker III in an offense that just paid $43M for its backfield. Handcuff logic, zero cost.

Cyrus Allen WR82 · KC · Ovr 177 Last round / undrafted

A fifth-round rookie turning heads in Kansas City camp with a path to real Patrick Mahomes targets. There is no cost to being wrong here.

Baker Mayfield QB20 · TB · Ovr 197 Rounds 12-13

Routinely available in the 12th or 13th round. If you punt quarterback entirely, this is where you land and it is a fine place to land.

Greg Dulcich TE28 · MIA · Ovr 209 Undrafted

In position to step forward as a second primary target in Miami. A late-round tight end with a defined role beats a late-round tight end without one.

Cheap, but not sleepers

  • Makai LemonWR · PHI — A hamstring has kept him out of practice for two weeks and rankers have him down to WR65 — while his ADP is still WR46. Cheap and hurt is not the same as cheap and good.
  • DK MetcalfWR · PIT — Right player, wrong offense. He is being drafted on name recognition, and the Pittsburgh passing game is not going to fund a WR3 season.
  • Jayden HigginsWR · HOU — One outlet removed him entirely for a torn ACL while others had him as Stroud's clear No. 2. Do not spend a real pick until you have confirmed which is true.
  • Jordyn TysonWR · NO — The eighth overall pick going in the ninth round looks like an obvious value — but a hamstring dropped him nearly 34 spots and may linger toward Week 7.
